Grammar: これは買ったかもしれへん uses the topic これは (this [topic]) + past 買った (bought) + Kansai negative conjectural かもしれへん (might not = かもしれない): "This might be a mistake." The ASR has rendered what is likely やばい or 間違えた as 買った, a phonetic error. The Kansai へん replaces standard ない in the conjectural auxiliary かもしれない. あ、こっち。じゃあ。 are telegraphic direction markers: "Ah, this way. Well then." The demonstrative こっ (this way) is the colloquial form of こちら, and じゃあ marks a decision point. よいしょ。よし。散歩。 are telegraphic effort and commencement markers: "Heave-ho. Okay. Walk." The noun 散歩 without する is a common vlog heading style, treating the activity as a named phase. リードが絡まった uses the subject が + past 絡まった (got tangled): "The lead got tangled." The spontaneous-form equivalent 絡まる describes the lead becoming tangled without specifying an agent, a common Japanese pattern for accidental states. それうちの指 uses the demonstrative それ (that) + Kansai possessive うちの (my = 私の) + noun 指 (finger): "That's my finger." The vlogger is likely correcting the goat or the cat about what is in its mouth. サビちゃん。サビちゃん。お前も行くか。 uses the name + repetition for attention + pronoun お前 (you; casual, slightly rough but affectionate with pets) + も (also) + verb 行く (go) + question か: "Sabi-chan. Sabi-chan. Are you coming too?" The お前 is commonly used with animals and close friends in casual Japanese, carrying a rough affection rather than true rudeness.

06 Key Grammar Patterns Summary

Kansai Dialect Core Particles and Copulas
へん (かもしれへん), やん, やで, やな, わ, で, やろう, もん. The negative へん, the tag question やん, the explanatory assertive やで, the exclamatory やな, the emphatic わ, the assertive で, the conjectural やろう, and the complaining もん form the emotional and grammatical backbone of Kansai-dialect rural vlog narration.
〜てくる / 〜ていく (Directional Auxiliaries)
斜めから入ろうとしてくるもん. "[He's] trying to come in from an angle, mon." The grammar of approach and attempted movement, marking the goat's challenging behavior as spatially directed toward the vlogger.
〜てしまう / 〜ちゃった (Completion and Regret)
ちゃった. "[He] did [it]." The casual past completion auxiliary marking the inevitable or regrettable completion of an action, here the goat peeing on the vlogger.
Hortative Variations and Code-Switching
行こっか, 行こう, 帰ろう, 戻りましょう, 戻ろう, 行こ. The grammar of suggesting movement spans from soft Kansai questions (行こっか) to polite hortatives (戻りましょう) to casual Kansai drops (行こ), demonstrating the full register range of Japanese hortative forms.
Rhetorical Questions and Exclamations
無視かよ, 虫かよ, 誰だ?, 絶対買ったやん. The grammar of exasperated rhetorical questioning: かよ for indignant surprise, やん for emphatic tag questions, だ for direct interrogation.
Telegraphic Vlog Narration and Onomatopoeia
よいしょ, おいしょいしょ, えいえいえい, ぎゅ, スリスリ, バリバリ, ういういういういういういうい. The grammar of non-lexical sound in rural vlogs: effort grunts, affectionate onomatopoeia, crunching sounds, and repeated interjections create the rhythmic texture of physical animal handling.
Comparative and Explanatory Patterns
ジョンより人間の方がしんどい, こっちの方が買ったわ, すすきは手切れるんやぞ. The grammar of comparison (より...の方が) and explanation (んやぞ) in Kansai dialect, marking relative suffering and rural warnings.
Animal-Care Imperatives and Affectionate Speech
待て, おいで, くよ, 良い子, ぎゅ, 挟む挟む挟む. The grammar of commanding and comforting animals: imperatives, affectionate noun phrases, onomatopoeic physical contact, and repeated warnings.
