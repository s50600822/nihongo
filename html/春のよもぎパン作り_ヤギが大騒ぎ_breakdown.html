Grammar: 今回はよもぎを使う uses the temporal noun 今回 (this time) + topic は + object を + plain verb 使う (will use): "This time [I] will use yomogi." The plain-form declaration sets the theme for the entire video. よもぎの柔らかい葉っぱだけ uses the genitive の + i-adjective 柔らかい (soft; tender) modifying 葉っぱ (leaves) + restrictive だけ (only): "Only the soft leaves of yomogi." The だけ restricts the harvest to tender young leaves, a key foraging principle for mugwort, which becomes bitter and fibrous as it matures. あら、やだ uses the feminine interjection あら (oh my) + やだ (oh dear; I don't want that), a classic expression of mild distress. Its use here responds to an accident, likely the cat getting caught in the harvesting or the plant coming out with roots and soil attached. 猫ごと抜けちゃった uses the suffix ごと (together with; including; and all) + colloquial past 抜けちゃった (from 抜けてしまった, came out [regrettably]): "[It] came out with the cat and all." The てしま auxiliary adds regret at the plant being uprooted rather than cleanly cut, and the ごと emphasizes that the cat was somehow involved in the mishap. よいしょ is the canonical Japanese effort interjection, like "heave-ho" or "up we go," used when lifting, pulling, or exerting physical force. 湯がいたら、湯がいたら少しになるやろな uses the conditional 湯がいたら (if [I] blanch [them]) repeated for emphasis + result 少しになる (become a little) + Kansai conjectural やろ (from だろう, [I] suppose) + exclamatory な: "If [I] blanch [them], if [I] blanch [them], [they'll] become a little, [I] suppose, na." The repetition of the conditional clause creates a contemplative, anticipatory tone, as the vlogger imagines the volume reduction from blanching. 湯がく (yugaku, to blanch; to scald) is a standard Japanese cooking term for briefly boiling vegetables to remove bitterness and soften them. だいぶいっぱい取った uses the adverb だいぶ (considerably) + いっぱい (a lot) + past 取った (harvested): "[I] harvested quite a lot." The double quantifier (だいぶ + いっぱい) emphasizes the abundance of the forage. こんなもんかな uses the demonstrative こんな (this kind of) + colloquial noun もん (thing; stuff) + speculation かな: "[Is it] about this much, [I] wonder?" The もん downplays the assessment to casual estimation. こんくらいあったらゆんちゃん足りそう uses the demonstrative extent こんくらい (about this much) + conditional あったら (if there is) + recipient ゆんちゃん (Yun-chan, the goat) + conjectural 足りそう (seems enough): "If [there is] about this much, [it] seems [it will] be enough for Yun-chan." The conjectural そう attached to the potential 足りる (to be enough) creates a tentative sufficiency assessment. よし is the readiness interjection: "Alright." よもぎを投入 uses the loanword-suru verb 投入 (tounyuu, input; addition) as a direct object-verb construction: "Add the yomogi." 投入 is commonly used in Japanese cooking shows for dramatic ingredient additions. グツグツシャッとゆく uses the onomatopoeia グツグツ (bubble-bubble; simmering) + シャッ (shh; swish) + classical verb ゆく (goes; proceeds): "[It] goes bubble-bubble-shh." The compound onomatopoeia creates a vivid soundscape of the blanching process, and the classical ゆく (rather than standard 行く) adds a slightly poetic, narrative quality. これはえぐい uses the demonstrative これ (this) + copula (elided) + slang adjective えぐい (intense; harsh; extreme): "This is intense." えぐい is a youth slang term that has broadened to mean anything extreme, vivid, or overwhelming, and its use here describes the intense green color or smell of the blanching mugwort. 絶対入れすぎよ uses the adverb 絶対 (absolutely) + excessive past 入れすぎ (put in too much) + assertive よ: "[I] definitely put in too much, yo." The excessive suffix すぎる attached to 入れる marks the quantity as beyond optimal, and the よ asserts this evaluation to the audience.

Grammar: え、どうしよう uses the interjection え (huh) + set phrase どうしよう (what should [I] do?): "Huh, what should [I] do?" This is the canonical Japanese expression of mild panic when something seems to be going wrong, and its use here responds to the unexpected color intensity of the yomogi dough. 80+80+35はう怖い uses the arithmetic recitation 80+80+35 (195 grams total, likely flour measurements) + topic は + interjection う (u) + adjective 怖い (scary): "80 plus 80 plus 35 is u, scary." The vlogger is calculating ingredient ratios and finding the numbers intimidating, a common baking anxiety. やばい色してるよ uses the slang adjective やばい (insane; extreme) modifying the noun 色 (color) + progressive してる (is making/doing) + assertive よ: "[It's] making an insane color, yo." The pattern 色をしている (to have a color; to be colored) is standard Japanese for describing hues, and the progressive してる frames the color as an ongoing, observable phenomenon. 大丈夫かいな uses the adjective 大丈夫 (alright; okay) + Kansai question particle かいな (from かな, [I] wonder): "[Is it] okay, [I] wonder?" The Kansai かいな is a soft, self-directed question marker that expresses tentative worry. 不安になってくる uses the noun 不安 (anxiety; unease) + になる (to become) + directional auxiliary てくる (coming to; starting to): "[I'm] becoming anxious [as time goes on]." The てくる with change-of-state verbs marks a gradual onset of the emotional state, as if anxiety is approaching the vlogger over time. 強力粉や uses the noun 強力粉 (kyouryokuko, bread flour; strong flour) + Kansai copula や: "[It's] bread flour, ya." 強力粉 is the Japanese term for high-gluten bread flour, as opposed to 薄力粉 (hakurikiko, cake flour) or 中力粉 (chuurikiko, all-purpose flour). The Kansai や confirms the flour type as a self-directed reassurance. あ、らみそう uses the interjection あ (ah) + conjectural 膨らみそう (seems [it will] rise): "Ah, [it] seems [it will] rise." The conjectural そう attached to the verb stem creates a visual prediction based on the dough's appearance. え、でもいい感じじゃん uses the interjection え (huh) + contrastive でも (but) + noun phrase いい感じ (good feeling; good vibe) + sentence-ending じゃん: "Huh, but [it's] a good feeling, jan [right?]." The じゃん invites the audience to share in the positive reassessment after the initial color shock. もうちょっと濃くても良かったかもしれへん uses the adverb もうちょっと (a bit more) + conditional 濃くても (even if [it were] more concentrated) + past 良かった (would have been good) + Kansai conjectural かもしれへん (might; may): "[It] might have been good [if it were] a bit more concentrated too." The counterfactual conditional ても...よかった expresses a regret about a past possibility, and the Kansai かもしれへん softens the regret into speculation. ちょっと薄くなった uses the diminutive ちょっと (a bit) + adverbial 薄く (thinly; palely) + past なった (became): "[It] became a bit pale/thin." 薄い (usui) when describing colors means pale or weak, and its use here laments the loss of the vivid green. 最初見た時めっちゃ緑でやばいって思ったけど uses the temporal clause 最初見た時 (when [I] first saw [it]) + adverb めっちゃ (super) + noun 緑 (green) + instrumental で + quotative やばいって ("insane" [that]) + past 思った (thought) + concessive けど: "When [I] first saw [it], [I] thought [it was] super green and insane, but..." The quotative って marks the content of the thought, and the concessive けど sets up the contrast with the current pale reality. 結構薄い uses the adverb 結構 (quite; rather) + adjective 薄い (pale; thin): "[it's] quite pale." いけるかな? uses the potential いける (can work; will do) + speculation かな: "Can [it] work, [I] wonder?" The potential いける is a ubiquitous Japanese colloquialism for whether something is viable, acceptable, or will succeed, and its use here frames the dough as a gamble that might still pay off.

The repetition marks the vlogger's discovery of the goat hiding nearby, a common hide-and-seek narrative pattern. うん is the simple affirmative interjection: "Mm." おまけで、おまけで反復飛びしとくわ uses the noun おまけ (bonus; extra; freebie) + instrumental particle で (as) repeated + noun 反復横飛び (hanpuku ou tobi, side-to-side jumps; lateral hops) + colloquial contraction しとく (from しておく, will do in advance) + assertive/feminine particle わ: "As a bonus, as a bonus [I'll] do side-to-side jumps, wa." The repetition of おまけで emphasizes the playful, extra nature of the activity, and the ておく auxiliary marks the exercise as something done in preparation or as a bonus addition to the video. 反復横飛び is a standard Japanese physical education exercise involving lateral hopping, and its inclusion here adds a fitness/comedic element to the rural vlog. え、ほら uses the interjection え (huh) + attention-directing ほら (look): "Huh, look." バイバイ is the English loanword "bye-bye," a standard friendly farewell. 死んだ uses the plain past of 死ぬ (to die): "[I'm] dead." In Japanese youth slang and casual speech, 死んだ is commonly used hyperbolically to mean "I'm exhausted; I'm done for," and its use here after baking and playing with the goat expresses extreme fatigue. 疲れた uses the plain past of 疲れる (to get tired): "[I'm] tired." The sequence 死んだ。疲れた。 creates a crescendo of exhaustion, a common vlog sign-off pattern. またね uses the adverb また (again; next time) + softening particle ね: "See you again." This is a standard casual Japanese farewell, softer and more intimate than さようなら.

〜てしまう / 〜ちゃった (Regret / Completion Auxiliary)
抜けちゃった, 詰めすぎた. "[It] came out [regrettably];" "[I] stuffed in too much [regrettably]." The grammar of accidental outcomes in baking and foraging.
すぎる (Excessive Suffix)
入れすぎよ, 詰めすぎた. "[I] put in too much;" "[I] stuffed too much." The grammar of overdoing ingredients in live baking narration.
〜かもしれへん / 〜かもせえへん (Kansai Conjectural)
崩れるかもせえへん, 綺麗かもしれん. "[They] might collapse;" "[it] might be beautiful." The Kansai form of tentative speculation in rural cooking vlogs.
〜なくちゃいけない (Contracted Obligation)
早くしなくちゃいけない. "[I] have to hurry." The grammar of urgent necessity under goat pressure, contracted from 〜なければならない.
〜そう (Conjectural / Similative)
膨らみそう, 美味しそう, ええそう. "Seems [it will] rise;" "looks delicious;" "seems good." The grammar of visual inference in baking assessment.
