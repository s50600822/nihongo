# Madara's Speech to Obito — Full Breakdown

**Source:** *Naruto* manga chapter 602 / *Shippuden* episode 344  
**Theme:** "Wake up to reality" (現実を見ろ)

---

## Full Original Text

> 現実を見ろ。  
> この世は思い通りにいかぬことばかりだ。  
> 長く生きれば生きるほど…  
> 現実は苦しみと痛みと虚しさだけが漂っていることに気付く…。  
>  
> いいか…。  
> この世の全てにおいて、  
> 光があたるところには必ず影がある。  
> 勝者という概念がある以上、  
> 敗者は同じくして存在する。  
> 平和を保ちたいという利己的な意志が戦争を起こし、  
> 愛を守るために憎しみが生まれる。  
> これらは因果関係にあり切り離すことができん。  
> 本来はな…。  
>  
> ……この世の因果を断ち切る。  
> 勝者だけの世界。  
> 平和だけの世界。  
> 愛だけの世界。  
> それらだけの世界を造る。  
>  
> オレは……うちはの亡霊。  
> うちはマダラだ。  
>  
> この現実こそ……地獄だ。

**English Translation:**  
"Wake up to reality. Nothing ever goes as planned in this world. The longer you live, the more you realize that this reality is only filled with pain, suffering, and futility. Listen... In this world, wherever there is light, there will always be shadows. As long as there is a concept of victors, the vanquished will also exist. The selfish intent of wanting to preserve peace initiates wars, and hatred is born in order to protect love. These are bound by causal relationships and cannot be separated. That is how it originally is... I will sever the causal bonds of this world. A world of only victors. A world of only peace. A world of only love. I will create such a world. I am... the ghost of the Uchiha. Madara Uchiha. This reality... is truly hell."

---

## Section 1: Opening Command

### Line 1

**Japanese:** 現実を見ろ。  
**Romaji:** Genjitsu o miro.  
**English:** "Wake up to reality." / "Look at reality."

| Japanese | Romaji | Part of Speech | Gloss |
|----------|--------|----------------|-------|
| 現実 | genjitsu | Noun | reality; actuality |
| を | o | Object particle | marks *genjitsu* as the direct object |
| 見ろ | miro | Verb (imperative) | "look!" / "see!" — imperative of 見る *miru* |

**Grammar Note:** A blunt imperative. 見ろ is the plain imperative of 見る (Group 2 verb). In modern polite speech this would be 見てください; Madara uses the raw command form to assert dominance and urgency. The object particle を marks what is to be looked at.

---

### Line 2

**Japanese:** この世は思い通りにいかぬことばかりだ。  
**Romaji:** Kono yo wa omoidoori ni ikanu koto bakari da.  
**English:** "Nothing ever goes as planned in this world." (lit. "In this world, it is nothing but things that do not go according to one's wishes.")

| Japanese | Romaji | Part of Speech | Gloss |
|----------|--------|----------------|-------|
| この | kono | Determiner | this |
| 世 | yo | Noun | world; society |
| は | wa | Topic particle | marks "this world" as the topic |
| 思い通り | omoidoori | Noun / Adverb | as one wishes; according to desire |
| に | ni | Adverbial particle | "in the manner of"; turns the noun into an adverb |
| いかぬ | ikanu | Verb (negative) | do not go — classical/literary negative of 行く *iku* |
| こと | koto | Nominalizer / Noun | things; matters (nominalizes the preceding clause) |
| ばかり | bakari | Adverbial particle | only; nothing but |
| だ | da | Copula | is (plain, assertive) |

**Grammar Note:** いかぬ is the classical negative form of 行く (modern: 行かない). 〜ばかりだ means "nothing but ~". The structure [clause] + ことばかりだ turns the clause into a noun phrase and asserts it exhaustively: "It is exclusively things that do not go as wished."

---

### Line 3

**Japanese:** 長く生きれば生きるほど…  
**Romaji:** Nagaku ikireba ikiru hodo…  
**English:** "The longer you live…" (lit. "The more one lives…")

| Japanese | Romaji | Part of Speech | Gloss |
|----------|--------|----------------|-------|
| 長く | nagaku | Adverb | long — adverbial form of 長い *nagai* |
| 生きれば | ikireba | Verb (conditional) | if one lives — conditional form of きる *ikiru* |
| 生きる | ikiru | Verb (dictionary) | to live |
| ほど | hodo | Adverbial particle | the more…; to the extent that… |

**Grammar Note:** 〜ば…ほど is a correlative construction meaning "the more X, the more Y". Here the second clause is omitted and implied: "The longer one lives, the more [one realizes]…". The trailing ellipsis (…) creates dramatic pause.

---

### Line 4

**Japanese:** 現実は苦しみと痛みと虚しさだけが漂っていることに気付く…。  
**Romaji:** Genjitsu wa kurushimi to itami to munashisa dake ga tadayotte iru koto ni kizuku…  
**English:** "…the more you realize that this reality is only filled with suffering, pain, and futility."

| Japanese | Romaji | Part of Speech | Gloss |
|----------|--------|----------------|-------|
| 現実 | genjitsu | Noun | reality |
| は | wa | Topic particle | as for reality |
| 苦しみ | kurushimi | Noun | suffering; distress |
| と | to | Conjunctive particle | and (lists nouns) |
| 痛み | itami | Noun | pain; ache |
| と | to | Conjunctive particle | and |
| 虚しさ | munashisa | Noun | emptiness; futility; vanity — nominalized from 虚しい *munashii* |
| だけ | dake | Limiting particle | only |
| が | ga | Subject particle | marks the subject of the sub-clause |
| 漂っている | tadayotte iru | Verb (progressive) | are drifting; are hovering — te-iru form of 漂う *tadayou* |
| こと | koto | Nominalizer | the fact that ~ |
| に | ni | Directional particle | to; toward (marks the object of realization) |
| 気付く | kizuku | Verb | to notice; to realize |

**Grammar Note:** The core structure is [clause] + ことに気付く — "to realize that [clause]". Inside the clause, だけが marks "only suffering, pain, and emptiness" as the subject. 漂っている (drifting/hovering) is used metaphorically: these negative things drift through reality like a miasma. Some anime subtitles render 虚しさ as "futility"; manga scanlations sometimes use "sadness" (悲しさ), but 虚しさ is the canonical manga reading.

## Section 2: The Duality of the World

### Line 5

**Japanese:** いいか…。  
**Romaji:** Ii ka…  
**English:** "Listen…" / "You hear me?"

| Japanese | Romaji | Part of Speech | Gloss |
|----------|--------|----------------|-------|
| いい | ii | Adjective | good; okay — used here as an interjection |
| か | ka | Final particle | rough, masculine tag question — "huh?" / "you got that?" |

**Grammar Note:** いいか is a rough, commanding interjection used to secure the listener's attention before an important statement. The か here is not a genuine question but a rhetorical device demanding acknowledgment.

---

### Line 6

**Japanese:** この世の全てにおいて、  
**Romaji:** Kono yo no subete ni oite,  
**English:** "In everything in this world," / "In all things of this world,"

| Japanese | Romaji | Part of Speech | Gloss |
|----------|--------|----------------|-------|
| この | kono | Determiner | this |
| 世 | yo | Noun | world |
| の | no | Genitive particle | of; 's |
| 全て | subete | Noun | all; everything |
| において | ni oite | Compound particle | in; regarding; with respect to — formal/literary locative |

**Grammar Note:** において is a formal, written-style locative particle. It elevates the register from conversational について or の中で to something more philosophical and absolute, fitting Madara's tone.

---

### Line 7

**Japanese:** 光があたるところには必ず影がある。  
**Romaji:** Hikari ga ataru tokoro ni wa kanarazu kage ga aru.  
**English:** "Wherever there is light, there will always be shadows."

| Japanese | Romaji | Part of Speech | Gloss |
|----------|--------|----------------|-------|
| 光 | hikari | Noun | light |
| が | ga | Subject particle | marks "light" as the subject |
| あたる | ataru | Verb | to strike; to shine on; to hit against |
| ところ | tokoro | Noun | place; spot |
| に | ni | Locative particle | in; at |
| は | wa | Topic particle | marks the locative phrase as the topic |
| 必ず | kanarazu | Adverb | without fail; certainly; always |
| 影 | kage | Noun | shadow |
| が | ga | Subject particle | marks "shadow" as the subject |
| ある | aru | Verb (existential) | exists; there is — for inanimate subjects |

**Grammar Note:** [relative clause] + ところに(は) creates a conditional locative: "In places where [clause], …". 必ず (always) reinforces the inevitability. This line establishes the central metaphor: light and shadow are inseparable, a direct nod to the Uchiha clan's fan symbol.

---

### Line 8

**Japanese:** 勝者という概念がある以上、  
**Romaji:** Shousha to iu gainen ga aru ijou,  
**English:** "As long as there is a concept of victors,"

| Japanese | Romaji | Part of Speech | Gloss |
|----------|--------|----------------|-------|
| 勝者 | shousha | Noun | victor; winner |
| という | to iu | Quotative particle | called; that is to say; marks a concept or naming |
| 概念 | gainen | Noun | concept; idea; notion |
| が | ga | Subject particle | marks the subject |
| ある | aru | Verb | exists |
| 以上 | ijou | Conjunctive particle | as long as; since; given that |

**Grammar Note:** 〜という概念 ("the concept called ~") elevates 勝者 from a simple noun to an abstract category. 以上 here is a conditional conjunction: "Given that X exists, Y must follow." It expresses logical necessity, not temporal sequence.

---

### Line 9

**Japanese:** 敗者は同じくして存在する。  
**Romaji:** Haisha wa onajiku shite sonzai suru.  
**English:** "the vanquished will likewise exist."

| Japanese | Romaji | Part of Speech | Gloss |
|----------|--------|----------------|-------|
| 敗者 | haisha | Noun | loser; the vanquished |
| は | wa | Topic particle | as for the vanquished |
| 同じくして | onajiku shite | Adverbial phrase | likewise; in the same manner — 同じく + して |
| 存在する | sonzai suru | Suru-verb | to exist |

**Grammar Note:** 同じくして is a formal, somewhat archaic adverbial meaning "in the same way". It mirrors the logical structure: victors imply losers by necessity. 存在する is the Sino-Japanese (漢語) counterpart to ある, adding gravitas.

---

### Line 10

**Japanese:** 平和を保ちたいという利己的な意志が戦争を起こし、  
**Romaji:** Heiwa o tamochitai to iu rikoteki na ishi ga sensou o okoshi,  
**English:** "The selfish intent of wanting to preserve peace initiates wars,"

| Japanese | Romaji | Part of Speech | Gloss |
|----------|--------|----------------|-------|
| 平和 | heiwa | Noun | peace |
| を | o | Object particle | marks the object of 保ちたい |
| 保ちたい | tamochitai | Verb (desiderative) | want to preserve/maintain — 〜たい form of 保つ *tamotsu* |
| という | to iu | Quotative particle | that; called |
| 利己的な | rikoteki na | Na-adjective | selfish; self-serving |
| 意志 | ishi | Noun | will; intent |
| が | ga | Subject particle | marks the subject |
| 戦争 | sensou | Noun | war |
| を | o | Object particle | marks the object of 起こし |
| こし | okoshi | Verb (continuative) | cause; start — continuative form of 起こす *okosu*, linking to the next clause |

**Grammar Note:** 保ちたい is the desiderative (want-to) form. The entire phrase 平和を保ちたいという利己的な意志 is a heavy subject noun phrase: "the selfish will [of wanting to preserve peace]". 起こし is the continuative (masu-stem) form, used to chain this clause to the next line — the comma in Japanese represents this soft linkage.

---

### Line 11

**Japanese:** 愛を守るために憎しみが生まれる。  
**Romaji:** Ai o mamoru tame ni nikushimi ga umareru.  
**English:** "and hatred is born in order to protect love."

| Japanese | Romaji | Part of Speech | Gloss |
|----------|--------|----------------|-------|
| 愛 | ai | Noun | love |
| を | o | Object particle | marks the object of 守る |
| 守る | mamoru | Verb (attributive) | to protect — modifies ために |
| ために | tame ni | Compound particle | in order to; for the sake of |
| 憎しみ | nikushimi | Noun | hatred; hate |
| が | ga | Subject particle | marks the subject |
| 生まれる | umareru | Verb (passive/potential) | is born — spontaneous passive of 生む *umu* |

**Grammar Note:** [Verb in attributive form] + ために expresses purpose: "for the purpose of protecting love". 生まれる is the intransitive/passive form of 生む (to give birth to), meaning "comes into being / is born". The line forms a paradox: love, the most positive emotion, structurally necessitates hatred as its defensive shadow.

---

### Line 12

**Japanese:** これらは因果関係にあり切り離すことができん。  
**Romaji:** Korera wa inga kankei ni ari, kirihanasu koto ga dekin.  
**English:** "These exist in a causal relationship; they cannot be separated."

| Japanese | Romaji | Part of Speech | Gloss |
|----------|--------|----------------|-------|
| これら | korera | Pronoun | these |
| は | wa | Topic particle | as for these |
| 因果関係 | inga kankei | Noun | causal relationship; cause and effect |
| に | ni | Locative particle | in |
| あり | ari | Verb (continuative) | exist — continuative form of ある *aru*, linking to the next clause |
| 切り離す | kirihanasu | Verb | to separate; to cut apart; to sever |
| こと | koto | Nominalizer | the act of ~ |
| が | ga | Subject particle | marks the subject |
| できん | dekin | Auxiliary (negative) | cannot — rough/contracted negative of できる *dekiru* |

**Grammar Note:** あり is the classical continuative form of ある, functioning like a te-form to chain clauses: "These exist in causality, and [one] cannot separate them." できん is a rough, masculine negative of できる, more forceful than できない. The word 因果 (inga) is the Buddhist term for karma/causation, giving the speech theological weight.

---

### Line 13

**Japanese:** 本来はな…。  
**Romaji:** Honrai wa na…  
**English:** "That is how it originally is…" / "That is their true nature…"

| Japanese | Romaji | Part of Speech | Gloss |
|----------|--------|----------------|-------|
| 本来 | honrai | Adverb / Noun | originally; essentially; by nature |
| は | wa | Topic particle | as for [their] original nature |
| な | na | Final particle | rough, masculine sentence ender — "it is, you see" |

**Grammar Note:** An extremely compressed line. 本来はな implies "[That is how things are] by nature, [you see]." The copula だ is omitted, and な (a rough masculine emphatic particle) closes the sentence with a tone of finality. The ellipsis trails into the next conceptual section.

## Section 3: Madara's Declaration

### Line 14

**Japanese:** ……この世の因果を断ち切る。  
**Romaji:** ……Kono yo no inga o tachikiru.  
**English:** "…I will sever the causal bonds of this world."

| Japanese | Romaji | Part of Speech | Gloss |
|----------|--------|----------------|-------|
| この | kono | Determiner | this |
| 世 | yo | Noun | world |
| の | no | Genitive particle | of |
| 因果 | inga | Noun | causality; karma; cause and effect |
| を | o | Object particle | marks the object |
| 断ち切る | tachikiru | Verb | to sever; to cut off; to break off — emphatic form of 切る |

**Grammar Note:** 断ち切る combines 断つ (to cut off) with 切る (to cut) into an emphatic compound verb, intensifying the finality of the action. The subject (I) is omitted, which is common in Japanese when the speaker is clearly referring to themselves in a declaration.

---

### Line 15

**Japanese:** 勝者だけの世界。  
**Romaji:** Shousha dake no sekai.  
**English:** "A world of only victors."

| Japanese | Romaji | Part of Speech | Gloss |
|----------|--------|----------------|-------|
| 勝者 | shousha | Noun | victor |
| だけ | dake | Limiting particle | only |
| の | no | Genitive particle | of; 's |
| 世界 | sekai | Noun | world |

**Grammar Note:** A nominal phrase with no verb — typical of proclamations. だけの creates an exclusive modifier: "a world consisting only of victors." The copula だ is omitted for punchiness.

---

### Line 16

**Japanese:** 平和だけの世界。  
**Romaji:** Heiwa dake no sekai.  
**English:** "A world of only peace."

| Japanese | Romaji | Part of Speech | Gloss |
|----------|--------|----------------|-------|
| 平和 | heiwa | Noun | peace |
| だけ | dake | Limiting particle | only |
| の | no | Genitive particle | of |
| 世界 | sekai | Noun | world |

**Grammar Note:** Parallel structure with the previous line. The anaphoric repetition of 〜だけの世界 creates a rhythmic, chant-like cadence — a rhetorical device called anaphora.

---

### Line 17

**Japanese:** 愛だけの世界。  
**Romaji:** Ai dake no sekai.  
**English:** "A world of only love."

| Japanese | Romaji | Part of Speech | Gloss |
|----------|--------|----------------|-------|
| 愛 | ai | Noun | love |
| だけ | dake | Limiting particle | only |
| の | no | Genitive particle | of |
| 世界 | sekai | Noun | world |

**Grammar Note:** The third repetition completes the tricolon. In rhetoric, three parallel phrases create a sense of completeness and inevitability. Each phrase strips away the shadow-side of a duality: victors without losers, peace without war, love without hatred.

---

### Line 18

**Japanese:** それらだけの世界を造る。  
**Romaji:** Sorera dake no sekai o tsukuru.  
**English:** "I will create such a world." (lit. "I will create a world of only those things.")

| Japanese | Romaji | Part of Speech | Gloss |
|----------|--------|----------------|-------|
| それら | sorera | Pronoun | those; those things |
| だけ | dake | Limiting particle | only |
| の | no | Genitive particle | of |
| 世界 | sekai | Noun | world |
| を | o | Object particle | marks the object |
| 造る | tsukuru | Verb | to create; to build; to construct — written with 造 (archaic/formal) rather than 作 |

**Grammar Note:** それら refers back to the three preceding ideals. 造る (using the kanji 造) carries connotations of divine or grand creation, as opposed to the more mundane 作る. The subject "I" is again omitted, but the declarative force is carried by the plain-form verb ending.

## Section 4: Identity & Final Judgment

### Line 19

**Japanese:** オレは……うちはの亡霊。  
**Romaji:** Ore wa…… Uchiha no bourei.  
**English:** "I am… the ghost of the Uchiha."

| Japanese | Romaji | Part of Speech | Gloss |
|----------|--------|----------------|-------|
| オレ | ore | Pronoun | I — rough, masculine first-person pronoun |
| は | wa | Topic particle | as for me |
| うちは | uchiha | Proper noun | Uchiha (clan name) |
| の | no | Genitive particle | of; 's |
| 亡霊 | bourei | Noun | ghost; specter; departed spirit |

**Grammar Note:** The copula だ is omitted after 亡霊, creating a dramatic pause filled by the ellipsis. オレ is the roughest common first-person pronoun, asserting raw ego. Calling himself a 亡霊 (ghost) rather than a 忍者 or 忍 emphasizes that Madara sees himself as something that should be dead yet persists — a revenant outside the normal order.

---

### Line 20

**Japanese:** うちはマダラだ。  
**Romaji:** Uchiha Madara da.  
**English:** "I am Madara Uchiha."

| Japanese | Romaji | Part of Speech | Gloss |
|----------|--------|----------------|-------|
| うちはマダラ | uchiha madara | Proper noun | Madara Uchiha (name) |
| だ | da | Copula | is; am — plain, assertive |

**Grammar Note:** The simplest possible sentence structure — [Name] + だ — yet devastating in context. After the philosophical buildup, this blunt self-naming lands with the force of a verdict. Japanese names are given family-first: Uchiha (clan) + Madara (given name).

---

### Line 21

**Japanese:** この現実こそ……地獄だ。  
**Romaji:** Kono genjitsu koso…… jigoku da.  
**English:** "This reality… is truly hell."

| Japanese | Romaji | Part of Speech | Gloss |
|----------|--------|----------------|-------|
| この | kono | Determiner | this |
| 現実 | genjitsu | Noun | reality |
| こそ | koso | Emphatic particle | precisely; indeed; none other than — strongly emphatic |
| 地獄 | jigoku | Noun | hell |
| だ | da | Copula | is |

**Grammar Note:** こそ is one of the strongest emphatic particles in Japanese. It replaces the topic particle は and singles out the noun as the one true thing: "Reality — and nothing else — is hell." This line bookends the speech, returning to the opening word 現実 and closing the philosophical arc. The ellipsis after こそ creates a dramatic beat before the final noun drops like a hammer.

## Section 5: Key Grammar Patterns Summary

| Pattern | Meaning | Example from Speech |
|---------|---------|---------------------|
| **〜ば…ほど** | "The more X, the more Y" | 長く生きれば生きるほど… |
| **〜ことに気付く** | "To realize that [clause]" | 漂っていることに気付く |
| **〜という概念 / 〜という意志** | "The concept of ~ / the will [of/desiring] ~" | 勝者という概念、平和を保ちたいという意志 |
| **〜ために** | "In order to ~ / for the sake of ~" | 愛を守るために |
| **〜以上** | "As long as / given that" | 勝者という概念がある以上 |
| **〜こそ** | "Precisely / none other than" | この現実こそ |
