Grammar: The song opens with the te-form 教えて used as an imperative: "Tell me." The repetition (教えて, 教えてよ) with the added emphatic particle よ on the second iteration creates escalating desperation. 仕組み is a telling word choice: it means "mechanism; structure; workings," not 理由 (reason) or 意味 (meaning). The speaker doesn't ask WHY they are changing; they ask HOW the mechanism works, as if their own identity were a machine they don't understand. 僕の中に誰がいるの uses の as a soft question marker, more vulnerable than か. The existential verb いる (for animate beings) rather than ある (for inanimate objects) is crucial: whoever is inside is a person, a consciousness, a living thing. The opening line is the song's central terror: the sense that one's identity has been colonized by something else.

Grammar: 壊れた is the past/attributive form of 壊れる (to break; to be broken), used here without a noun to modify, creating a fragment that feels like a gasp: "[It is] broken." The repetition with よ mirrors the opening's structure, but where 教えてよ was a plea, 壊れたよ is a confession. 何も見えずに uses the universal negative pattern 何も + negative verb = "not seeing anything; without seeing anything." The adverbial form 見えず (not seeing) + に creates a concurrent state: "while not seeing anything; in a state of seeing nothing." The tragedy is precise: "you" laugh because you cannot see what has broken. The speaker's transformation is invisible to the one person whose perception matters most. The grammar of blindness (見えず) makes the beloved's innocence into a kind of cruelty.

Grammar: [Noun/pronoun] + なんて + さ is a self-deprecating pattern: "someone like [me], [you know]." 壊れた僕なんてさ = "someone like broken me." The さ final particle is casual and masculine, adding a flat, resigned tone. 息を止めて is the te-form of 息を止める (to hold one's breath), used here as a concurrent action or a command to oneself: "holding my breath" or "I hold my breath." ほどける means "to come undone; to unravel; to untie," and its negative potential ほどけない means "cannot unravel." This is the song's title word, and its repetition (ほどけない / もう / ほどけないよ) creates a tightening, claustrophobic feeling: the more the speaker tries to escape their transformation, the more trapped they become. 真実さえ freeze uses さえ (even; including) to emphasize that truth itself, the most fundamental thing, has frozen solid. The English "freeze" shatters the Japanese sentence like ice cracking, a bilingual intrusion that mirrors the speaker's fracturing identity.

02 Pre-Chorus

壊せる 壊せない 狂える 狂えない あなたを見つけて
Kowaseru, kowasenai, kurueru, kuruenai, anata wo mitsukete.
"Can destroy, can't destroy. Can go mad, can't go mad. Finding you."
JapaneseRomajiPart of SpeechGloss
壊せるkowaseruVerb (potential)can destroy/break — potential of 壊す kowasu
壊せないkowasenaiVerb (potential negative)can't destroy — potential negative of 壊す kowasu
狂えるkurueruVerb (potential)can go mad — potential of 狂う kuruu
狂えないkuruenaiVerb (potential negative)can't go mad — potential negative of 狂う kuruu
あなたanataPronounyou — formal/distant
をwoObject particlemarks the object

Grammar: The pre-chorus is built on a stunningly simple grammatical pattern: [potential] + [potential negative], repeated. 壊せる (can destroy) / 壊せない (can't destroy). 狂える (can go mad) / 狂えない (can't go mad). The potential form expresses capability, and its negation expresses incapability. The verbs are carefully chosen: 壊す (to destroy; to break [something]) is the transitive counterpart to 壊れる (to be broken), suggesting the speaker now has the power to destroy others, not merely be destroyed. 狂う (to go mad; to lose one's mind) is a strong, visceral verb. The te-form 見つけて at the end connects this litany of contradictory capabilities to the chorus that follows: "finding you" is the context in which all these possibilities exist. The shift from 君 (intimate "you") in the verse to あなた (formal/distant "you") is significant: as the speaker feels themselves becoming monstrous, they put distance between themselves and the beloved. The grammar of potentiality (can/can't) creates a grid of possible selves, all suspended around the act of finding the other.

Grammar: 揺れた and 歪んだ are both past/attributive forms modifying 世界: "the world [that] shook and twisted." The comma after 揺れた creates a breathless, listing quality. だんだん is an adverb of gradual progression: "gradually; little by little." 透き通る means "to be transparent; to be see-through," and its te-form 透き通って connects to the next verb phrase. 見えなくなって combines the negative adverbial 見えなく (invisibly; unable to be seen) with なって (becoming): "becoming unable to be seen" = "becoming invisible." The te-form at the end connects to the following imperative sentences. The grammar of gradual disappearance (だんだん + te-form chain) makes the invisibility feel inevitable, a process already underway. The world distorts, and the speaker fades within it, as if the two transformations are causally linked: a broken world makes broken people transparent.
Chorus
見つけないで 僕のことを 見つめないで
Mitsukenaide, boku no koto wo, mitsumenaide.
"Don't find me. Don't gaze at me."
JapaneseRomajiPart of SpeechGloss

Grammar: [Verb in negative] + ないで is the negative request form: "don't [verb]." 見つけないで = "don't find [me]." 見つめないで = "don't gaze at [me]." The two verbs form a chilling progression: 見つける (to find; to discover) is about locating someone who is lost or hidden; 見つめる (to gaze at; to stare at) is about sustained, intense looking. The speaker first asks not to be found, then asks not to be looked at even if found. 僕のこと uses the nominalizer こと to turn the pronoun into an object of comprehensive scope: "me as a person; everything about me; the fact of my existence." Saying 僕のことを instead of just 僕を makes the rejection more total: don't find ANY of me. The grammar of negated discovery (見つけないで) and negated perception (見つめないで) expresses the core fear of the monstrous: being seen in one's true, transformed state.

Grammar: 誰かが描いた modifies 界: "the world [that] someone drew." The image is of a world that is artificial, constructed, not natural: someone designed this reality, and the speaker is trapped inside it. 傷つけたくはない is the desiderative negative with contrastive は: 傷つける (to hurt) + たい (want to) → 傷つけたい (want to hurt) → 傷つけたく (adverbial) + は (contrastive) + ない (not). The word 鮮やか is the emotional climax of the chorus: the speaker wants to be remembered not as they are becoming, but as they once were, bright and clear and undamaged. The grammar of preservation (まま) against the grammar of transformation (だんだん...なって) creates the song's central tension.

Grammar: 動けない is the potential negative of 動く (to move): "cannot move." The repetition six times creates a claustrophobic, panic-attack rhythm. Each repetition is grammatically identical but emotionally escalated, with the final よ adding a note of desperate confession. The English phrase "unravelling the world" inserted after the Japanese creates a bilingual fracture. Grammatically, it could be read as an apposition to the repeated 動けない: "[I am] unravelling the world [even though I] can't move." Or it could be a separate declaration: "[I] can't move. The English "unravelling" echoes the Japanese ほどけない from the first verse, creating a bridge between the two languages at the exact center of the song. The grammar of repetition (動けない x6) performs paralysis; the grammar of bilingual intrusion (unravelling the world) performs the very unraveling it names.

05 Verse 3

変わってしまった 変えられなかった 2つが絡まる 2人が滅びる
Kawatte shimatta, kaerarenakatta, futatsu ga karamaru, futari ga horobiru.
"Changed completely. Couldn't change [it]. Two things entangle. Two people perish."

Grammar: 変わってしまった uses [te-form] + しまう, where しまう as an auxiliary adds a sense of completion with regret, resignation, or irreversibility: "changed [completely/regrettably/irreversibly]." The change is done, over, and the speaker laments it. 変えられなかった is the negative past of the potential form of 変える (to change [something]): "could not change [it]." The contrast between 変わる (intransitive: to change [oneself]) and 変える (transitive: to change [something else]) is crucial: the speaker changed (against their will, 変わってしまった) but could not change the situation (変えられなかった). 2つ (two things) and 2人 (two people) are phonetically similar but semantically distinct, creating a wordplay: two abstract things entangle, and two concrete people perish as a result. 滅びる is a strong, literary verb for destruction, often used for the fall of nations, species, or worlds. Applied to 2人, it elevates a personal tragedy to cosmic significance. The four short sentences are grammatically complete but emotionally compressed, like gasps between sobs.

Kowaseru, kowasenai, kurueru, kuruenai, anata wo yogosenai yo.
"Can destroy, can't destroy. Can go mad, can't go mad. I can't defile you."
JapaneseRomajiPart of SpeechGloss
汚せないyogosenaiVerb (potential negative)can't defile; can't stain — potential negative of 汚す yogosu
よyoFinal particleemphatic; assertive
Grammar: This pre-chorus mirrors the first pre-chorus exactly in structure (four potential verbs in pairs), but replaces あなたを見つけて (finding you) with あなたを汚せないよ (I can't defile you). The first pre-chorus ended with a te-form that connected forward; this one ends with a complete sentence, a declaration. 汚す means "to defile; to stain; to dirty; to pollute," and its potential negative 汚せない means "cannot defile." The verb carries moral and physical weight: it suggests making something pure into something impure. The speaker acknowledges their capacity for destruction and madness (壊せる, 狂える) but draws a line at defiling the beloved. This is the song's moral center: transformation may be inevitable, but choice remains possible. The grammar of potential negation (汚せない) expresses not incapacity but refusal: the speaker COULD defile the beloved (they have the power), but they WON'T. The よ makes it a vowel, a promise shouted into the distortion.

07 Key Grammar Patterns Summary

〜てしまう
Completion with regret/irreversibility. 変わってしまった. Expresses changes that are complete, unwanted, and cannot be undone.
〜える / 〜えない (Potential)
"Can / can't [verb]." The song's central grammatical engine. 壊せる/壊せない, 狂える/狂えない, 汚せない. Creates grids of capability and refusal.
〜なくて / 〜ずに
Negative te-form / adverbial negative. 見えずに, 見えなくなって. Expresses absence, incapacity, and gradual disappearance.
〜ないで
Negative request: "don't [verb]." 見つけないで, 見つめないで. The grammar of hiding, of begging not to be perceived.
〜たくはない
Desiderative negative with contrastive は. 傷つけたくはない. "As for wanting to [verb], [I] don't." Emphatic refusal.
〜ていて (Imperative Progressive)
"Be [verb]ing [continuously]." 覚えていて. A command for ongoing remembrance, not a single act.
〜なまま
"As it is; still; remaining." 鮮やかなまま. The grammar of preservation against transformation.
Attributive Fragments
Past/attributive verbs used without nouns as complete utterances. 壊れた, 壊れたよ. The grammar of gasps, of speech beyond sentences.
