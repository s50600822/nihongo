# Korean Language Lesson: Master "맛 표현" (Flavor Descriptions)

Welcome to your guide on **맛 표현** (mat pyo-hyeon). Korean culture revolves deeply around food, and because of this, the Korean language has developed an exceptionally rich vocabulary to describe how things taste. 

In this lesson, you will learn the basic building blocks, everyday nuanced vocabulary, texture terms, and cultural slang related to food.

---

## 1. The Big Five: Basic Tastes (기본적인 맛)

These are the fundamental words for taste. In Korean, these are descriptive verbs (adjectives) in their dictionary forms. To use them in a sentence, you change the ending.

| Dictionary Form | Pronunciation | English | Polite Present Tense Form |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **달다** | dal-da | Sweet | **달아요** (da-ra-yo) |
| **짜다** | jja-da | Salty | **짜요** (jja-yo) |
| **맵다** | maep-da | Spicy | **매워요** (mae-wo-yo) |
| **시다** | si-da | Sour | **셔요** (syeo-yo) / **시큼해요** |
| **쓰다** | sseu-da | Bitter | **써요** (sseo-yo) |

### Simple Sentence Pattern:
> **[Food Item] + 이/가 + [Taste Form].**
*   케이크**가** 달아요. (The cake is sweet.)
*   국**이** 짜요. (The soup is salty.)

---

## 2. Level Up: Nuanced Flavor Descriptors

Koreans rarely just say something is "spicy" or "sweet." Instead, they use specific words that describe *how* something tastes. The suffix **-콤하다** generally implies a pleasant, delicious, or subtle note.

*   **매콤하다 (mae-kom-hada):** Pleasantly, beautifully spicy (e.g., Tteokbokki).
*   **달콤하다 (dal-kom-hada):** Deliciously sweet (e.g., ripe fruit, high-end chocolate).
*   **새콤하다 (sae-kom-hada):** Refreshingly sour/tangy (e.g., a lemon dessert or fresh vinaigrette).
*   **고소하다 (go-so-hada):** Nutty, aromatic, toasted, or savory (e.g., sesame oil, roasted almonds, freshly baked bread).
*   **구수하다 (gu-su-hada):** Deeply earthy, hearty, and comforting savory taste (e.g., roasted barley tea, soybean paste stew).
*   **감칠맛 (gam-chil-mat):** *Noun.* Umami; a deep, rich, lip-smacking flavor.

---

## 3. Beyond Taste: Textures (식감 - Sik-gam)

In Korean **맛 표현**, texture is just as important as flavor. **식감** translates literally to "food feeling."

*   **바삭바삭하다 (ba-sak-ba-sak-hada):** Extremely crispy or crunchy (e.g., Korean fried chicken).
*   **쫄깃쫄깃하다 (jjol-git-jjol-git-hada):** Chewy, bouncy, and elastic (e.g., rice cakes, good noodles).
*   **부드럽다 (bu-deu-reop-da):** Soft, smooth, and meltingly tender (e.g., tofu, cheesecake).
*   **아삭아삭하다 (a-sak-a-sak-hada):** Crisp and crunchy for fresh produce (e.g., fresh apples, freshly made kimchi).

## 4. Modern Slang & Pop-Culture Metaphors

Flavors in Korea are often used metaphorically to describe situations, personalities, and television content.

### The Spice Scale (매운맛)
*   **순한맛 (Mild Taste):** Wholesome, PG-rated, therapeutic, or easy content.
*   **매운맛 (Spicy Taste):** Intense, savage, shocking, or hardcore (e.g., dramatic reality TV or brutal video game difficulty).
*   **핵매운맛 (Nuclear Spicy Taste):** Extremely chaotic, scandalous, or mind-blowingly difficult.

### Other Popular Terms
*   **단짠단짠 (Dan-jjan-dan-jjan):** *Sweet-Salty-Sweet-Salty.* The ultimate flavor combination strategy where you loop eating sweet and salty foods back-to-back so you never get full.
*   **겉바속촉 (Geot-ba-sok-chok):** *Crispy on the outside, moist on the inside.* The ultimate gold standard for fried or baked food.
