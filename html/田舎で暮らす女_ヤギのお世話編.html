Grammar: あ、怒ってる uses the interjection あ (ah; oh) + progressive 怒ってる (is angry): "Ah, [it's] angry." The progressive て-form + いる (contracted to てる/でる in casual speech) marks an ongoing emotional state. 暴れんの? uses the verb 暴れる (to act wild; to struggle) + contracted nominalized question んの?: "Is [it] acting wild?" The んの contraction (from の) turns the verb into a nominalized question, common in casual speech when reacting to animal behavior. The repetition 暴れんの? 暴れんの? creates a sense of mounting concern as the goat becomes agitated. ヤッホー is a friendly, non-threatening greeting call used to approach animals; its placement after the concern shows the vlogger's attempt to calm the situation. あっち行った uses the colloquial demonstrative あっち (over there; that way) + past 行った (went): "[It] went over there." The fragment じ、じん is likely an ASR artifact or a hesitation sound; it may represent the vlogger pointing or the goat's movement. 逃げる is the plain non-past form used as an immediate observation: "[It's] running away." The plain form in live narration functions as a present-tense report of ongoing action.

The volitional + か creates a self-directed deliberation, a common Japanese pattern when psyching oneself up for a difficult task. よし is the readiness interjection that affirms the decision: "Okay; here goes." で、あ、薬持ってきてる uses the continuative particle で (and; then) + realization interjection あ (ah) + progressive directional compound 持ってきてる (have brought = 持ってくる in progressive): "Then, ah, [I] brought medicine." The directional くる in compound verbs marks movement toward the speaker; 持ってくる means "to bring" (as opposed to 持っていく, "to take"). 薬がいるわ uses the subject marker が + verb いる (need; required) + particle わ: "[I] need medicine." Note that いる (要る, to need) is distinct from いる (居る, to exist/animate), though they share the same form. The particle わ is a sentence-ending particle common in Kansai and female speech that adds emphasis and emotional color. 元気ないわ uses the noun 元気 (energy; health) + negative ない + わ: "[It's] not energetic." The absence of a copula (だ/です) is standard in casual self-directed speech. The sequence (brought medicine → need medicine → [goat is] not energetic) establishes the medical context for the hoof-trimming procedure.

07 Key Grammar Patterns Summary

〜てしまう / 〜ちゃう (Completion / Regret Auxiliary)
食ちゃう, なっちゃう. "[I'll] eat [it all] up;" "ends up becoming." The grammar of inevitable completion and slightly regrettable thoroughness in casual rural speech.
〜すぎる (Excessive Auxiliary)
切れすぎた. "[I] cut too much." The grammar of action beyond the appropriate degree, essential for describing mistakes in animal care and manual work.
Kansai Dialect Particles and Copulas
わ, やん, で, ええ, えらい, な. The soft assertive わ, the benefactive auxiliary やん, the copula で, the affirmative ええ, the praise adjective えらい, and the exclamatory な form the emotional texture of rural Kansai-influenced domestic narration.
Volitional + か (Self-Directed Deliberation)
頑張ろうか, 切り頑張ろうか. "Shall [we] do [our] best?" "Shall [we] try cutting?" The grammar of psyching oneself up before difficult physical tasks.
Telegraphic Imperatives in Physical Labor
上げて。前。こっち。開けて。たって。待って。向いて。 The particle-dropping imperative grammar of urgent physical direction, standard when hands are occupied and speed matters.
Effort Grunts and Onomatopoeia
よいしょ, うい, せーの. The non-lexical grammar of physical exertion: heave-ho, effort grunts, and coordination cues that structure manual labor rhythm.
ASR Uncertainty and Colloquial Fragmentation
じん, ことなってる, がるか, くりゃな, めちゃいさん足げて, 高速マス君ます. The grammar of machine-transcribed rural speech: where dialect, animal sounds, child voices, and acoustic chaos meet automatic speech recognition.
Rural Domestic and Animal-Care Vocabulary
ヤギ, 蹄, hoof trimming, おっこ, 体重かける, えらい, おしまい, 白パン, おかず, タレ. The specialized lexicon of Japanese countryside animal husbandry, home baking, and thrifty lunch preparation.
Standard Vlog Outro Rhetoric
ご視聴ありがとうございました, 〜てもらえると嬉しいです, よろしくお願いします, お会いしましょう. The formal grammar of Japanese YouTube audience engagement: gratitude, conditional requests, and honorific farewells.
