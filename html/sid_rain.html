六月の嘘 目の前の本当 セピアにしまいこみ
Rokugatsu no uso, me no mae no hontou, sepia ni shimaikomi.
"June's lies, the truth before my eyes, locked away in sepia."

Grammar: This is a sequence of noun phrases with the verb しまいこみ (continuative form) at the end, creating a compressed, poetic syntax. セピアにしまいこむ means "to shut away into sepia" — the metaphor suggests that both the lies and the truth of June (a past relationship) have been preserved like an old photograph, faded into a nostalgic brown tone. The continuative form links this phrase to the next line without a conjunction.

The contraction なってた gives the line a raw, conversational intimacy. The speaker has been in a state of emotional numbness for some time.

Grammar: The quoted line 「君はひとりで平気だから ね」 is the excuse the other person gave for leaving — or possibly what the speaker told themselves. The te-form 押しつけて connects directly to さよなら, compressing the action into a single devastating motion: forcing the words, then leaving.

The speaker logically knows they should be over this kind of hollow consolation, but emotionally they are not — the pain persists despite reason.

02 Verse 1 (Continued)

Grammar: 鳴り止む combines 鳴る (to ring/sound) + 止む (to stop): "to stop echoing / ringing out." The negative 鳴り止まない personifies the memories as sounds that refuse to go silent. 〜てくれそうに(も)ない is a complex construction: 〜てくれる (do for me) + そうだ (seem/appear) + にもない (not even). Literally: "they don't even appear likely to forgive me." The personification is powerful: memories are treated as active agents that could choose to forgive but refuse to.

The image is haunting: when the speaker closes their eyes, memories intensify, and the lost person's smile appears at a remove — close enough to see but too far to touch.

The speaker admits that instead of moving forward (次), they have been running after what has already gone (過). The single-kanji 過 is particularly striking — a fragment of a word representing a fragment of a life.

The line juxtaposes the two people: the one who gave, and the one who could only resent and fear. The と is not just "and" but carries the weight of a relationship now fractured.

The compound verb こぼれ落ちる combines "to spill / overflow" with "to fall / drop," creating an image of emotional overflow that the speaker can no longer contain.

05 Bridge

過去を知りたがらない瞳 洗い流してくれる指

JapaneseRomajiPart of SpeechGloss

Grammar: [Verb stem] + たがる expresses a third person's desire: "wants to [verb]." 知りたがらない = "doesn't want to know." 洗い流す is a compound verb: 洗う (to wash) + 流す (to flow / wash away). Combined with くれる (to do for me), it creates a tender image: someone else's fingers washing away the speaker's past pain. The bridge introduces a new presence — someone who offers cleansing and doesn't need to know the speaker's history. This is the first hint of possible healing.

The new person's gentle presence heals, but a gap remains — the speaker is not fully ready to be reached.

07 Key Grammar Patterns Summary

〜のに / 〜なのに
"Although / even though / and yet" — concessive particle expressing regret, disappointment, or emotional contradiction. Central to the song's emotional logic.
〜ばかり
"Only; merely; nothing but" — expresses limitation or continuous repetitive action. "Does nothing but ~."
〜てくれる / 〜てくれそうに(も)ない
"Do for me" auxiliary + conjectural + negative — "doesn't even seem like it will ~ for me." Personifies memories as agents capable of forgiveness.
〜そうだ / 〜そうに(も)ない
Conjectural auxiliary: "seems like / appears that." Combined with negative: "doesn't even appear likely to ~."
〜ながら
"While ~ing" — marks simultaneous action. The song ends on this note of ongoing, incomplete healing.
〜ずに
Negative adverbial: "without ~ing." Classical/literary negative form. "Not knowing how to stop."
〜たがる
"Wants to ~" — expresses a third person's desire. "Eyes that don't want to know the past."
〜そうで / 〜そうで〜ない
Conjectural + contrast: "seems ~ and yet [is] not ~." Creates tantalizing near-miss: "seems reachable, yet unreachable."
