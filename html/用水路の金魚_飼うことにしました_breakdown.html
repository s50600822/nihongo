Grammar: 3匹なんか1匹 is a fragmented count where the vlogger starts with one number then self-corrects: "Three fish, or like, one [wait that's not right]." This is the grammar of live counting, where the speaker thinks aloud and revises. 泣かされちゃった is a complex verbal construction: 泣く (to cry) in causative (泣かす, to make cry) + passive (泣かされる, to be made to cry) + てしまった (regrettable/completed action) = "[I] got made to cry [and it affected me]." The causative-passive combination (泣かされる) expresses being affected by someone/something else's action, and the ちゃった adds emotional weight. In context, the vlogger is saying the situation (seeing goldfish abandoned in a canal) is so moving/sad it "made them cry." いてる is the colloquial progressive contraction of いる (to exist [animate]) = いて + いる → てる: "[they] are [there]." Its repetition (いてるいてる) is the grammar of excited confirmation, common when spotting something. なんか1匹違う気するけど uses なんか (somehow; like) + [number] + 違う気がする (feel different/wrong): "[I] somehow feel like one fish is different." The omission of が in 気がする is standard in casual speech. ちゃう is the Kansai form of 違う (different; wrong), used here as a self-correction: "[That's] wrong [it is] = Wait, that's not right." The grammar of counting and self-correction (3匹→1匹→ちゃう→4匹) performs the cognitive process of enumeration in real time.

Grammar: この頃 is a temporal noun phrase meaning "around this time; recently; lately." Its use as a standalone sentence fragment sets the temporal frame for the rescue narrative. 今から網で救います uses 今から (from now) + instrumental で + polite verb: "From now, [I] will rescue [them] with a net." The verb 救う (sukuu, to rescue; to save) is stronger than 助ける (tasukeru, to help) and carries connotations of salvation and deliverance, appropriate for the moral framing of rescuing abandoned pets. よいしょ is the quintessential Japanese effort sound, used when lifting, pulling, or exerting physical force. It has no lexical meaning but functions as a paralinguistic marker of labor. 先のか is a truncated question: 先の[魚]か = "[Is it] the front one?" The noun is omitted because the referent is clear from context. 逃げられるな uses the potential/passive form of 逃げる (to escape) + assertive な: "[They] will escape, [I'm telling you]." The potential 逃げられる can express either ability (can escape) or passive (be escaped by), but in this context it functions as a prediction of the fish's action: "[They're] going to get away." 取れた is the past potential of 取る: "could take = got [one]." The potential past in Japanese often expresses successful achievement rather than mere ability: "[I] managed to get [one]." 流さちゃった uses the passive 流される (to be washed away; to be carried away by current) + てしまった (regrettable completion): "[It] got washed away [unfortunately]." The passive marks the fish as the victim of the water's force, and ちゃった adds the vlogger's dismay at losing it.

Grammar: 出てこうへんかな uses the Kansai negative potential form へん attached to the compound verb 出てくる (to come out): 出てこない (Standard) → 出てこうへん (Kansai). The かな adds dubitative wondering: "Won't [they] come out, I wonder?" This is the grammar of anxious waiting, where the vlogger worries that a fish swept into a pipe or culvert won't reappear. 流された is the plain past passive: "[It] was washed away." Unlike 流されちゃった (which adds regret), the plain passive is a factual statement of what happened. The triple よし is the grammar of incremental reassurance, marking each small success (the fish entering a safe spot, the net positioned correctly) before the final confirmation. これでここ5匹全部取った uses これで (with this; by this means) + location ここ + quantity + 全部 + past verb: "With this, [I] got all 5 fish here." これで is one of the most important discourse markers in Japanese vlogs, signaling task completion and transition. The grammar of enumeration (1、2、3、4、5匹) performed aloud is a vlog staple that lets the audience participate in the counting. 次のとこ uses the colloquial contraction とこ (ところ, place) as a standalone noun: "next spot." The omission of the particle after 次のとこ is standard in telegraphic, task-oriented speech.

Grammar: おってんけど is a rich Kansai contraction: おる (Kansai いる, to exist [animate]) + て + ん (explanatory nominalizer の) + けど (concessive "but") = "[they] are [there], [you see]." The けど softens the assertion and invites the listener's attention, making it less blunt than a plain statement. This is one of the most characteristic Kansai dialect constructions in casual speech. どっか行ってしまった uses the colloquial adverb どっか (どこか, somewhere) + 行ってしまった (went [regrettably]): "[they] went somewhere [and I lost them]." The てしまう auxiliary adds a sense of finality and regret to the disappearance. 時間経ってからまた来ます uses the te-form + から (after) + また (again) + verb: "[I'll] come again after time has passed." 経つ (tatsu) is the verb for time passing, and its te-form creates a temporal clause. 疾風黒とレンジの1匹 uses goldfish variety terminology as noun modifiers without copulas: "[one] hayate-black and [one] orange." レンジ is a colloquial clipping of オレンジ (orenji, orange), common in casual Japanese when describing animal colors. ヒレの横にちょっと出血してて uses the progressive te-form contraction してて (していて): "bleeding a bit beside the fin [and]." The te-form links the bleeding to the parasite attachment, creating a medical description chain. 寄生虫ついてる細長いやつ uses the attributive progressive ついてる (付いている) as a pre-noun modifier: "the long thin one [with] parasites attached." The grammar of pathological description (出血してて + 寄生虫ついてる) is the language of animal rescue, where physical symptoms are observed and narrated for the audience's concern.

Grammar: 一旦これで帰ります uses 一旦 (ittan, for now; temporarily) to mark a provisional conclusion: "[I'll] go home with this for now." 一旦 is essential to Japanese task-management grammar, signaling that an activity is being paused, not completed. 結局全部捕まえることできましたと uses 結局 (in the end) + [verb phrase nominalized with こと] + potential past polite + quotative と: "[That I] was able to catch all [of them], [I say]." The と at the end marks the entire preceding clause as a quoted summary statement, almost like signing off on a report. The grammar of retrospective summary (結局〜できましたと) is common at the end of a task segment. こんな容器しかなかったから uses the exclusive pattern しか + negative: [noun] + しか + ない = "only [noun]; nothing but [noun]." しか requires a negative verb, creating a construction where the presence of only one option is framed through negation of alternatives. 水合わせしていきます uses the aquarium technical term 水合わせ (mizuawase, water acclimation: the process of gradually equalizing water temperature and parameters between the fish's current water and the new tank water) + していく: "[I'll] go ahead and do water acclimation." The grammar of fish-keeping jargon (水合わせ) marks the vlogger's domain knowledge and frames the rescue as requiring technical care rather than just goodwill.

Grammar: 溝におった子たちで uses the Kansai past おった (were) as an attributive modifier: "the children [that] were in the ditch." The で after the noun phrase connects to the following なります (will become): "[These] will become senior goldfish [to] the ones [that] were in the ditch." The use of 先輩 (senpai) for fish is anthropomorphic humor, applying Japanese social hierarchy to aquarium inhabitants. The new rescued fish will be "juniors" (後輩, kouhai) to the existing fish. すごい勢いで茂ってきた uses すごい (amazing; intense) as an adjectival modifier for 勢い (vigor; momentum): "with amazing vigor." で marks the manner. 茂ってきた is the directional compound 茂る (shigeru, to grow thick; to luxuriate) + 来る: "have grown thick [coming toward the present]." The くる indicates that the overgrowth has been developing and is now visible as a problem. 手入れせないか is a Kansai contraction of 手入れしなければならないか (have to do maintenance): し → せ (Kansai verb stem variation) + ないか. The か at the end is not a genuine question but a self-directed rhetorical marker: "[I] gotta do maintenance, [don't I]." なんやっけな is the Kansai form of なんだっけな (nan dakke na): "What was it again?" やっけ is the Kansai contraction of だっけ (dakke), which itself is a casual contraction of だ + っけ (recollection marker). The grammar of forgetting (なんやっけな → 忘れた) creates a comic moment of failed expertise, humanizing the vlogger.

This refers to an under-gravel filter system, a common aquarium filtration method. フタがきついな uses the adjective きつい (tight; tough) + reflective な: "The lid [is] tight, [huh]." しっかり太陽当たったら uses the adverb しっかり (firmly; properly) + conditional 当たったら: "if the sun hits [it] firmly." The grammar of DIY completion (できました → 完成です → できました) creates a triple confirmation of task success.

Grammar: ビビりながら食べてる uses the noun ビビり (bibiri, being scared; jitters — from the slang verb ビビる, to be scared) + ながら + progressive verb: "eating while being scared." ながら is the simultaneous-action auxiliary that places two states in the same temporal frame without subordinating either. The fish are both eating AND scared, and both conditions are equally real. 飛び出すぎ uses the verb 飛び出す (to jump out; to leap out) + auxiliary すぎる clipped to すぎ: "jumping out too much." The omission of る is common in hurried speech. 買ってきたんで uses the compound verb 買ってくる (buy and come back) in its past attributive form + ので (contracted to んで): "[I] bought [it] [and came back], [you see]." The ので presents the purchase as the reason for starting treatment. 治療始めたいと思います uses the desiderative 始めたい (want to start) + quotative と + 思います (think): "[I] think [I'd] like to start treatment." This is the standard polite Japanese pattern for expressing intentions softly. よう見たら is the Kansai form of よく見たら (when [I] look closely): よう (Kansai よく) + 見たら (conditional of 見る). よく見たら is a common phrase for discovering something upon closer inspection. お尻何匹か付いてるんで uses the honorific お- + 尻 (bottom) + indefinite 何匹か (several fish) + progressive 付いている + ので: "[parasites] are attached to several [fish's] rear ends, [you see]." The omission of the parasite noun is typical in context-dependent speech; the referent is clear from the medicine discussion. していこうかなって思います uses the hortative していこう (shall [I] go ahead and do) + dubitative かな + quotative って + 思います: "[I] think [I] shall go ahead and do [this treatment]." The grammar of medical decision-making (よう見たら〜付いてるんで〜していこうかなって思います) structures the treatment as a reasoned response to observation rather than panic.

07 Key Grammar Patterns Summary

〜てんけど / 〜んやけど (Kansai Explanatory + Concessive)
おってんけど, あるんやけど, 高かってんけど. The signature Kansai softening pattern: explanatory の + や + けど.
〜やっけな / 〜なんやっけな (Kansai Recollection)
どの子やっけな, なんやっけな. Kansai for 〜だっけな = "what was ~ again?" The grammar of forgetting.
〜せないか (Kansai Obligation)
手入れせないか. Kansai for 〜しなければならないか = "gotta do ~."
〜へん (Kansai Negative)
開かへん, 近づかれへん, 出てこうへん. The systematic Kansai negative replacing 〜ない.
〜ちゃった / 〜されちゃった (Regrettable Completion)
泣かされちゃった, 流されちゃった. Passive/causative + てしまった = emotional impact of unfortunate events.
〜てほしい (Desiderative for Others' Actions)
頑張って欲しいな. Wanting animals to do their best = the grammar of emotional investment in pets.
〜ながら (Simultaneous Action)
ビビりながら食べてる. The grammar of doing two things at once: eating while being scared.
〜てくる / 〜ていく (Directional Compounds)
買ってくる, 作っていきます, 茂ってきた. The grammar of going, coming, and temporal progression.
