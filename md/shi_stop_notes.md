# 止

A study note on the readings, meanings, and vocabulary of **止**, with
Chinese, Cantonese, and Vietnamese comparisons.

## 1. Why does 止 have so many readings?

Japanese kanji often have more than one reading because Japanese adopted
Chinese characters to write an existing Japanese language. Two broad
reading categories developed:

-   **訓読み（くんよみ）**: native Japanese readings associated with the
    meaning of the character.
-   **音読み（おんよみ）**: readings adapted historically from Chinese
    pronunciations.

A character can also have several native readings because it is used in
different established Japanese words. Therefore, it is usually more
effective to learn the reading as part of a vocabulary item rather than
memorize an isolated list.

## 2. Etymology: why does 止 mean "stop"?

Originally, 止 was a **pictograph of a foot / sole** in oracle bone
script. The meaning "stop" developed from the idea of where the foot
comes to rest. This original "foot" meaning survives as a component in
characters such as **歩**（あるく, to walk）, which depicts two feet
moving forward.

Knowing this makes the character easier to remember: the place where the
foot stands still.

## 3. Main readings of 止

| Reading    | Type     | Core meaning                           | Example               |
|------------|----------|----------------------------------------|-----------------------|
| とまる     | 訓読み   | to stop; come to a halt (intransitive) | 車が止まる            |
| とめる     | 訓読み   | to stop something (transitive)         | 車を止める            |
| やむ       | 訓読み   | to cease; come to an end               | 雨が止む              |
| やめる     | 訓読み   | to quit; discontinue                   | 勉強を止める          |
| とどまる   | 訓読み   | to remain; stay                        | 現状にとどまる        |
| とどめる   | 訓読み   | to keep; retain; confine               | 記憶にとどめる        |
| し         | 音読み   | Sino-Japanese reading                  | 中止（ちゅうし）, 禁止（きんし） |

The readings とどまる and とどめる are less common in everyday writing
with 止; depending on the word and nuance, 留まる／留める may be used.

## 4. とまる and とめる: intransitive vs transitive

### 止まる（とまる）

Something stops or comes to a halt. The subject undergoes the change; it
does not directly stop something else.

-   車が止まった。  
    *The car stopped.*
-   時計が止まった。  
    *The clock stopped.*

### 止める（とめる）

Someone or something causes another thing to stop.

-   車を止めた。  
    *I stopped the car.*
-   音楽を止めてください。  
    *Please stop the music.*

A useful pattern is the contrast between:

-   **が + 止まる**: something stops
-   **を + 止める**: someone stops something

Compare other common verb pairs:

| Intransitive             | Transitive             |
|--------------------------|------------------------|
| 始まる（はじまる）       | 始める（はじめる）     |
| 決まる（きまる）         | 決める（きめる）       |
| 集まる（あつまる）       | 集める（あつめる）     |
| 止まる（とまる）         | 止める（とめる）       |

This is a useful tendency, not a universal rule for every Japanese verb.

## 5. やむ and やめる: cease vs quit

These readings are worth distinguishing carefully.

### 止む（やむ）

Usually describes something coming to an end or ceasing, often a natural
phenomenon.

-   雨が止んだ。  
    *The rain stopped.*
-   風が止んだ。  
    *The wind died down.*

### 止める（やめる）

Means to quit, discontinue, or give something up. The object is often an
activity, habit, plan, or practice.

-   勉強をやめる。  
    *To stop studying.*
-   タバコをやめる。  
    *To quit smoking.*
-   旅行をやめた。  
    *I decided not to go on the trip.*

### とめる vs やめる

A useful general distinction:

-   **とめる**: stop or halt something, often its movement or operation.
-   **やめる**: quit or discontinue an activity, habit, or plan.

Examples:

-   車を止める（くるまをとめる）: stop a car.
-   仕事を辞める（しごとをやめる）: quit one's job.
-   勉強をやめる（べんきょうをやめる）: stop studying.

For leaving a job or position, **辞める** is especially common. For
discontinuing an activity, **止める** or **やめる** may be used
depending on context and writing style.

### A note on writing やめる

When やめる means "to quit one's job," it is very commonly written
**辞める** rather than 止める. In fact, because 止める / 辞める /
已める all share the same reading and overlapping meanings, modern
Japanese often writes the "quit" sense in hiragana (やめる) to avoid
ambiguity.

Quick guide:

-   Quit a job: **辞める** (or やめる in hiragana)
-   Discontinue an activity: **止める** or やめる
-   Stop a physical movement: **止める** (とめる)

## 6. Common compounds with the On'yomi シ

The On'yomi of 止 is **シ**. It appears in many Sino-Japanese compounds,
especially formal, administrative, and technical vocabulary.

| Word     | Reading      | Meaning                          |
|----------|--------------|----------------------------------|
| 中止     | ちゅし     | cancellation; suspension         |
| 禁止     | きんし       | prohibition; ban                 |
| 阻止     | そし         | prevention; blocking             |
| 止血     | しけつ       | stopping bleeding; haemostasis   |
| 防止     | ぼうし       | prevention                       |
| 廃止     | はいし       | abolition; discontinuation       |
| 停止     | ていし       | suspension; stopping             |
| 休止     | きゅうし     | suspension; pause                |
| 制止     | せいし       | suppression; restraint           |
| 終止符   | しゅうしふ   | full stop; period                |
| 足止め   | あしどめ     | confining someone; foot-stopping |

Examples in context:

-   旅行をやめる。  
    *Stop / give up the trip.*
-   旅行を中止する。  
    *Cancel the trip.*
-   使用を禁止する。  
    *Prohibit use.*
-   サービスを停止する。  
    *Suspend the service.*
-   ～に終止符を打つ。  
    *To put an end to ... (figurative)*

Sino-Japanese compounds often sound more formal or institutional than an
everyday native Japanese verb, though the nuance depends on the
particular word.

## 7. Useful set phrase: 止むを得ない

**止むを得ない（やむをえない）**: unavoidable; cannot be helped.

This phrase appears constantly in formal announcements, business emails,
and public notices.

-   悪天候のため、イベントは中止なる。止むを得ない事情です。  
    *Due to bad weather, the event is cancelled. These are unavoidable circumstances.*
-   止むを得ない理由で欠席する。  
    *To be absent for unavoidable reasons.*

## 8. Similar-sounding words: 止まる, 停まる, 留まる, 泊まる

Several kanji can be read とまる, but their meanings differ.

| Word     | Reading               | Meaning / typical use                                          |
|----------|-----------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------|
| 止まる   | とまる                | to stop; cease (general)                                     |
| 停まる   | とまる                | a vehicle stops at a designated point; emphasizes a scheduled/formal halt |
| 留まる   | とどまる／とまる      | to remain; stay; be retained                                   |
| 泊まる   | とまる                | to stay overnight                                              |

Examples:

-   時計が止まった。  
    *The clock stopped.*
-   バスがバス停に停まった。  
    *The bus stopped at the bus stop.*
-   心に留まる。  
    *To remain in one's heart; to leave an impression.*
-   ホテルに泊まる。  
    *To stay at a hotel overnight.*

Related forms:

-   止める（とめる）: stop something.
-   留める（とどめる）: retain, keep, or confine something; also used in
    expressions such as 心に留める (*keep in mind*).

The choice of kanji can depend on the word's established spelling and
the nuance the writer wants to emphasize.

## 9. 止 as a radical

止 is itself a **radical** (Radical 77). As a component, it often
contributes meaning related to stopping, standing, or feet. Recognizing
this helps when encountering characters such as:

-   **正**（せい / ただしい）: correct; straight
-   **歩**（あるく）: walk
-   **此**（こ / 此れ）: this

### Notes on older signs

You may occasionally see **止步（とほ）** on older or more formal signs,
especially in temples, shrines, historic buildings, or traditional
hotels. This is a Sino-Japanese style meaning "stop (walking)." It is
less common in modern everyday signage but worth recognizing.

Examples:

-   立入止步: do not enter.
-   歩行者止步: pedestrians stop.
-   車両止步: vehicles stop.

## 11. Chinese, Cantonese, Japanese, and Vietnamese comparison

The character 止 is shared across the Sinitic and Sino-Xenic writing
traditions.

| Language            | Reading    | Notes                          |
|---------------------|------------|--------------------------------|
| Mandarin            | zhǐ        | Modern Mandarin pronunciation  |
| Cantonese           | zi2        | Jyutping                       |
| Japanese On'yomi    | シ (shi)   | Sino-Japanese reading          |
| Vietnamese Hán-Việt | chỉ        | Sino-Vietnamese reading        |

A useful comparison is the word for stopping or suspension:

| Language     | Word       | Reading / pronunciation |
|--------------|------------|-------------------------|
| Chinese      | 停止       | tíngzhǐ                 |
| Japanese     | 停止       | ていし (*teishi*)       |
| Vietnamese   | đình chỉ   | đình chỉ                |

The pronunciations have diverged over centuries, but the shared
character and meaning make these words useful memory aids. The Japanese
シ is a historical Sino-Japanese adaptation; it is not pronounced like
modern Mandarin zhǐ.

## 12. What to learn first

For practical reading, prioritize these six vocabulary items:

1.  **止まる（とまる）** --- to stop; come to a halt
2.  **止める（とめる）** --- to stop something
3.  **止む（やむ）** --- to cease; for example, rain stopping
4.  **止める（やめる）** --- to quit; discontinue
5.  **中止（ちゅうし）** --- cancellation; suspension
6.  **禁止（きんし）** --- prohibition; ban

### Quick self-test

Try reading these without looking back:

-   雨が止む。
-   車を止める。
-   会議は中止になった。
-   ここで写真を撮ることは禁止されています。
-   タバコをやめた。
-   止むを得ない事情で、サービスを停止します。

**Answers**

-   あめがやむ。
-   くるまをとめる。
-   かいぎはちゅうしになった。
-   ここでしゃしんをとることは禁止されています（きんしされています）。
-   タバコをやめた。
-   やむをえないじじょうで、サービスをていしします。

## Key takeaway

Do not assume that one kanji has one pronunciation. With **止**, learn
the reading through the complete word:

-   とまる／とめる: stopping something or coming to a stop
-   や／やめる: ceasing or quitting
-   シ: the On'yomi found in compounds such as 中止 and 禁止

Learning vocabulary in context is much more reliable than trying to
assign one fixed sound to each kanji.
