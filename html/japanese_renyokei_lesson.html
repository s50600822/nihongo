Japanese Grammar Lesson: Understanding the Ren'yōkei (連用形)

The 連用形 (れんようけい - ren'yōkei) is the continuative form or conjunctive form in Japanese grammar. Its name literally translates to "the form that connects to words of utility" (verbs and adjectives). It is one of the most versatile building blocks in both Modern and Classical Japanese.

1. The Foundation: 天高く馬肥ゆる秋

In the famous idiom breakdown, we see exactly how the ren'yōkei operates as a sentence-connecting link:

天高く、馬肥ゆる秋

Ten takaku, uma koyuru aki

Literal Meaning: "Autumn is the season when the sky is high and blue, and horses grow plump."

Instead of completing the first thought with 高い (takai), it is changed into its ren'yōkei form, 高く (takaku). This acts as a natural comma ("and"), allowing the sentence to flow seamlessly into the next clause without fully stopping.

2. Formations Across Word Types

Here is how the ren'yōkei is formed across adjectives and verbs in Modern Japanese:

Word Type Dictionary Form Ren'yōkei (連用形) Example Construction
I-Adjectives 高い (takai / high) 高く (takaku) 高く飛ぶ (Fly high)
Na-Adjectives 静かだ (shizuka da / quiet) 静かに (shizuka ni) 静かに歩く (Walk quietly)
Group 1 Verbs (U) 行く (iku / to go) 行き (iki) 行きます (Go - polite) / 行きながら (While going)
Group 2 Verbs (Ru) 食べる (taberu / to eat) 食べ (tabe) 食べたい (Want to eat) / 食べすぎる (Overeat)

3. Practical Examples of the Ren'yōkei

A. Elegant Clause Linking (The "And" Function)

While casual conversations rely heavily on the て-form (te-form) to chain sentences, written text, essays, and news broadcasts use the pure ren'yōkei to sound professional and literary.

  • Modern / Formal Written Style (Ren'yōkei):
    彼は部屋に入り、本を読んだ。 (Kare wa heya ni iri, hon o yonda.)
    "He entered the room, and read a book."
  • Casual Spoken Style (Te-form equivalent):
    彼は部屋に入って、本を読んだ。 (Kare wa heya ni haitte, hon o yonda.)

B. Compound Words (Verb + Verb)

You can combine the ren'yōkei of one verb with another verb to build more specific actions.

  • 書く (kaku / to write) → 書き (ren'yōkei) + 直す (naosu / to fix)
    → 書き直す (kakinaosu / to rewrite)
  • 泳ぐ (oyogu / to swim) → 泳ぎ (ren'yōkei) + 切る (kiru / to finish)
    → 泳ぎ切る (oyogikiru / to swim all the way)

C. Noun Conversion (Verbs Turning into Nouns)

Dropping the dictionary endings to form the ren'yōkei frequently creates standalone nouns that you likely already use.

  • 話す (hanasu / to speak) → 話 (hanashi / a talk, a story)
  • 動く (ugoku / to move) → 動き (ugoki / movement)
  • 休み (yasumu / to rest) → 休み (yasumi / a break, a holiday)
