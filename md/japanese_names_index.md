# Index of Common Japanese Names

A reference guide to common Japanese full names (surname + given name), with romaji, meanings, and cultural notes. Japanese names are written **surname first, given name second** (e.g., 田中太郎 = Tanaka Tarō, where Tanaka is the surname).

---

## Part 1: Most Common Surnames

These surnames account for a large share of Japan's population and appear constantly in daily life, fiction, and business.

| Kanji | Romaji | Meaning | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 佐藤 | Satō | "help/assist" + "wisteria" | The single most common surname in Japan. Extremely common in fiction as a generic/everyman name. |
| 鈴木 | Suzuki | "bell" + "tree" | 2nd most common surname. Also a major car brand (Suzuki Motor Corp.). |
| 高橋 | Takahashi | "high/tall" + "bridge" | Top 3 surname; very common nationwide. |
| 田中 | Tanaka | "rice field" + "inside/middle" | Extremely common; often used as a default "average person" name, similar to "John Smith." |
| 渡辺 | Watanabe | "cross/ford" + "area/vicinity" | Common surname; several variant kanji spellings exist. |
| 伊藤 | Itō | (place name) + "wisteria" | Common in central/eastern Japan. Also a major trading company name (Itochu). |
| 山本 | Yamamoto | "mountain" + "origin/base" | Common nationwide; note: Isoroku Yamamoto was the WWII admiral. |
| 中村 | Nakamura | "middle" + "village" | Very common; straightforward descriptive meaning ("middle of the village"). |
| 小林 | Kobayashi | "small" + "forest/woods" | Common; poetic, nature-based meaning. |
| 加藤 | Katō | "add/increase" + "wisteria" | Common; part of a large family of "-tō" (藤, wisteria) surnames tracing back to the Fujiwara clan. |

**Cultural note:** Many common surnames end in **藤 (tō/fuji, "wisteria")** — Satō, Itō, Katō, Andō, Kudō, etc. This traces back to the powerful **Fujiwara clan** of the Heian period; many families adopted a modified version of "Fujiwara" as their own surname over centuries.

## Part 2: Common Male Given Names

| Kanji | Romaji | Meaning | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 太郎 | Tarō | "thick/big" + "son" (eldest son) | Classic, almost archetypal male name — like "John Doe" for a Japanese man. Traditionally given to firstborn sons. |
| 一郎 | Ichirō | "one" + "son" | "First son"; part of a numbered naming set (Ichirō, Jirō, Saburō...) |
| 二郎/次郎 | Jirō | "two/next" + "son" | Traditionally the **second** son's name. |
| 健太 | Kenta | "healthy/strong" + "big/great" | Popular modern name conveying strength and vitality. |
| 大輔 | Daisuke | "big" + "help" | Common; conveys a "great helper" image. |
| 翔太 | Shōta | "soar/fly" + "big" | Popular in recent decades; evokes flight/ambition. |
| 悠斗 | Yūto | "leisurely/calm" + "the Big Dipper/star" | Very trendy modern name (2000s–2020s); combines calmness with a celestial image. |
| 蓮 | Ren | "lotus" | Extremely popular single-kanji name in recent years; strong Buddhist/nature imagery (the lotus symbolizes purity rising from mud). |
| 陸 | Riku | "land" | Popular modern name; simple, strong, nature-based. |
| 拓也 | Takuya | "pioneer/open up" + "also" | Common; conveys ambition and forging one's own path. |

**Cultural note:** Traditional naming often used **numbered suffixes** (一郎, 二郎, 三郎…) to indicate **birth order among sons**. This practice has declined but the names (especially Tarō and Jirō) remain iconic.

## Part 3: Common Female Given Names

| Kanji | Romaji | Meaning | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 花子 | Hanako | "flower" + "child" | The classic, archetypal female name — Japan's equivalent of "Jane Doe." Common in old textbooks/examples. |
| 明美 | Akemi | "bright" + "beautiful" | Common name from the mid-20th century; still widely recognized. |
| 由美 | Yumi | "reason/cause" + "beautiful" | Popular mid-late 20th century name. |
| 恵子 | Keiko | "blessing/grace" + "child" | Very common name for women born mid-20th century (Shōwa era). |
| 咲 | Saki | "to bloom" | Popular modern name; evokes blossoming flowers. |
| 陽菜 | Hina | "sun/sunshine" + "greens/vegetable (poetic: 'green shoot')" | One of the most popular girls' names in the 2010s–2020s. |
| 结衣/結衣 | Yui | "tie/bind" + "clothing" | Very trendy modern name; also the name of actress **Yui Aragaki**, boosting popularity. |
| 美咲 | Misaki | "beautiful" + "bloom" | Popular; poetic floral imagery. |
| 葵 | Aoi | "hollyhock (flower)" | Popular unisex-leaning name, also historically tied to the Tokugawa shogunate's family crest (the aoi/hollyhock leaf). |
| さくら | Sakura | "cherry blossom" (often written in hiragana rather than kanji) | Iconic name evoking Japan's national flower; extremely common in anime/media (e.g., *Naruto*'s Sakura Haruno, *Cardcaptor Sakura*). |

**Cultural note:** Many female names traditionally ended in **子 (ko, "child")** — Hanako, Keiko, Akiko, Yoko, etc. This suffix was extremely dominant for women born before the 1990s but has become less common among younger generations in favor of more varied, often nature-inspired names.

## Quick Reference: Naming Conventions to Know

- **Order:** Surname (myōji/sei) comes first, given name (namae) second — opposite of Western convention. When writing in Romaji for international contexts, names are sometimes flipped to "given name surname" for clarity (e.g., Tarō Tanaka).
- **Placeholder names:** Just as English uses "John Doe" / "Jane Doe," Japanese uses **田中太郎 (Tanaka Tarō)** for men and **山田花子 (Yamada Hanako)** for women in forms, textbooks, and examples.
- **Gendered kanji patterns:** Male names often use kanji like 太 (thick/big), 郎 (son), 大 (big), 翔 (soar); female names often use 子 (child, older generation), 美 (beautiful), 花/菜/咲 (flower/bloom-related, nature imagery).
- **Reading flexibility:** The same kanji can often be read multiple ways depending on the name (e.g., 陽菜 can be read Hina, Haruna, or Yōna) — parents sometimes choose non-standard readings, making some names genuinely ambiguous even to native speakers.
