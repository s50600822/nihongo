01 Verse 1

嗚呼いつもの様に 過ぎる日々にあくびが出る
Aa, itsumo no you ni, sugiru hibi ni akubi ga deru.
"Ah, just like always, a yawn escapes into the passing days."

Grammar: 嗚呼 (aa) is a literary interjection expressing emotion, here a sigh of weary recognition. いつもの様に combines the adverb いつも (always) with the attributive particle の and the comparative 様に (like; as): "in the usual way; as always." 過ぎる modifies 日々: "the days that pass / passing days." The intransitive verb 出る describes the yawn escaping involuntarily, suggesting boredom so deep it manifests physically without conscious effort. The に after 日々 is interesting: the yawn is directed "into" the passing days, as if the days themselves are so monotonous they draw out the yawn. The line establishes the starting point: a life of comfortable numbness.

The continuative form 越え connects the clause to the main sentence: "crossing beyond [the night], [then] ~." 降る normally means "to fall" for precipitation (rain, snow), but here it is used metaphorically for dawn "falling" on the city, as if morning were a physical substance settling over Shibuya. The も after 今日 emphasizes repetition: "today too; yet again today." The specificity of Shibuya grounds the song in a real place, the urban landscape where creative young people live and struggle.
どこか虚しいような そんな気持ち つまらないな

"Somehow empty, that kind of feeling — it's boring, isn't it."

Grammar: どこか is the indefinite form of どこ (where), used adverbially to mean "somewhat; somehow; rather." ような after an adjective softens the statement into a suggestion: "somewhat empty, as it were." The phrase どこか虚しいような modifies 気持ち through apposition: "that kind of feeling, [which is] somehow empty." つまらないな ends with the final particle な, which here adds a tone of resigned self-commentary rather than seeking agreement. The speaker is not asking anyone; they are stating a flat observation about their own emotional state. The progression from 虚しい (empty) to つまらない (boring) is a downward spiral: the realization of emptiness leads to the judgment that life is dull.

The repetition of "ii" three times in slightly different forms (それでいい / そんなもんさ / これでいい) creates a mantra of resignation. The grammar is deliberately simple, even childlike, reflecting the speaker's attempt to convince themselves that mediocrity is acceptable. The contrast between this complacency and what follows makes the later transformation more powerful.

Grammar: 知らず知らず is a reduplicated negative adverbial meaning "unwittingly; without realizing; unconsciously." The reduplication intensifies the sense of something happening beneath awareness. 隠した is the colloquial contracted form of 隠していた (had been hiding), using the past progressive ていた. 響かせて is the causative form of 響く (to resound; to echo): "to make [something] resound; to let [something] ring out." The imperative force comes from the te-form plus the emphatic particles よ and ほら. The line is a self-command: the speaker is urging their own hidden voice to emerge. The shift from third-person description to second-person self-address marks the first crack in the resignation of the verse.
見ないフリしていても 確かにそこにある
Minai furishite ite mo, tashika ni soko ni aru.
"Even if I pretend not to see, it's certainly there."
JapaneseRomajiPart of SpeechGloss

フリfuriNounpretense; act — from 振り furi

Grammar: [Verb in negative] + フリして is a set phrase meaning "to pretend (not) to [verb]." 見ないフリして = "pretending not to see." The te-form して connects to いて (the te-form of いる, progressive auxiliary), creating していて: "being in the state of pretending." していても = "even while being in the state of ~ing." The concessive も acknowledges that the pretense continues, but the second clause (確かにそこにある) overrides it with a statement of fact. The true voice exists independently of the speaker's denial. The grammar of pretense (フリ) versus the grammar of certainty (確かに) creates a tension between self-deception and self-knowledge.

The line is the song's first declaration of artistic authenticity: draw not what you think you should, but what you actually feel, using the color that is genuinely yours. The word 色 (color) is both literal and metaphorical, referring to artistic style, emotional truth, and personal identity.

The "blue world" is the song's central image: a realm of authenticity and feeling that appears when one stops pretending and starts creating from the heart. 青い carries connotations of youth, freshness, and possibility in Japanese, beyond just the color blue.

嗚呼 手を伸ばせば伸ばすほどに 遠くへゆく
Aa, te wo nobaseba nobasu hodo ni, tooku e yuku.
"Ah, the more I stretch out my hand, the farther it goes."

Grammar: [Verb in conditional] + [same verb in dictionary form] + ほどに is a proportional construction meaning "the more [verb], the more ~." 伸ばせば伸ばすほどに = "the more I stretch [my hand], the more ~." This is one of Japanese's most elegant proportional patterns, creating a sense of endless, perhaps futile, extension. ゆく is the literary form of 行く (to go), elevating the diction. The line captures the paradox of ambition: the more you reach, the more distant the goal seems. This is not presented as despair but as the natural condition of growth. The hand reaching toward something far away is a visual metaphor for creative striving, where mastery recedes as skill advances.
思うようにいかない今日も また慌ただしくもがいてる

Grammar: 思うようにいかない is a common expression: "things don't go as [I] think/wish." ように after a verb creates a comparative standard: "in the way that [I] think." The negative いかない applies to this standard: "doesn't go according to [my] wishes." もがく is a vivid verb meaning "to struggle; to writhe; to flounder," often used for physical struggling but here applied to emotional/creative effort. The progressive もがいている (colloquially もがいてる) emphasizes ongoing action: "am in the state of struggling." 慌ただしく (hurriedly; busily) adds the sense of frantic, ungraceful effort. The line rejects the romanticized image of the artist for something more honest: most days are frustrating, messy, and full of failure. The も (too; again) after 今日 and また (again) reinforce the cyclical nature of this struggle.

The line is an honest admission that creative or personal growth is not just mentally hard but physically taxing. The compound verb 踏み込む (to step into; to delve into) suggests intentional, active engagement with difficulty rather than passive suffering.

08 Key Grammar Patterns Summary

〜たままに / 〜たまま
"Just as [verb]ed; in the state of [verb]ing." Preserves raw, unfiltered feeling. Used for authentic creation and movement.
〜ば〜ほどに
"The more [verb], the more ~." Proportional construction expressing endless extension and the paradox of growth.
〜てきた
"Have been [verb]ing up to now." Te-form + くる. Expresses accumulated history leading to the present moment.
〜しか〜ない
"Only; nothing but." Restrictive pattern creating absolute uniqueness: "only I can do."
〜たって / 〜ても
Concessive: "even if." Casual たって form used for dismissal of comparison and external judgment.
〜ずに / 知らず知らず
"Without ~ing." Negative adverbial. Reduplicated form means "unwittingly; unconsciously."
〜だけじゃない / 〜だけだ
"Not only ~ / is only ~." Restriction and its negation. Used to expand and contract the meaning of creative commitment.
〜気がする / 〜気がした
"To feel like; to have the sensation that." Softens certainty to perception, expressing tentative self-discovery.
