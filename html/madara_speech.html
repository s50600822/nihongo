Naruto · Chapter 602 / Shippuden 344

Madara's Speech to Obito

A full grammatical and lexical breakdown of Uchiha Madara's "Wake up to reality" monologue.

現実を見ろ。 この世は思い通りにいかぬことばかりだ。 長く生きれば生きるほど… 現実は苦しみと痛みと虚しさだけが漂っていることに気付く…。 いいか…。 この世の全てにおいて、 光があたところには必ず影がある。 勝者という概念がある以上、 敗者は同じくして存在する。 平和を保ちたいという利己的な意志が戦争を起こし、 愛を守るために憎しみが生まれる。 これらは因果関係にあり切り離すことができん。 本来はな…。 ……この世の因果を断ち切る。 勝者だけの世界。 平和だけの世界。 愛だけの世界。 それらだけの世界を造る。 オレは……うちはの亡霊。 うちはマダラだ。 この現実こそ……地獄だ。

"Wake up to reality. Nothing ever goes as planned in this world. The longer you live, the more you realize that this reality is only filled with pain, suffering, and futility. Listen… In this world, wherever there is light, there will always be shadows. As long as there is a concept of victors, the vanquished will also exist. The selfish intent of wanting to preserve peace initiates wars, and hatred is born in order to protect love. These are bound by causal relationships and cannot be separated. That is how it originally is… I will sever the causal bonds of this world. A world of only victors. A world of only peace. A world of only love. I will create such a world. I am… the ghost of the Uchiha. Madara Uchiha. This reality… is truly hell."

01 Opening Command

現実を見ろ。
Genjitsu o miro.
"Wake up to reality." / "Look at reality."
JapaneseRomajiPart of SpeechGloss
現実genjitsuNounreality; actuality
をoObject particlemarks genjitsu as the direct object
見ろmiroVerb (imperative)"look!" / "see!" — imperative of 見る miru
Grammar: A blunt imperative. 見ろ is the plain imperative of 見る (Group 2 verb). In modern polite speech this would be 見てください; Madara uses the raw command form to assert dominance and urgency. The object particle を marks what is to be looked at.
この世は思い通りにいかぬことばかりだ。
Kono yo wa omoidoori ni ikanu koto bakari da.
"Nothing ever goes as planned in this world." (lit. "In this world, it is nothing but things that do not go according to one's wishes.")
JapaneseRomajiPart of SpeechGloss
このkonoDeterminerthis
世yoNounworld; society

Grammar: いかぬ is the classical negative form of 行く (modern: 行かない). 〜ばかりだ means "nothing but ~". The structure [clause] + ことばかりだ turns the clause into a noun phrase and asserts it exhaustively: "It is exclusively things that do not go as wished."
長く生きれば生きるほど…

"The longer you live…" (lit. "The more one lives…")
JapaneseRomajiPart of SpeechGloss

Grammar: 〜ば…ほど is a correlative construction meaning "the more X, the more Y". Here the second clause is omitted and implied: "The longer one lives, the more [one realizes]…". The trailing ellipsis (…) creates dramatic pause.
現実は苦しみと痛みと虚しさだけが漂っていることに気付く…。
Genjitsu wa kurushimi to itami to munashisa dake ga tadayotte iru koto ni kizuku…
"…the more you realize that this reality is only filled with suffering, pain, and futility."
JapaneseRomajiPart of SpeechGloss

Grammar: The core structure is [clause] + ことに気付く — "to realize that [clause]". Inside the clause, だけが marks "only suffering, pain, and emptiness" as the subject. 漂っている (drifting/hovering) is used metaphorically: these negative things drift through reality like a miasma. Some anime subtitles render 虚しさ as "futility"; manga scanlations sometimes use "sadness" (悲しさ), but 虚しさ is the canonical manga reading.

02 The Duality of the World

いいか…。
Ii ka…
"Listen…" / "You hear me?"
JapaneseRomajiPart of SpeechGloss
いいiiAdjectivegood; okay — used here as an interjection
かkaFinal particlerough, masculine tag question — "huh?" / "you got that?"
Grammar: いいか is a rough, commanding interjection used to secure the listener's attention before an important statement. The か here is not a genuine question but a rhetorical device demanding acknowledgment.
この世の全てにおいて、
Kono yo no subete ni oite,

Grammar: において is a formal, written-style locative particle. It elevates the register from conversational について or の中で to something more philosophical and absolute, fitting Madara's tone.
光があたるところには必ず影がある。

"Wherever there is light, there will always be shadows."

Grammar: [relative clause] + ところに(は) creates a conditional locative: "In places where [clause], …". 必ず (always) reinforces the inevitability. This line establishes the central metaphor: light and shadow are inseparable, a direct nod to the Uchiha clan's fan symbol.
勝者という概念がある以上、

"As long as there is a concept of victors,"

Grammar: 〜という概念 ("the concept called ~") elevates 勝者 from a simple noun to an abstract category. 以上 here is a conditional conjunction: "Given that X exists, Y must follow." It expresses logical necessity, not temporal sequence.
敗者は同じくして存在する。

Grammar: 同じくして is a formal, somewhat archaic adverbial meaning "in the same way". It mirrors the logical structure: victors imply losers by necessity. 存在する is the Sino-Japanese (漢語) counterpart to ある, adding gravitas.
平和を保ちたいという利己的な意志が戦争を起こし、

Grammar: 保ちたい is the desiderative (want-to) form. The entire phrase 平和を保ちたいという利己的な意志 is a heavy subject noun phrase: "the selfish will [of wanting to preserve peace]". 起こし is the continuative (masu-stem) form, used to chain this clause to the next line — the comma in Japanese represents this soft linkage.
愛を守るために憎しみが生まれる。

"and hatred is born in order to protect love."

Grammar: [Verb in attributive form] + ために expresses purpose: "for the purpose of protecting love". 生まれる is the intransitive/passive form of 生む (to give birth to), meaning "comes into being / is born". The line forms a paradox: love, the most positive emotion, structurally necessitates hatred as its defensive shadow.
これらは因果関係にあり切り離すことができん。

"These exist in a causal relationship; they cannot be separated."

The word 因果 (inga) is the Buddhist term for karma/causation, giving the speech theological weight.

Grammar: An extremely compressed line. 本来はな implies "[That is how things are] by nature, [you see]." The copula だ is omitted, and な (a rough masculine emphatic particle) closes the sentence with a tone of finality. The ellipsis trails into the next conceptual section.

03 Madara's Declaration

……この世の因果を断ち切る。
……Kono yo no inga o tachikiru.
"…I will sever the causal bonds of this world."
JapaneseRomajiPart of SpeechGloss
このkonoDeterminerthis
世yoNounworld
のnoGenitive particleof
因果ingaNouncausality; karma; cause and effect
をoObject particlemarks the object
断ち切るtachikiruVerbto sever; to cut off; to break off — emphatic form of 切る
Grammar: 断ち切る combines 断つ (to cut off) with 切る (to cut) into an emphatic compound verb, intensifying the finality of the action. The subject (I) is omitted, which is common in Japanese when the speaker is clearly referring to themselves in a declaration.
勝者だけの世界。

Grammar: A nominal phrase with no verb — typical of proclamations. だけの creates an exclusive modifier: "a world consisting only of victors." The copula だ is omitted for punchiness.
平和だけの世界。
Heiwa dake no sekai.
"A world of only peace."

だけdakeLimiting particleonly
のnoGenitive particleof

Grammar: Parallel structure with the previous line. The anaphoric repetition of 〜だけの世界 creates a rhythmic, chant-like cadence — a rhetorical device called anaphora.
愛だけの世界。
Ai dake no sekai.
"A world of only love."
JapaneseRomajiPart of SpeechGloss
愛aiNounlove
だけdakeLimiting particleonly
のnoGenitive particleof
世界sekaiNounworld
Grammar: The third repetition completes the tricolon. In rhetoric, three parallel phrases create a sense of completeness and inevitability. Each phrase strips away the shadow-side of a duality: victors without losers, peace without war, love without hatred.
それらだけの世界を造る。
Sorera dake no sekai o tsukuru.
"I will create such a world." (lit. "I will create a world of only those things.")
JapaneseRomajiPart of SpeechGloss
それらsoreraPronounthose; those things
だけdakeLimiting particleonly
のnoGenitive particleof
世界sekaiNounworld
をoObject particlemarks the object
造るtsukuruVerbto create; to build; to construct — written with 造 (archaic/formal) rather than 作
Grammar: それら refers back to the three preceding ideals. 造る (using the kanji 造) carries connotations of divine or grand creation, as opposed to the more mundane 作る. The subject "I" is again omitted, but the declarative force is carried by the plain-form verb ending.

04 Identity & Final Judgment

オレは……うちはの亡霊。
Ore wa…… Uchiha no bourei.
"I am… the ghost of the Uchiha."
JapaneseRomajiPart of SpeechGloss
オレorePronounI — rough, masculine first-person pronoun
はwaTopic particleas for me
うちはuchihaProper nounUchiha (clan name)
のnoGenitive particleof; 's
亡霊boureiNounghost; specter; departed spirit
Grammar: The copula だ is omitted after 亡霊, creating a dramatic pause filled by the ellipsis. オレ is the roughest common first-person pronoun, asserting raw ego. Calling himself a 亡霊 (ghost) rather than a 忍者 or 忍 emphasizes that Madara sees himself as something that should be dead yet persists — a revenant outside the normal order.
うちはマダラだ。
Uchiha Madara da.
"I am Madara Uchiha."
JapaneseRomajiPart of SpeechGloss
うちはマダラuchiha madaraProper nounMadara Uchiha (name)
だdaCopulais; am — plain, assertive
Grammar: The simplest possible sentence structure — [Name] + だ — yet devastating in context. After the philosophical buildup, this blunt self-naming lands with the force of a verdict. Japanese names are given family-first: Uchiha (clan) + Madara (given name).
この現実こそ……地獄だ。
Kono genjitsu koso…… jigoku da.
"This reality… is truly hell."
JapaneseRomajiPart of SpeechGloss
このkonoDeterminerthis
現実genjitsuNounreality
こそkosoEmphatic particleprecisely; indeed; none other than — strongly emphatic
地獄jigokuNounhell
だdaCopulais
Grammar: こそ is one of the strongest emphatic particles in Japanese. It replaces the topic particle は and singles out the noun as the one true thing: "Reality — and nothing else — is hell." This line bookends the speech, returning to the opening word 現実 and closing the philosophical arc. The ellipsis after こそ creates a dramatic beat before the final noun drops like a hammer.

05 Key Grammar Patterns Summary

〜ば…ほど
"The more X, the more Y" — correlative proportional construction.
〜ことに気付く
"To realize that [clause]" — nominalized clause as the object of realization.
〜という概念 / 〜という意志
"The concept of ~ / the will [of/desiring] ~" — という elevates nouns to abstract categories.
〜ために
"In order to ~ / for the sake of ~" — purpose construction. Verb must be in attributive (plain) form.
〜以上
"As long as / given that" — conditional; expresses logical necessity, not time.
〜こそ
"Precisely / none other than" — strong emphasis; replaces は and intensifies the noun.
