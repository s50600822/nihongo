01 Watermelon Harvest — Vine Signals and the Picking Decision

スイカのツルが緑色やったになんか見るみるうちに黄色になってきて枯れてきちゃったんで、今日はスイカ収穫したいと思います。これとかめっちゃ大きい。なんかまだ早い気がするんだよね。本当はもうちょっと置いときたかったんだけど、このまま置いてても多分もう傷んでいきそうなんで、もう今日は収穫します。
Suika no tsuru ga midori-iro yatta no ni nan ka miru miru uchi ni kiiro ni natte kite karete kichatta n de, kyou wa suika shuukaku shitai to omoimasu. Kore to ka meccha ookii. Nan ka mada hayai ki ga suru n da yo ne. Hontou wa mou chotto oito kitakatta n dakedo, kono mama oitete mo tabun mou kizunde iki-sou na n de, mou kyou wa shuukaku shimasu.
"Even though the watermelon vine was green, somehow in the blink of an eye it turned yellow and started withering, so today [I] think [I'd] like to harvest the watermelons. This one [for example] is super big. [I] somehow feel [it's] still early, [you know]. [I] really wanted to leave [them] a bit longer, but even if [I] leave [them] like this, [they] will probably already start spoiling, so [I'll] harvest [them] today."

Grammar: スイカのツルが緑色やったのに uses the subject スイカのツルが (watermelon vine [subject]) + noun phrase 緑色やった (was green, Kansai past copula やった from だった) + concessive particle のに (even though; despite): "Even though the watermelon vine was green..." The Kansai past copula やった replaces standard だった, and the のに sets up a contrast between the previous healthy green state and the current yellowing condition. なんか見るみるうちに黄色になってきて枯れてきちゃったんで uses the colloquial filler なんか (somehow; kinda) + set phrase 見るみるうちに (miru miru uchi ni, in the blink of an eye; before [one's] eyes) + noun 黄色 (yellow) + directional progressive になってきて (is becoming [toward me]) + te-form 枯れて (withering) + directional regret auxiliary きちゃった (came to [regrettably], from きてしまった) + causal contraction んで (from ので): "...somehow in the blink of an eye it turned yellow and started withering, so..." The compound directional progressive になってきて marks the color change as a process unfolding toward the observer, a common Japanese narrative perspective for observing change. The てしまう auxiliary contracted to ちゃった adds a sense of regrettable, inevitable outcome, appropriate for the disappointment of premature vine death. 今日はスイカ収穫したいと思います uses the temporal topic 今日は (today [topic]) + object スイカ (watermelon) + desiderative 収穫したい (want to harvest) + quotative と + polite 思います (think): "Today [I] think [I'd] like to harvest the watermelons." The desiderative たい attached to the verb stem expresses the vlogger's desire, and the quotative と思います frames it as a planned intention. これとかめっちゃ大きい uses the demonstrative これ (this one) + listing particle とか (and such; for example) + slang adverb めっちゃ (super; extremely) + adjective 大きい (big): "This one [for example] is super big." The とか marks the watermelon as an example from a set, and めっちゃ intensifies the size observation. なんかまだ早い気がするんだよね uses the filler なんか (somehow) + adverb まだ (still) + adjective 早い (early; premature) + set phrase 気がする (to feel like; to have a feeling that) + explanatory nominalizer んだ + assertive particle よ + agreement-seeking ね: "[I] somehow feel [it's] still early, [you know], [right]?" The 気がする pattern is the canonical Japanese expression for intuitive feeling or gut sense, and the んだよね combination frames the intuition as both explanatory (んだ) and seeking confirmation (よね). 本当はもうちょっと置いときたかったんだけど uses the adverb 本当は (really; actually; truthfully) + adverb もうちょっと (a bit more) + colloquial contraction 置いときたかった (wanted to leave [aside], from 置いておきたかった, desiderative past of 置いておく) + explanatory nominalizer ん + concessive だけ: "[I] really wanted to leave [them] a bit longer, but..." The ておく auxiliary (contracted to とく in Kansai) marks the leaving as a preparatory or sustained action, and its desiderative past 置いときたかった expresses the vlogger's preference that was overridden by circumstances. このまま置いてても多分もう傷んでいきそうなんで uses the demonstrative phrase このまま (like this; as is) + te-form conditional 置いてても (even if leaving [them]) + adverb 多分 (probably) + adverb もう (already) + verb 傷んで (spoiling; rotting) + directional conjectural いきそう (seems [it will] go [that way]) + causal なんで (from なので): "...even if [I] leave [them] like this, [they] will probably already start spoiling, so..." The directional conjectural いきそう combines the progressive auxiliary いく (go; proceed) with the conjectural そう, creating a prediction about the future trajectory of the watermelons. 傷む (kizumu, to spoil; to rot; to go bad) is the standard Japanese verb for food spoilage, and its use here reflects practical agricultural knowledge about watermelon shelf life on the vine. もう今日は収穫します uses the adverb もう (already; now) + temporal topic 今日は (today [topic]) + polite verb 収穫します (will harvest): "[I'll] harvest [them] today." The もう marks the decision as finalized after the preceding deliberation.

Grammar: 大きいの取れた uses the nominalized adjective 大きいの (a big one) + past potential 取れた (was able to get/was harvested): "[I] got a big one." The potential 取れる marks the harvest as a successful acquisition, and the nominalization turns the adjective into a noun phrase that can serve as the subject. これでかい uses the demonstrative これ (this) + colloquial i-adjective でかい (huge; big): "This [is] huge." でかい is the colloquial variant of でかい/大きい, common in casual speech and Kansai-influenced narration. 1番でかいかな? uses the superlative 1番 (ichiban, number one; most) + adjective でかい (big) + speculation particle かな: "[Is it] the biggest, [I] wonder?" The superlative 1番 is the standard Japanese way to form "-est" comparisons, and the self-directed question かな frames the assessment as tentative. スイカ追加1個目 uses the noun スイカ (watermelon) + noun 追加 (tsuika, addition; extra) + number + counter + ordinal suffix 1個目 (ikko-me, first one): "Watermelon, additional first one." The 目 suffix marks ordinal enumeration, and 追加 specifies that this is an additional item beyond what was already counted. これ絶対白い気する uses the demonstrative これ (this) + adverb 絶対 (zettai, absolutely; definitely) + i-adjective 白い (white; pale; unripe [inside]) + set phrase 気がする (to feel like; to have a feeling that): "[I] definitely feel this [is] white [inside]." The adjective 白い when describing watermelon flesh means unripe or pale, and the 絶対 intensifies the intuition. The 気がする pattern is the canonical Japanese expression for gut feeling or intuitive assessment. でもしゃあない uses the contrastive conjunction でも (but; however) + Kansai adjective しゃあない (shaa nai, can't be helped; it is what it is, from 仕方ない): "But [it] can't be helped." The Kansai contraction しゃあない is ubiquitous in casual Japanese and expresses resigned acceptance of circumstances beyond one's control. かんなかったらジョンにちょっとあげようよ uses the conditional かんなかったら (if [it] didn't ripen, from 完熟しなかったら or 熟さなかったら) + recipient に + adverb ちょっと (a bit) + volitional あげよう (shall give) + assertive particle よ: "If [it] didn't ripen, [I'll] give [it] to John a bit, yo." The conditional negative なかったら frames the unripe watermelon as a contingency plan for goat feeding, and the volitional あげよう marks the decision as a planned action. The かん stem is likely from 完熟 (kanshuku, full ripeness) or 熟す (kanjiru/kaneru), and its use here reflects agricultural knowledge about watermelon ripeness indicators. これで3つ uses the demonstrative/instrumental これで (with this; by this) + number + counter 3つ (mittsu, three [items]): "With this [it's] three." The これで pattern is a standard Japanese counting frame for aggregating items. ちょっとめっちゃちっちゃいやつ uses the adverb ちょっと (a bit) + slang adverb めっちゃ (super; extremely) + colloquial adjective ちっちゃい (tiny; small, from 小さい) + colloquial noun やつ (one; thing): "[There's] a super tiny one." The diminutive ちっちゃい is the colloquial/affectionate form of 小さい, and やつ is the casual demonstrative for things. ほら is the attention-directing interjection: "Look." これはちょっとジョンのおやつにしよう uses the topic これは (this [topic]) + adverb ちょっと (a bit) + genitive phrase ジョンのおやつ (John's snack) + target に + volitional しよう (shall make): "[I'll] make this [into] a bit of John's snack." The にする pattern marks a repurposing decision, turning the unripe watermelon into goat food. めっちゃ手のひらサイズのスイカや uses the slang adverb めっちゃ (super) + compound noun 手のひらサイズ (palm size, from 手のひら + サイズ) + genitive の + noun イカ (watermelon) + Kansai copula や: "[It's] a super palm-sized watermelon, ya." The compound noun 手のひらサイズ is a common Japanese size descriptor using the English loanword サイズ. ただこっちのスイカが大きそうやから uses the conjunction ただ (but; however) + demonstrative phrase こっちのスイカ (the watermelon on this side) + subject が + conjectural adjective 大きそう (seems big) + Kansai copula や + causal から (because): "But since the watermelon on this side seems big..." The conjectural そう attached to the adjective stem creates a visual inference from the watermelon's external appearance. これは今日取らんともうちょっと待ってみます uses the topic これは (this one [topic]) + temporal 今日 (today) + Kansai negative 取らん (toran, won't take, from 取らない) + contrastive particle と (if; when) + adverb もうちょっと (a bit more) + te-form auxiliary 待ってみます (will try waiting): "[I] won't take this one today and [I'll] wait a bit more." The Kansai negative 取らん replaces standard 取らない, and the てみる auxiliary (待ってみます) marks the waiting as a trial or experiment. ちっちゃいスイカの中身こんな感じ uses the attributive adjective ちっちゃい (tiny) modifying スイカ (watermelon) + genitive の + noun 中身 (inside; contents) + demonstrative phrase こんな感じ (this kind of feeling = like this): "The inside of the tiny watermelon [is] like this." The noun 中身 (nakami, inside; contents) combines 中 (naka, inside) + 身 (mi, body; flesh), and is the standard Japanese word for the interior of food items. うっすらピンク uses the adverb/onomatopoeia うっすら (ussura, faintly; slightly; dimly) + noun ピンク (pinku, pink): "Faintly pink." The onomatopoeia うっすら describes something barely visible or lightly colored, and its use here laments the underripe flesh. これはちょっとジョンに今からあげます uses the topic これは (this [topic]) + adverb ちょっと (a bit) + recipient に + temporal 今から (from now) + polite verb あげます (will give): "[I'll] give this to John right now." めっちゃ甘くない匂いする uses the slang adverb めっちゃ (super) + negative adjective 甘くない (not sweet) + noun 匂い (smell; aroma) + verb する (does; makes): "[It] smells super not-sweet." The structure [adjective] + 匂いがする is the standard Japanese pattern for describing smells with adjectival qualities. こんなちっちゃいのでもピンクなってる uses the demonstrative phrase こんなちっちゃいの (such a tiny one) + concessive particle でも (even though; even if) + noun ピンク (pink) + progressive なってる (from なっている, has become): "Even [though it's] this tiny, [it's] turned pink." The concessive でも marks the small size as a surprising contrast to the pink color that has developed.

Grammar: じゃあいいもんあるで uses the conjunction じゃあ (well then; so) + adjective いい (good) + colloquial noun もん (thing; stuff) + existential ある (there is) + Kansai sentence-ending particle で: "Well, [there are] good things, de." The Kansai で replaces standard だ/です, and the structure frames the watermelon scraps as desirable items for the goat. ジョン。実はいいのあんねん uses the proper noun ジョン (Jon, the goat) as a direct address + adverb 実は (jitsu wa, actually; in fact) + adjective いい (good) + nominalizer の + Kansai existential あんねん (an-nen, there is/are, from あるのだ/あるのです): "John. Actually, [there are] good ones, an-nen." The Kansai あんねん combines ある + の + やん/ねん, a common Osaka/Kansai emphatic existential that asserts the existence of something noteworthy. ほら is the attention-directing interjection "look." ちょっとこっちであげようか uses the adverb ちょっと (a bit) + demonstrative こっち (this way; here) + locative で + volitional あげよう (shall give) + question か: "Shall [I] give [them] over here a bit?" The volitional + か creates a self-directed deliberation about where to feed the goat. ちょっと待って uses the te-form imperative of 待つ: "Wait a moment." はい is the affirmative "yes" or "here you go," likely marking the moment of giving the watermelon to John. おしまい is the set phrase for "[that's] the end; all done; finished," commonly used in Japanese when something is used up or completed. あと1個おしまい uses あと (remaining; one more) + number + counter 1個 (one item) + おしまい: "One more, [that's] the end." 美味しかった is the plain past of 美味しい: "[It was] delicious." 皮舐めたんやけど uses the noun 皮 (kawa, rind; peel; skin) + past verb 舐めた (licked) + nominalizer ん + Kansai copula や + concessive けど: "[I] licked the rind, [you know], but..." The Kansai や + けど combination frames the rind-licking as a slightly embarrassing admission softened by the concessive. スイカおしまい uses the noun スイカ + おしまい: "Watermelon [is] finished." ま、食べすぎない uses the interjection ま (well; I mean, from まあ) + negative 食べすぎない (won't overeat): "Well, [I] won't eat too much." The negative of the excessive verb 食べすぎ (to overeat) marks self-restraint after tasting the watermelon. 痛い痛い is the pain adjective repeated: "Ow ow." よいしょ is the effort interjection. 全然ない uses the adverb 全然 (zenzen, not at all) with an elided negative verb (likely 取れない or いない): "Not at all." 取れそう uses the conjectural そう attached to the potential verb 取れる (can get; can harvest): "[It] seems [I] can get [some]." あかんかった uses the Kansai negative adjective あかん (akan, no good; failed) + past かった: "[It] was no good." The Kansai あかん replaces standard だめ/いけない, and its past form marks a failed attempt. 逃げられちゃった uses the passive past 逃げられちゃった (from 逃げられてしまった, escaped [regrettably]): "[They] escaped." The passive + てしまう auxiliary creates a sense of victimhood or regret at the escape. あ、でかかったな uses the exclamation あ (ah) + past colloquial adjective でかかった (was huge) + exclamatory な: "Ah, [it] was big, na." The past form of でかい marks the escaped creature as having been impressively large. 皮抜く uses the noun 皮 (rind) + verb 抜く (nuku, to peel; to remove): "Peel the rind." ちょっと切っちゃう uses the adverb ちょっと (a bit) + regret auxiliary 切っちゃう (from 切ってしまう, cut [regrettably]): "[I] cut [it] a bit [regrettably]." めっちゃ大きいなったのに取れんかった uses the slang adverb めっちゃ (super) + past progressive 大きいなった (got big) + nominalizer のに (even though) + Kansai negative past 取れんかった (couldn't get, from 取れなかった): "Even though [it] got super big, [I] couldn't get [it]." The のに concessive marks the size as a surprising contrast to the failure to harvest, and the Kansai 取れんかった replaces standard 取れなかった. 皮むくとエビ2匹 uses the conditional 皮むくと (when [I] peel the rind) + noun エビ (ebi, shrimp; crayfish) + number + counter 2匹 (ni-hiki, two [small animals]): "When [I] peel the rind, [there are] 2 shrimp." The と conditional marks a discovered consequence of peeling. ちょっと大きい凧ってんけど取れんかった uses ちょっと (a bit) + adjective 大きい (big) + noun 凧 (tako, kite/octopus) + Kansai quotative/quotation ってん (from っている/ってるの) + concessive けど + Kansai negative past 取れんかった: "[They're] a bit big [for] kite[s], [I] wonder, but [I] couldn't get [them]." The noun 凧 (tako) is likely an ASR artifact or playful reference; the context suggests river creatures (possibly crayfish or small fish) that were too large or fast to catch. ちっちゃいな uses the colloquial adjective ちっちゃい (tiny) + exclamatory な: "[They're] tiny, na." 逃します uses the polite verb 逃します (nigashimasu, will release; will let go): "[I'll] release [them]." The verb 逃す means both "to let escape" and "to release," and in catch-and-release contexts it carries the positive meaning of returning creatures to the wild. よいしょ is the effort interjection. はい is the affirmative. バイバイ is "bye-bye." ありがとうね uses the gratitude phrase ありがとう + softening particle ね: "Thank you, ne." The ね softens the thanks into a gentle acknowledgment, directed at the river creatures before releasing them.

The existential structure with が + いっぱい is standard Japanese for quantified existence. リトルはい uses the English loanword リトル (ritoru, little) + affirmative はい: "Little, yes." This likely refers to a small crab or creature being identified. でかい。でかい。 repeats the colloquial adjective for emphasis: "Huge. Huge." 取れると思うなんか uses the potential 取れる (can get; can catch) + quotative と + verb 思う (think) + colloquial filler なんか (somehow): "[I] think [I] can get [some], somehow." The volitional marks a decisive commitment to catch-and-release ethics. めっちゃ立派なサイズ uses the slang adverb めっちゃ (super) + na-adjective 立派な (splendid; fine; impressive) modifying サイズ (size): "A super splendid size." 立派 (rippa) is a standard Japanese commendation adjective for impressive quality or stature. ありがとう is directed at the river or the creatures themselves, a common Japanese expression of gratitude toward nature in foraging and fishing contexts. ちょっと逃げ逃がすわ uses the adverb ちょっと (a bit) + compound verb 逃げ逃がす (to let escape while fleeing) + assertive/feminine particle わ: "[I'll] release [them] a bit, wa." 05 Key Grammar Patterns Summary

〜てしまう / 〜ちゃった / 〜きちゃった (Regret / Completion Auxiliary)
枯れてきちゃった, 切っちゃう, 逃げられちゃった. "Started withering [regrettably];" "[I] cut [regrettably];" "[they] escaped [regrettably]." The grammar of unintended outcomes in harvest, preparation, and creature encounters.
〜ていく / 〜てくる (Directional Auxiliaries)

〜そう (Conjectural / Visual Inference)
傷んでいきそう, 大きそうや, 取れそう, 食べにくそうだった. "Seems [they will] spoil;" "seems big;" "seems [I] can get [some];" "seemed difficult to eat." The grammar of visual prediction and sensory inference.
〜気がする (Intuitive Feeling)
まだ早い気がする, 白い気する. "[I] feel [it's] still early;" "[I] feel [it's] white [inside]." The grammar of gut intuition in harvest timing and ripeness assessment.
〜のに / 〜だけど / 〜けど (Concessive Contrasts)
緑色やったのに, 置いときたかったんだけど, 大きいなったのに取れんかった. "Even though [it] was green;" "[I] wanted to leave [them] but...;" "even though [it] got big, [I] couldn't get [it]." The grammar of expectation versus reality.
〜ておく / 〜とく (Preparatory / Sustained Action)
置いときたかった, 置いてても. "[I] wanted to leave [them] [for later];" "even if [I] leave [them] [as is]." The grammar of deferred harvest and sustained state.
