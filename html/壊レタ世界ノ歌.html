A Song for the Ruined World — A full grammatical and lexical breakdown of Yoko Taro's haunting elegy for a broken earth, where machines rust, nature reclaims, and love persists beyond meaning.

01 Verse 1

消えゆく救い 途絶えるカラダ 空は今も暗く
Kieyuku sukui, todaeru karada, sora wa ima mo kuraku.
"Salvation fades away. A body that ceases to continue. The sky is still dark."
JapaneseRomajiPart of SpeechGloss
消えゆくkieyukuCompound verb (attributive)fading away; disappearing — 消える + 行く
救いsukuiNounsalvation; rescue; deliverance
途絶えるtodaeruVerb (attributive)to cease; to be cut off; to be severed

空soraNounsky
はwaTopic particleas for
今もima moAdverbial phraseeven now; still now
暗くkurakuAdjective (adverbial)darkly; dark — adverbial of 暗い kurai
Grammar: 消えゆく is a compound verb: 消える (to disappear; to vanish) + 行く (to go), where 行く as an auxiliary indicates gradual progression away from the speaker: "gradually disappearing; fading into the distance." This is the opposite of 消えてくる (appearing gradually toward the speaker). 途絶える modifies カラダ: "a body [that is] ceasing / being cut off." The katakana カラダ instead of 体 is a deliberate stylistic choice by Yoko Taro, creating emotional distance and suggesting the body is not quite human, or has been objectified by the ruined world. 今も uses も (also; even) to emphasize persistence: "even now, still." 暗く is the adverbial form of 暗い, but the sentence lacks a final copula, creating a fragment that feels like a stage direction or a haiku: "The sky, even now, dark." The grammar of omission is central to the song's desolate beauty.
止まった時計 鳴らない機械 声届かなくて
Tomatta tokei, naranai kikai, koe todokanakute.
"A stopped clock. A machine that doesn't sound. A voice that cannot reach."
JapaneseRomajiPart of SpeechGloss
止まったtomattaVerb (attributive / past)stopped — past of 止まる tomaru
時計tokeiNounclock; watch; timepiece
鳴らないnaranaiVerb (negative)doesn't sound; doesn't ring — negative of 鳴る naru
機械kikaiNounmachine; mechanism
声koeNounvoice
届かなくてtodokanakuteVerb (negative te-form)not reaching; unable to reach — negative te-form of 届く todoku
Grammar: The verse consists of three parallel noun phrases, each a complete conceptual unit without a main verb. This is a hallmark of poetic Japanese: sentences can be built entirely from modified nouns. 止まった modifies 時計: "the stopped clock." 鳴らない modifies 機械: "the machine [that] doesn't sound." 声届かなくて is the most complex: 届かない (doesn't reach) is in the negative te-form, which normally connects to another clause, but here it trails off, suggesting the voice fails to reach and then... nothing. The te-form at the end of a sentence creates incompleteness, a hanging feeling. The imagery is quintessentially NieR: stopped clocks (time has ended), silent machines (androids without purpose), and a voice that cannot bridge the distance. The grammar of absence (negative verbs, past tense, hanging te-form) constructs a world where function has ceased.
これが僕の呪い 犯した罪の深さが キミの願い 穢れた魂抱く贖いだけど
Kore ga boku no noroi, okashita tsumi no fukasa ga, kimi no negai, kegareta tamashii idaku aganai dakedo.
"This is my curse: the depth of the sin I committed is your wish, an atonement that embraces a defiled soul, but..."

Grammar: The sentence structure is deliberately compressed and ambiguous, typical of Yoko Taro's lyric writing. これが僕の呪い is a complete copular sentence: "This is my curse." What follows is an apposition or elaboration: the curse is that "the depth of the sin I committed [is] your wish." The subject marker が after 深さ suggests that this depth is the grammatical subject of an unstated copula: "[It is] your wish." 穢れた魂抱く贖い is a noun phrase where 抱く (to embrace/hold) modifies 贖い: "an atonement [that] embraces a defiled soul." The katakana キミ (instead of 君) is a crucial stylistic choice: it distances the beloved, making them almost otherworldly, or marking them as non-human (fitting NieR:Automata's android protagonists). だけど at the end hangs the sentence, unresolved, before the chorus crashes in. The theological density of the vocabulary (呪い, 罪, 魂, 贖い) frames the song's conflict in terms of sacred violence: the speaker's sin has become the beloved's wish, and atonement requires embracing defilement itself.

02 Chorus 1

Chorus
そう僕らは今 ああ 無価値でも叫ぶ あの壊レタ世界ノ歌
Sou bokura wa ima, aa, muchi demo sakebu, ano kowareta sekai no uta.
"Yes, we are here now. Ah, even if worthless, we scream: that song of the ruined world."
JapaneseRomajiPart of SpeechGloss

僕らbokuraPronounwe; us — masculine plural

ノnoGenitive particle (katakana)of; 's — の in katakana for archaic feel
歌utaNounsong
Grammar: そう at the beginning of the chorus functions as an affirmative response to the unresolved だけど from the verse: "Yes [despite all that], we are here now." 無価値でも uses でも after a noun to mean "even [noun]; even if [noun]." 無価値 (worthless; valueless) is a Sino-Japanese noun (無 + 価値) used here as a concessive condition: "even if [it is] worthless." The most striking grammatical feature is the mixed-script title: 壊レタ世界ノ歌. 壊レタ writes the past/attributive form of 壊れる (to break) with the kanji 壊 + katakana レタ, and ノ writes the genitive particle の in katakana. This is a deliberate stylistic choice evoking archaic Japanese (when の was sometimes written in man'yōgana) and creating visual fragmentation that mirrors the "broken world" of the title. The katakana fractures the words, making them look damaged, mechanical, or alien. The chorus is the song's existential core: meaning is not a prerequisite for expression. Worthlessness does not invalidate the scream.
Chorus
そう僕らは今 ああ 無意味でも願う ただ君との未来を
Sou bokura wa ima, aa, muimi demo negau, tada kimi to no mirai wo.
"Yes, we are here now. Ah, even if meaningless, we wish for: just a future with you."
JapaneseRomajiPart of SpeechGloss
無意味muimiNa-adjective / Nounmeaningless; pointless
でもdemoAdverbial particleeven; even if
願うnegauVerbto wish; to pray; to hope
ただtadaAdverbmerely; only; just; simply

Grammar: 無意味でも follows the same pattern as 無価値でも: [noun/na-adjective] + でも = "even if [adjective]; even though [noun]." 願う is a more solemn verb than 望む or 希望する; it carries religious and prayerful connotations, fitting the song's theological vocabulary. ただ is a restrictive adverb meaning "only; merely; simply," stripping the wish down to its absolute minimum: not wealth, not happiness, not survival, just a future with you. The を after 未来 marks it as the object of the unstated but implied verb of wishing. The object is fronted for emphasis, creating a fragment that feels like a gasp: "Just a future with you." The contrast between キミ (katakana, distant, in the verse) and 君 (standard script, intimate, in the chorus) is significant: in the verse, the beloved is part of the curse's structure; in the chorus, they are the reason for hope. The script shift mirrors the emotional shift from despair to defiant love.
Chorus
あの日の笑顔は愛しいまま 消えて
Ano hi no egao wa itoshii mama, kiete.
"That day's smile, still beloved, vanishes."
JapaneseRomajiPart of SpeechGloss
あの日ano hiTemporal noun phrasethat day
のnoGenitive particleof
笑顔egaoNounsmiling face; smile
はwaTopic particleas for
愛しいitoshiiI-adjectivebeloved; dear; precious
ままmamaNominalizeras it is; still; remaining
消えてkieteVerb (te-form)vanishing; disappearing — te-form of 消える kieru
Grammar: 愛しいまま uses the まま construction: [adjective] + まま = "in the state of being [adjective]; still [adjective]." 愛しい (beloved; dear) is a classical/literary i-adjective with deep emotional weight, stronger than 大好き or 可愛い. まま preserves the state without change: the smile is still beloved, still precious, even as it disappears. 消て is the te-form of 消える, trailing off without a main verb or a connecting clause. The te-form at the end creates a sense of something fading into silence, the word itself dissolving like the smile it describes. The grammar performs the semantics: the sentence vanishes just as the smile vanishes. This is one of the most beautiful lines in the song, a haiku-like compression of love and loss.

03 Verse 2

溢れる緑 零れる命 風は今も遠く
Afureru midori, koboreru inochi, kaze wa ima mo tooku.
"Overflowing green. Spilling life. The wind is still far away."
JapaneseRomajiPart of SpeechGloss
溢れるafureruVerb (attributive)overflowing; brimming; flooding
緑midoriNoungreen; verdure; greenery; nature
零れるkoboreruVerb (attributive)spilling; overflowing; dripping
命inochiNounlife; soul; spirit
風kazeNounwind
はwaTopic particleas for
今もima moAdverbial phraseeven now; still
遠くtookuAdverb / Nounfar; distant; far away
Grammar: The verse opens with two parallel attributive phrases: 溢れる緑 (overflowing green) and 零れる命 (spilling life). Both use intransitive verbs of overflow, but the second carries greater weight because 命 (life) is something that should not spill. The parallel structure equates nature's abundance with life's fragility: both overflow, but one is a blessing and the other a loss. 風は今も遠く mirrors the first verse's 空は今も暗く exactly in structure: [noun] + は + 今も + [adverb]. This parallelism binds the two verses together as descriptions of the same ruined world from different angles. Where the first verse saw darkness and stopped time, the second sees life persisting but out of reach. The wind, a traditional symbol of spirit, voice, and freedom in Japanese poetry (going back to the Man'yōshū), remains distant. The grammar of absence (遠く without a verb, like 暗く before it) leaves the sentence incomplete, a fragment of perception without conclusion.
剥がれる錆びと うごめく刃 死が潜む森に
Hagareru sabi to, ugomeku yaiba, shi ga hisomu mori ni.
"Peeling rust and squirming blades, in the forest where death lurks."
JapaneseRomajiPart of SpeechGloss
剥がれるhagareruVerb (attributive)peeling off; flaking; coming off
錆びsabiNounrust; corrosion
とtoCoordinating particleand

Grammar: 剥がれる is the spontaneous passive-like form of 剥がす (to peel off), but as an intransitive verb it suggests something peeling on its own: rust spontaneously flaking away from corroded metal. This is nature reclaiming the artificial: rust returns to the earth. うごめく is a visceral verb meaning "to squirm; to writhe; to crawl," usually used for worms, snakes, or things that move in a disturbing, undulating way. Applied to 刃 (blades), it creates an image of weapons that are almost alive, moving with malevolent intent. 死が潜む modifies 森: "the forest [in which] death lurks." 潜む is a perfect verb for this: it means not just "exists" but "lies hidden, waiting to strike." The に at the end marks the location where the next clause's action takes place, but the main verb is deferred, creating suspense. The grammar of deferral (ending with に without a main verb) makes the forest feel like a threshold, a place one enters before something happens.

Grammar: 約束殺す is a compound phrase where 殺す modifies 痛み: "pain [that] kills promises." 殺す is a strong, active verb; the pain doesn't just hurt, it destroys the capacity for commitment. The subject marker が after み makes it the grammatical subject of an implied copula: "[It is] your wish." 最後の夢を捨てた modifies 償い: "atonement [that] threw away its final dream." The word 償い (tsugunai) is a near-synonym of 贖い (aganai) from the first verse, but with a subtle difference: 償い is more about compensation or making amends, while 贖い carries stronger religious overtones of redemption. The shift from 贖い to 償い across the two verses suggests the atonement has become more transactional, more desperate. The だけど at the end again defers resolution, setting up the chorus as a response to the curse's second formulation. Where the first verse's curse was about sin's depth becoming the beloved's wish, the second verse's curse is about pain destroying promises, and atonement requiring the sacrifice of even the last dream.

04 Chorus 2 & Final Choruses

Chorus 2
そう僕らは今 ああ 無価値でも叫ぶ この汚れた世界の愛
Sou bokura wa ima, aa, muchi demo sakebu, kono kegareta sekai no ai.
"Yes, we are here now. Ah, even if worthless, we scream: the love of this polluted world."
JapaneseRomajiPart of SpeechGloss
このkonoDeterminerthis
汚れたkegaretaVerb (attributive / past)polluted; stained; defiled — past of 汚れる kegareru
世界sekaiNounworld
のnoGenitive particleof; 's
愛aiNounlove
Grammar: The chorus structure is identical to the first chorus, but the object of the scream changes from あの壊レタ世界ノ歌 (that song of the ruined world) to この汚れた世界の愛 (the love of this polluted world). The determiner shifts from あの (that, distant) to この (this, near), bringing the object closer, making it more immediate and personal. 汚れた is the past/attributive of 汚れる (to be polluted; to be stained), the same verb root as 穢れた (defiled) from the first verse. The world is not just broken (壊レタ) but actively stained (汚れた), and what is screamed is not a song about the world but the love that persists within it. The grammar of possession (世界の愛 = "the world's love" or "love of the world") is ambiguous and productive: it could mean the love that exists in the world, the love the world feels, or the love directed toward the world. All three readings are valid and deepen the line's resonance. The chorus answers the verse's curse with a paradox: even a defiled world can be loved, and that love is worth screaming for even when the scream itself is worthless.
Chorus 2
そう僕らは今 ああ 無意味でも願う ただキミとの時間を 溺れた意識は優しいまま 消えて
Sou bokura wa ima, aa, muimi demo negau, tada kimi to no jikan wo, oboreta ishiki wa yasashii mama, kiete.
"Yes, we are here now. Ah, even if meaningless, we wish for: just time with you. Drowned consciousness, still gentle, vanishes."
JapaneseRomajiPart of SpeechGloss
時間jikanNountime; hours; moments
溺れたoboretaVerb (attributive / past)drowned; submerged — past of 溺れる oboreru

Grammar: 時間 (time; hours) replaces 未来 (future) from the first chorus, narrowing the wish from an open-ended future to the simple fact of shared duration. The shift from 君 (standard) to キミ (katakana) is significant: where the first chorus used 君 for intimacy, the second chorus returns to キミ, the same katakana form used in the verse. This creates a cyclical structure: verse uses キミ, first chorus uses 君, second chorus returns to キミ. The beloved oscillates between distant and near, reflecting the speaker's unstable grasp on them. 溺れた modifies 意識: "drowned consciousness; consciousness [that has] drowned." 溺れる means "to drown; to be submerged," but also carries the sense of being overwhelmed, of sinking into something too deep to escape. 優しいまま again uses the まま construction: "still gentle; remaining gentle." The drowned consciousness stays gentle even as it sinks, even as it vanishes. 消えて trails off in the te-form, just as it did in the first chorus, the word dissolving into silence. The parallelism between the two choruses (愛しいまま 消えて / 優しいまま 消えて) creates a refrain of loss: what is precious and what is gentle both vanish, but they do so while still being what they are.
Final Chorus
祈りが赦しを得られずとも 僕は
Inori ga yurushi wo erarezu tomo, boku wa.
"Even if prayers cannot obtain forgiveness, I [will]."
JapaneseRomajiPart of SpeechGloss
祈りinoriNounprayer; supplication
がgaSubject particlemarks the subject
赦しyurushiNounforgiveness; pardon; absolution
をwoObject particlemarks the object
得られずerarezuVerb (negative adverbial)not being able to obtain — negative of 得られる erareru
ともtomoConcessive particleeven if; even though — literary
僕bokuPronounI; me
はwaTopic particleas for
Grammar: 得られずとも is a literary concessive construction: [potential negative] + ず + とも = "even if [one] cannot [verb]." 得る (eru/uru) is a literary verb meaning "to obtain; to get; to acquire." Its potential form 得られる means "can obtain," and the negative adverbial 得られず means "not being able to obtain." The ず form is the classical negative, more elevated than modern ない. とも after ず creates a strong concessive: "even if [it] cannot be obtained." The sentence ends with 僕は, the topic marker hanging without a predicate. The main verb is omitted, left for the listener to fill in: "Even if prayers cannot obtain forgiveness, I [will continue / will scream / will wish / will love]." This is the song's final grammatical gesture: the speaker refuses to complete the sentence because the action is ongoing, unconditional, and beyond words. The fragment is more powerful than any verb could be. It echoes the hanging te-forms throughout the song (届かなくて, 消えて) but elevates them into a statement of will. The theological vocabulary (祈り, 赦し) returns from the verses, but now the speaker accepts that forgiveness may be impossible and chooses to act anyway. The grammar of omission becomes a grammar of defiance.

05 Key Grammar Patterns Summary

〜て (Hanging Te-Form)
The te-form used at the end of a sentence creates incompleteness, trailing off, or dissolution. Used for 声届かなくて, 消えて. The grammar performs the semantics of loss.
〜まま
"As it is; still; remaining." Preserves a state without change. 愛しいまま, 優しいまま. The beloved remains precious and gentle even as they vanish.
〜でも (Concessive)
"Even if; even though." [Noun/Na-adj] + でも. 無価値でも, 無意味でも. Expresses defiant action in the absence of worth or meaning.
〜ずとも
Literary concessive: "even if [one] cannot [verb]." Classical negative ず + とも. 得られずとも. The song's most elevated grammatical register, reserved for its final statement.
Attributive Verb Phrases as Complete Sentences
Noun phrases built from attributive verbs function as complete poetic statements. 止まった時計, 鳴らない機械, 溢れる緑. No copula needed; the image is enough.
だけど (Hanging Conjunction)
"But; and yet" used at the end of a sentence to defer resolution. Creates tension that the chorus answers. The grammar of unresolved curse.
Script Mixing (壊レタ, ノ, カラダ, キミ)
Katakana inserted into kanji words creates visual fragmentation and emotional distance. 壊レタ = broken on the page as in the world. カラダ = body as object. キミ = beloved as other.
〜が (Subject as Apposition)
が marks subjects that are then equated with other nouns without a copula. 犯した罪の深さがキミの願い. The depth of sin IS the beloved's wish. Grammar of identity through suffering.
