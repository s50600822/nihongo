Japanese names are written surname first, given name second. Given-name cards show one common kanji spelling as the headline, plus other real variants for the same reading — most Japanese readings can be written several different ways.

Recognizing patterns, not just names

There are too many real names below to memorize one by one. Learn the recurring building blocks instead, and you can make a reasonable guess at names you've never seen before.

Common surname components

  • 田 (ta/da) — rice field Tanaka, Yamada, Ikeda
  • 山 (yama) — mountain Yamamoto, Yokoyama, Maruyama
  • 川 (kawa/gawa) — river Ishikawa, Nakagawa, Kawaguchi
  • 村 (mura) — village Nakamura, Kitamura, Murata
  • 本 (moto) — origin / base Yamamoto, Hashimoto, Sakamoto
  • 木 (ki/gi) — tree Suzuki, Aoki, Takagi
  • 井 (i) — well Inoue, Fujii, Ishii
  • 藤 (tō/fuji) — wisteria (Fujiwara clan lineage) Satō, Itō, Kudō, Fujita

Common given-name endings — male

  • -太郎 (-tarō) — traditionally "eldest son" Tarō, Shintarō
  • -郎 (-rō) — "son"; often paired with a birth-order number Ichirō, Jirō, Shirō
  • -太 (-ta) — "big / great," a common modern ending Kenta, Shōta, Sōta
  • -斗/-人 (-to) — "star" or "person," very common in recent decades Yūto, Hayato
  • -也 (-ya) — softening suffix, no strong meaning on its own Takuya, Shūya

Common given-name endings — female

  • -子 (-ko) — "child"; dominant for women born before ~1990 Hanako, Keiko, Yōko
  • -美 (-mi) — "beautiful" Akemi, Yumi, Naomi
  • -菜/-奈 (-na) — "greens" or a phonetic filler kanji Hina, Ayana
  • -花/-咲 (-ka/-saki) — flower / bloom imagery Misaki, Sakika
  • -衣 (-i) — "clothing," common in trendy 2000s–2010s names Yui, Mizuki

Reading the same kanji differently

The biggest trap for learners: the same kanji can be read multiple ways depending on the name, and different kanji can share the same reading. Below, many given-name cards list several real kanji spellings for one reading — that's not a data error, it's how Japanese naming actually works. When in doubt, check the furigana or romaji rather than guessing from kanji alone.
