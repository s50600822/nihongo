我が家のヤギを紹介します

Introducing My Goat — A full grammatical and lexical breakdown of pet loss narrative, adoption decision grammar, animal behavior description, and the emotional rhetoric of rural pet keeping.

Vlogger: Kuma  •  Goat: John  •  Genre: Pet Introduction / Life Story Vlog
こんにちは。くまです。今回はジョンを飼うことになったきっかけをお話ししていこうと思います。 実はヤギを迎える前に2頭の羊を11年ほど飼っていました。性格は2頭とも大変おとなしく、何をするのも一緒でした。散歩に行くとこのようにじゃれ合ったり、とても仲が良かったです。 1頭が先に旅立ってしまい、その1年後、もう1頭も旅立ってしまいました。お別れは寂しかったですが、とても楽しい時間を過ごせました。 2頭がいなくなってからは、心にぽっかり穴が開いたように寂しい気持ちが残りました。空っぽの小屋を見るのはとても辛かったです。 次はヤギとの出会いをお話ししていきます。畑には小屋だけが残っている状態で、取り壊そうかと考えていたところ、とあるSNSでヤギの飼い主募集という投稿を見つけ、すぐに連絡を取りました。 実際に会いに行くと、未去勢のおやぎでした。その場では決めることができず、ヤギを飼っている知り合いに相談したところ、未去勢のおやぎは物を壊す、調子に乗る、臭いとのことで、やめといた方がいいと言われました。やっぱり飼いたいという気持ちが強く、家に迎えることに決めました。 ここからはジョンの紹介をしていきます。名前はジョン。年齢はおそらく3から5歳くらいとのことです。性格はとても甘えん坊で、元気いっぱいのやんちゃな男の子です。 ジョンの好きなことは、飼い主の足の間に顔をスリスリすること。膝の上に乗ることも大好きです。お散歩も大好きで、ジョンより人間の方が先に疲れてしまいます。 我が家に来たばかりの頃は、緊張していたのか、なかなか鳴いてくれませんでした。でも少しずつ心を開いてくれるようになり、今ではとても仲良しです。 ただ実際に飼ってみると大変なこともあります。まずは匂いです。去勢をしていないおやぎなので、独特の匂いがあり、自分のおしっこを体や顔にかけて舐める習性もあって、とても強烈です。服にも匂いが残り、洗濯してもなかなか取れません。 次に物をよく壊します。気分が乗ると小屋や柵を壊し始めたり、飼い主に向かって突進してきて怪我をすることもあります。 さらに餌の問題もあります。以前の飼い主の元ではおやつを食べていなかったらしく、いろんな種類を試してもなかなか食べてくれません。 今は大葉を少し食べてくれるので、朝の草刈りと合わせて与えています。 大変なことも多いですが、それ以上に癒しや楽しさをもらっています。毎日一緒に過ごせる時間は本当に宝物です。 これからもジョンとの暮らしや日常を動画にしていきたいと思います。

01 Before John — Life with the Sheep

実はヤギを迎える前に2頭の羊を11年ほど飼っていました。性格は2頭とも大変おとなしく、何をするのも一緒でした。散歩に行くとこのようにじゃれ合ったり、とても仲が良かったです。
Jitsu wa yagi wo mukaeru mae ni ni-tou no hitsuji wo juuichinen hodo katte imashita. Seikaku wa ni-tou tomo taihen otonashiku, nani wo suru no mo issho deshita. Sanpo ni iku to kono you ni jareattari, totemo naka ga yokatta desu.
"Actually, before welcoming the goat, [I] had been raising two sheep for about 11 years. Both were very docile in personality, and whatever [they] did, [they] did together. When [we] went for walks, [they] would frolic together like this, and [they] got along very well."

Grammar: 実は is a discourse adverb that signals a revelation or background information, commonly used at the beginning of personal stories to create narrative depth. It tells the audience that what follows is not obvious context but a hidden layer of the vlogger's life. ヤギを迎える前に uses the verb 迎える (to welcome; to receive) rather than 飼う (to raise) or 買う (to buy), framing the goat's arrival as a ceremonial event rather than a transaction. The temporal particle 前に (before) establishes the chronology: sheep first, then goat. 11年ほど飼っていました uses the past progressive 飼っていました (had been raising), which is crucial here: it emphasizes duration and continuity over 11 years, not merely a completed action. The progressive past frames the sheep as a long-term presence rather than a brief episode. 性格は2頭とも大変おとなしく uses the inclusive particle とも after a number + counter: 2頭とも (both [sheep]). とも is stronger than も alone and emphasizes that the quality applied to every member of the group without exception. 何をするのも一緒でした uses the nominalized question word pattern 何 + verb + の + も: "whatever [they] did, [it] was together." The の nominalizes the clause "what [they] did," and も universalizes it: "every single thing." This is the grammar of inseparable bonds. 散歩に行くとこのようにじゃれ合ったり uses the generic conditional と after a motion verb: "when [we] went for walks, [they] would frolic like this." The と marks a habitual, discovered state upon walking. じゃれ合ったり uses the representative たり to list a typical activity without exhausting possibilities, suggesting the sheep had many such playful moments.
1頭が先に旅立ってしまい、その1年後、もう1頭も旅立ってしまいました。お別れは寂しかったですが、とても楽しい時間を過ごせました。2頭がいなくなってからは、心にぽっかり穴が開いたように寂しい気持ちが残りました。空っぽの小屋を見るのはとても辛かったです。
Ittou ga saki ni tabidatte shimai, sono ichinen go, mou ittou mo tabidatte shimaimashita. Owakare wa sabishikatta desu ga, totemo tanoshii jikan wo sugosemashita. Ni-tou ga inaku natte kara wa, kokoro ni pokkari ana ga aita you ni sabishii kimochi ga nokorimashita. Karappo no koya wo miru no wa totemo tsurakatta desu.
"One passed away first, and one year after that, the other one passed away too. The farewell was lonely, but [we] were able to spend a very enjoyable time. Since the two disappeared, a lonely feeling remained as if a gaping hole had opened in [my] heart. Looking at the empty shed was very painful."

Grammar: 旅立ってしまい uses the euphemistic verb 旅立つ (tabidatsu, to depart on a journey) + てしまう in its stem form (しまい) connecting to the next clause with て. 旅立つ is the standard Japanese euphemism for death, gentler than 死ぬ (shinu, to die) and appropriate for beloved animals. The てしまう auxiliary adds both regret and finality: "regrettably passed away." The repetition of 旅立ってしまいました for both sheep creates a solemn, rhythmic structure that mirrors the sequential loss. お別れは寂しかったですが uses the topic marker は to topicalize the farewell: "As for the farewell, [it] was lonely, but..." The が after an adjective + ですが sets up a concessive contrast: lonely, BUT [we had good times]. This is the grammar of grief balanced by gratitude, a common emotional structure in pet loss narratives. 過ごせました is the past potential of 過ごす (to spend [time]): "were able to spend." The potential form here expresses fortunate capability, emphasizing that the time together was a gift. 2頭がいなくなってからは uses いなくなる (to disappear; to be gone) + てから (since): "since the two became nonexistent." いなくなる is the standard Japanese expression for a living being's disappearance/death, softer than 死ぬ. The は topicalizes the entire temporal clause, framing everything that follows as a consequence of the loss. 心にぽっかり穴が開いたように is a simile using ように (like; as if) after a complete clause: "as if a gaping hole opened in [my] heart." ぽっかり is an onomatopoeia for a floating, hollow gap, and its combination with 穴が開く (a hole opens) creates a vivid metaphor for emotional emptiness. 寂しい気持ちが残りました uses 残る (to remain; to be left behind) to describe the lingering sadness: "a lonely feeling remained." The grammar of grief (旅立ってしまい → ぽっかり穴が開いたように → 寂しい気持ちが残りました) moves from event to metaphor to emotional residue, a complete poetic arc. 空っぽの小屋を見るのは uses the nominalizer の to turn the verb phrase into a noun clause: "looking at the empty shed." The は topicalizes the act of looking, making it the subject of 辛かった (was painful). 空っぽ (karappo, empty; hollow) is a more evocative word than plain 空 (kara) because its reduplicated structure mimics hollowness.

02 Meeting John — The Decision

畑には小屋だけが残っている状態で、取り壊そうかと考えていたところ、とあるSNSでヤギの飼い主募集という投稿を見つけ、すぐに連絡を取りました。実際に会いに行くと、未去勢のおやぎでした。その場では決めることができず、ヤギを飼っている知り合いに相談したところ、未去勢のおやぎは物を壊す、調子に乗る、臭いとのことで、やめといた方がいいと言われました。
Hatake ni wa koya dake ga nokotte iru joutai de, torikowasou ka to kangaete ita tokoro, to aru SNS de yagi no kainushi boshuu to iu toukou wo mitsuke, sugu ni renraku wo torimashita. Jissai ni ai ni iku to, mi-kyosei no oyagi deshita. Sono ba de wa kimeru koto ga dekinazu, yagi wo katte iru shiriai ni soudan shita tokoro, mi-kyosei no oyagi wa mono wo kowasu, choushi ni noru, kusai to no koto de, yame to ita hou ga ii to iwaremashita.
"In a state where only the shed remained in the field, [when I] was wondering whether to demolish [it], [I] found a post on a certain SNS seeking an owner for a goat, and immediately made contact. When [I] actually went to meet [him], [he] was an unneutered male goat. Unable to decide on the spot, [I] consulted an acquaintance who raises goats, [who said] that unneutered male goats break things, get carried away, and smell, so [I] was told [it] would be better to refrain."

Grammar: 取り壊そうかと考えていたところ is a sophisticated temporal pattern: [volitional] + か + と + 考えていた + ところ. The volitional 取り壊そう (let's demolish) + か creates an embedded question ("whether to demolish"), and と考えていた nominalizes it as an ongoing thought. ところ marks the moment when that ongoing thought was interrupted by a new event (finding the SNS post). This is the standard Japanese grammar for "was just [doing] when [something happened]," and its use here creates a fateful, almost cinematic transition from demolition to adoption. とあるSNS uses the indefinite adjective とある (a certain; some) before a noun: "a certain SNS." とある implies that the specific platform is unimportant or that the vlogger prefers not to name it, adding a touch of privacy while maintaining narrative specificity. ヤギの飼い主募集という投稿 uses という after a noun phrase to specify the type or title of the post: "a post [called/saying] 'seeking an owner for a goat.'" という is the standard Japanese way to introduce names, titles, or categories. すぐに連絡を取りました uses the set phrase 連絡を取る (to make contact; to get in touch): "immediately made contact." The object-verb compound 連絡を取る is a fixed collocation. 未去勢のおやぎ uses the prefix 未- (mi-, not yet; un-) attached to 去勢 (kyosei, castration; neutering): "unneutered." おやぎ (oyagi, male goat; buck) is distinct from ヤギ (yagi, goat generally) and メス (mesu, female). The specification of gender and neutering status is crucial to goat-keeping vocabulary, as intact males have dramatically different behavior and odor. その場では決めることができず uses the noun その場 (that place; on the spot) + は + potential negative in te-form (できず): "being unable to decide on the spot." できず is the literary/connected form of できない, used to chain clauses without a full stop. It creates a sense of hesitation flowing into the next action (consultation). 相談したところ uses た + ところ in a special sense: "when [I] consulted [them], [they said]..." In this pattern, ところ marks the result or response of an action, especially consultation. It is the grammar of seeking and receiving advice. 物を壊す、調子に乗る、臭いとのことで uses a list of plain-form verbs/adjectives + とのことで: "saying that [they] break things, get carried away, [and] smell." とのことで is a hearsay marker that reports what someone said as information rather than fact, preserving the source's voice. やめといた方がいい uses the pattern [te-form] + おく + た + 方がいい: "it would be better to have kept [oneself] from [doing]." やめておく means "to refrain from; to let [it] be," and 方がいい is the advice pattern "it would be better to." The combination creates strong cautionary advice: "you'd better not."
やっぱり飼いたいという気持ちが強く、家に迎えることに決めました。ここからはジョンの紹介をしていきます。名前はジョン。年齢はおそらく3から5歳くらいとのことです。性格はとても甘えん坊で、元気いっぱいのやんちゃな男の子です。

Grammar: やっぱり飼いたいという気持ちが強く uses やっぱり (after all; still) + desiderative 飼いたい (want to raise) + quotative という + 気持ち (feeling) + が強く (is strong): "the feeling that [I] still want to raise [him] is strong." The quotative という nominalizes the desire into a feeling, making it a psychological entity that can have strength. This is the grammar of emotional conviction, where a desire is objectified as a force. 家に迎えることに決めました uses the pattern [verb phrase] + こと + に + 決める: "decided to [welcome him into the home]." ことに決める is the standard Japanese decision pattern, more formal and committed than plain 〜することにした. Its use here marks the adoption as a deliberate, weighty choice made against advice. ここからはジョンの紹介をしていきます uses ここからは (from here [on]) + は (topic marker) + progressive していきます: "From here, [we'll] go ahead with John's introduction." The directional いく marks the narrative as moving forward into the present-tense introduction. 年齢はおそらく3から5歳くらいとのことです uses the hearsay pattern とのことです (it is said that; apparently) after a quoted statement: "apparently about 3 to 5 years old." とのことです is a formal hearsay marker that distances the speaker from the claim, appropriate for information received from a previous owner. おそらく (probably; presumably) adds another layer of uncertainty. The grammar of uncertain biography (おそらく〜くらいとのことです) is standard for rescued or rehomed animals whose exact age is unknown. 甘えん坊で uses the noun 甘えん坊 (amaenbou, someone who loves being pampered) + で (te-form of copula): "being a spoiled/affectionate [boy]." 甘えん坊 is a compound of 甘える (amaeru, to depend on; to seek affection) + 坊 (bou, boy), and it describes both children and animals who crave physical closeness. 元気いっぱいのやんちゃな男の子 uses two noun-modifier chains: [元気いっぱいの (full of energy)] + [やんちゃな (mischievous)] + 男の子 (boy). The use of 男の子 for a goat is affectionate anthropomorphism, common in Japanese pet culture where animals are assigned human gender roles.

03 John's Personality — Affection and Energy

ジョンの好きなことは、飼い主の足の間に顔をスリスリすること。膝の上に乗ることも大好きです。お散歩も大好きで、ジョンより人間の方が先に疲れてしまいます。我が家に来たばかりの頃は、緊張していたのか、なかなか鳴いてくれませんでした。でも少しずつ心を開いてくれるようになり、今ではとても仲良しです。
Jon no suki na koto wa, kainushi no ashi no aida ni kao wo surisuri suru koto. Hiza no ue ni noru koto mo daisuki desu. Osanpo mo daisuki de, Jon yori ningen no hou ga saki ni tsukarete shimaimasu. Wagaya ni kita bakari no koro wa, kinchou shite ita no ka, nakanaka naite kuremasen deshita. Demo sukoshi zutsu kokoro wo hiraite kureru you ni nari, ima de wa totemo nakayoshi desu.
"What John likes is rubbing [his] face between the owner's legs. [He] also loves getting on [my] lap. [He] loves walks too, and the human side gets tired before John does. When [he] had just come to our house, perhaps because [he] was nervous, [he] wouldn't bleat for [me] very much. But [he] gradually started opening [his] heart [to me], and now [we] are very close."

Grammar: ジョンの好きなことは uses the nominalized adjective pattern 好きな + こと: "what John likes [is]." The は topicalizes the entire nominalized clause, making it the subject of an equational sentence. This is the standard Japanese grammar for listing preferences or habits. 飼い主の足の間に顔をスリスリすること uses the onomatopoeia スリスリ (surisuri, rubbing affectionately; nuzzling) as a noun modifier via する: "the act of nuzzling [his] face between the owner's legs." スリスリ is one of the most characteristic Japanese pet-affection onomatopoeias, describing the gentle friction of a face against a beloved object or person. Its use here immediately establishes John as physically affectionate. 膝の上に乗ることも大好きです uses the nominalized verb 乗ること + も (also) + 大好きです: "[He] also loves getting on [my] lap." The も adds this behavior to the previous one, building a portrait of John as a clingy, lap-oriented animal. ジョンより人間の方が先に疲れてしまいます uses the comparative pattern [A] より [B] の方が: "rather than John, the human side [gets tired] first." The 方 (hou, side; direction) turns 人間 (human) into a comparable party, and が marks it as the subject of the comparison. 先に (first; before) adds the temporal sequence: humans tire BEFORE John. The てしまいます adds a touch of humorous regret: "the human regrettably gets tired first." 我が家に来たばかりの頃は uses the recent-past pattern 来たばかり (had just come) + の頃 (the time when): "around the time [when he] had just come to our house." ばかり after a past verb means "just [did]," emphasizing recency. The は topicalizes this temporal frame, setting up a contrast with the present. 緊張していたのか uses the explanatory の + question か: "perhaps because [he] was nervous." The のか turns a statement into a tentative explanation, softening the claim from certainty to speculation. This is the grammar of charitable interpretation, where an animal's silence is attributed to stress rather than coldness. なかなか鳴いてくれませんでした uses なかなか + negative verb: "not readily [did]." なかなか with a negative means "not easily; not readily; hardly," and its use here expresses disappointed expectation: the vlogger wanted John to bleat but he wouldn't. 鳴いてくれませんでした uses the benefactive くれる (to do for me) in negative polite past: "wouldn't bleat [for me]." The benefactive frames the bleating as a gift the vlogger hoped to receive. 少しずつ心を開いてくれるようになり uses 少しずつ (little by little) + 心を開く (to open one's heart) + てくれる (do for me) + ようになり (came to [do]): "gradually came to open [his] heart [for me]." This is the standard Japanese grammar for taming and trust-building: ようになる marks the behavioral change as a developmental process, and てくれる frames the trust as a gift given by the animal to the human. The grammar of relationship development (鳴いてくれませんでした → 心を開いてくれるようになり → 仲良しです) traces a complete arc from initial distance to present intimacy.

Grammar: ただ実際に飼ってみると uses ただ (however; but) + 実際に (actually) + compound verb 飼ってみる (try raising) + conditional と: "However, when [I] actually tried raising [him]." てみる is the trial auxiliary meaning "to try [doing] and see," and its use here frames the challenges as discovered through lived experience rather than predicted. The と after みる marks the discovery that follows as a natural consequence of the trial. まずは匂いです uses まず (first of all) + は (topic marker) + です: "First of all is the smell." This is the standard enumeration opener in Japanese, setting up a numbered or sequenced list of points. 去勢をしていないおやぎなので uses the negative progressive していない (has not done) as an attributive modifier for おやぎ, with なので providing objective causality: "Because [he] is a goat that has not had neutering done." The grammar of medical status (去勢をしていない) is important for animal care contexts, where procedures are described as states rather than events. 自分のおしっこを体や顔にかけて舐める習性もあって uses the te-form chain かけて (splash) + 舐める (lick) to describe a two-step behavior pattern, with もあって (also exists) adding it to the previously mentioned odor: "there is also a habit of spraying urine and licking [it]." おしっこ is the childish/casual word for urine, and its use here rather than the clinical 尿 (nyou) or polite おしっこ creates an intimate, unvarnished tone appropriate for honest pet care discussion. 服にも匂いが残り uses に as the locative target of 残る (to remain): "the smell remains on clothes too." The も (also) expands the smell's reach from the goat's body to the owner's clothing. 洗濯してもなかなか取れません uses the concessive ても (even when) + なかなか + negative potential 取れません (cannot be removed): "even when [I] do laundry, [it] cannot readily be removed." なかなか with a negative expresses frustrated effort: the vlogger has tried washing but the smell persists. 次に物をよく壊します uses 次に (next) as a transition marker + よく (often) + verb: "Next, [he] often breaks things." よく here means "frequently; often," a different sense from its usual "well." 気分が乗ると uses the set phrase 気分が乗る (to be in the mood; to get excited; to get into it) + conditional と: "When [he] gets in the mood." This idiom uses 乗る (to ride; to get on) metaphorically for emotional momentum: one's mood "gets on" like boarding a vehicle. 小屋や柵を壊し始めたり uses the compound verb 壊し始める (to start breaking) + representative たり: "starts breaking the shed or fence [among other things]." 飼い主に向かって突進してきて uses the directional compound 突進してくる (charge [toward me]) + te-form chaining to 怪我をする: "charges at [me] and injures [me]." The くる marks the charge as movement toward the speaker, making the danger immediate and personal. ことがあります uses the nominalizer こと + が + あります: "sometimes [it] happens that." This is the standard Japanese pattern for occasional events, softening the danger from constant to intermittent.

The benefactive くれる frames the eating as a favor to the vlogger, emphasizing how much effort was required to get John to accept food. 今は大葉を少し食べてくれるので uses 今は (now [topic]) + object + 少し (a little) + benefactive 食べてくれる (eats [for me]) + ので (because): "Because now [he] eats a little perilla leaf [for me]." 大葉 (ooba, perilla leaf; shiso) is a common Japanese herb, and its use as goat food reflects the vlogger's resourceful use of available plants. 朝の草刈りと合わせて与えています uses 朝の草刈り (morning grass cutting) + と (with) + 合わせて (combining; matching) + 与えています (am giving): "[I] give [it] combined with morning grass cutting." 合わせる means "to match; to combine; to coordinate," and its te-form creates a means clause: "by combining with grass cutting." The grammar of integrated care (草刈りと合わせて与えています) shows how John's feeding is woven into the vlogger's daily rural routine. 大変なことも多いですが uses 大変なこと (difficult things) + も (also) + 多い (many) + ですが: "There are also many difficult things, but..." The が sets up the following contrast. それ以上に uses それ (that) + 以上 (more than; above) + に: "beyond that; more than that." It is a comparative adverb that measures the following positive against the preceding negative. 癒しや楽しさをもらっています uses the nominalized adjectives 癒し (healing; from 癒す) and 楽しさ (joy; from 楽しい) as objects of もらっています (am receiving): "[I] am receiving healing and joy." The grammar of emotional valuation (大変なことも多いですが → それ以上に → 宝物です) creates a measured, reasoned affirmation of love that acknowledges difficulty without being diminished by it.

05 Key Grammar Patterns Summary

〜かと考えていたところ (Interrupted Thought)
取り壊そうかと考えていたところ. "Was just wondering whether to [do] when..." The grammar of fate interrupting plans.
〜とのことで / 〜とのことです (Hearsay)
臭いとのことで, 3から5歳くらいとのことです. Reporting others' words as information while preserving distance.
〜てくれる / 〜てくれませんでした (Benefactive)
鳴いてくれませんでした, 心を開いてくれる, 食べてくれる. The grammar of animals doing things as gifts to their human.
〜ようになり (Behavioral Development)
心を開いてくれるようになり. "Came to [do]." The standard grammar for trust-building and taming.
〜てみると (Discovery Through Trial)
飼ってみると. "When [I] tried [doing]." The grammar of experiential discovery in pet keeping.
〜た方がいい (Advice)
やめといた方がいい. "It would be better to [do]." The grammar of received cautionary wisdom.
〜以上に (Exceeding Comparison)
それ以上に癒しや楽しさ. "More than that." The grammar of emotional accounting where love outweighs difficulty.
