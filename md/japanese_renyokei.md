#The Continuative Form (連用形 - Ren'yōkei)

This lesson explores the versatile **連用形 (*ren'yōkei*)**, often translated as the **continuative form** or **conjunctive form**. We use the classical idiom **天高く馬肥ゆる秋** (*ten takaku uma koyuru aki*) as our foundation to explore how this form bridges clauses, builds compounds, and transforms vocabulary in both Classical and Modern Japanese.

---

## 1. Foundation: Breakdown of 天高く馬肥ゆる秋

In the phrase **天高く馬肥ゆる秋** ("Autumn is the season when the sky is high and horses grow plump"), the *ren'yōkei* acts as the primary syntactic engine linking descriptions together.

```
 [ Clause A: 天高く ]       +      [ Clause B: 馬肥ゆる ]     →   [ Core Noun: 秋 ]
(Sky is high [Ren'yōkei])        (Horses grow plump [Rentaikei])       (Autumn)
```

*   **天 (てん - *ten*):** Noun; "sky". Subject of Clause A.
*   **高く (たかく - *takaku*):** Adjective; **連用形 (*ren'yōkei*)** of the i-adjective *takai* (high). Instead of ending the thought, it acts like a comma or the word "and", suspending the sentence to smoothly transition into the next clause.
*   **馬 (うま - *uma*):** Noun; "horse(s)". Subject of Clause B.
*   **肥ゆる (こゆる - *koyuru*):** Verb; **連体形 (*rentaikei* / attributive form)** of the classical verb *koyu* (to grow plump). It modifies the final noun.
*   **秋 (あき - *aki*):** Noun; "autumn".

---

## 2. Structural Patterns of the 連用形 (Modern Japanese)

To form the *ren'yōkei*, look at the grammatical category of the word:

| Word Category | Dictionary Form | 連用形 (*Ren'yōkei*) | Sentence Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **I-Adjectives** | 高い (*takai* / high) | **高く** (*takaku*) | 空が**高く**澄み渡る。<br>(The sky clears up **high**.) |
| **Na-Adjectives** | 静かだ (*shizuka da* / quiet) | **静かに** (*shizuka ni*) | **静かに**部屋を出る。<br>(Leave the room **quietly**.) |
| **Group 1 Verbs (U)** | 書く (*kaku* / to write) | **書き** (*kaki*) | 論文を**書き**終えた。<br>(I finished **writing** the essay.) |
| **Group 2 Verbs (Ru)** | 食べる (*taberu* / to eat) | **食べ** (*tabe*) | 朝食を**食べ**始める。<br>(Begin **eating** breakfast.) |
| **Irregular Verbs** | する (*suru* / to do)<br>来る (*kuru* / to come) | **し** (*shi*)<br>**き** (*ki*) | 勉強**し**に行く (Go to study)<br>日常を生き**き**る (Live out daily life) |

## 3. Four Core Functions of the 連用形

### Function A: Clause Linking (Literary "And")
In modern spoken Japanese, you link two clauses using the *te*-form (て形). In written, formal, or classical Japanese, you drop the *te* and use the pure *ren'yōkei*. This adds rhythm, brevity, and sophistication.

*   **Casual / Spoken (*Te*-form):**
    *   物価が**上がって**、生活が苦しくなる。
    *   *Bukka ga agatte, seikatsu ga kurushiku naru.*
    *   (Prices rise, **and** life becomes difficult.)
*   **Formal / Literary (*Ren'yōkei*):**
    *   物価が**上がり**、生活が苦しくなる。
    *   *Bukka ga agari, seikatsu ga kurushiku naru.*
    *   (Prices rise, **and** life becomes difficult.)

### Function B: Noun Conversion (Nominalisation)
Many verbs shift into self-standing nouns when placed in their *ren'yōkei* form.

*   話す (*hanasu* / to speak) → **話** (*hanashi* / a talk, a story)
*   動く (*ugoku* / to move) → **動き** (*ugoki* / movement)
*   休む (*yasumu* / to rest) → **休み** (*yasumi* / a break, a holiday)
*   答える (*kotaeru* / to answer) → **答え** (*kotae* / an answer)

### Function C: Building Compound Verbs (複合動詞)
You can stitch two separate actions together by putting the first verb into its *ren'yōkei* form and attaching a second auxiliary verb.

*   **書き** (*kaki* / write) + 直す (*naosu* / to fix) = **書き直す** (*kakinaosu* / to rewrite)
*   **走り** (*hashiri* / run) + 出す (*dasu* / to exit/start) = **走り出す** (*hashiridasu* / to burst into a run)
*   **食べ** (*tabe* / eat) + すぎる (*sugiru* / to exceed) = **食べすぎる** (*tabesugiru* / to overeat)

### Function D: Attaching Grammar Helpers (Auxiliary Endings)
The *ren'yōkei* is the necessary anchor point for critical grammatical extensions:
*   Politeness: 行き + **ます** (*iki-masu* / I go)
*   Desire: 食べ + **たい** (*tabe-tai* / I want to eat)
*   Difficulty: 分かり + **にくい** (*wakari-nikui* / hard to understand)

## 4. Classical Japanese Focus: The Shift of 肥ゆる (*Koyuru*)

Understanding how the verb **肥ゆる** (*koyuru*) functions reveals how Japanese verb morphology evolved over the centuries.

In Classical Japanese (*bungo*), *koyu* is categorized as a **Lower Bigrade Verb (下二段活用 - *Shimo-nidan katsuyō*)**. It conjugates using vowels along the *u* and *e* rows.

### Classical Conjugation Matrix for 肥ゆ (*koyu*)
*   **Imperfect Form (未然形):** 肥え (*koe*) — as in 肥えず (*koezu* / does not grow plump)
*   **Continuative Form (連用形):** **肥え** (*koe*) — as in 肥えたり (*koetari* / grew plump)
*   **Terminal Form (終止形):** 肥ゆ (*koyu*) — ends a sentence: 馬肥ゆ。 (*The horse grows plump.*)
*   **Attributive Form (連体形):** **肥ゆる** (*koyuru*) — modifies a noun: **肥ゆる秋** (*The autumn where horses grow plump*)
*   **Exclamatory Form (已然形):** 肥ゆれ (*koyure*) — used with grammatical particles like *ba*.
*   **Imperative Form (命令形):** 肥えよ (*koeyo*) — command form.

### The Evolution into Modern Japanese
Because managing two vowel tiers (*u* and *e*) was grammatically complex, spoken Japanese eventually flattened these bigrade verbs into standard **Monograde Verbs (下一段活用 - *Shimo-ichidan katsuyō*)** by dropping the *u* variations entirely.

Here is how the forms split and merged over time:

| Syntactic Role | Classical Form (*Bungo*) | Modern Form (*Kogo*) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sentence End (Terminal)** | 馬**肥ゆ**。 (*Uma koyu.*) | 馬が**肥える**。 (*Uma ga koeru.*) |
| **Modifying Noun (Attributive)** | **肥ゆる**秋 (*Koyuru aki*) | **肥える**秋 (*Koeru aki*) |
| **Continuative (*Ren'yōkei*)** | **肥え**たり (*Koetari*) | **肥え**て / **肥え**ます (*Koete* / *Koemasu*) |

By studying the *ren'yōkei* through idioms like **天高く馬肥ゆる秋**, you gain access to the architectural lineage of Japanese poetry, formal prose, and advanced sentence composition.
