Grammar: 食べときや is a Kansai contraction of 食べておきや, where 〜ておく (to do in advance; to do for later) is contracted to 〜とく and then combined with the Kansai assertive particle や. 食べておく means "to eat [while one has the chance]; to eat properly [in advance of something]," and with や it becomes a recommendation or mild command: "[You] should eat [properly], [I'm telling you]." かもしれん is the Kansai form of かもしれない (might; may), with the negative ない reduced to ん. 見てもらわなかん is a heavy Kansai contraction: the full Standard form is 見てもらわなければならない (must get [someone] to look at [it]). In Kansai, なければならない contracts dramatically: 見てもらわなかん = "[I] gotta get [them] to look." The grammar of obligation in Kansai is much more compressed than Standard Japanese, turning a multi-morpheme auxiliary chain into a single rhythmic unit. The shift from commenting on the goat eating (美味しいなってよかったね) to realizing the vlogger needs medical attention (病院に先生見てもらわなかんな) is the comedic pivot of the video: the walk has gone from cute to genuinely injurious.

Grammar: なんでやねん is the most famous phrase in Kansai dialect, often considered its signature exclamation. It literally means "Why is it [that]!" where なんで = why, や = Kansai copula (だ), and ねん = emphatic/emotive final particle (from の/ん). In practice, it functions as an indignant "Why the hell!" or "What the heck!" and is the standard Kansai reaction to something absurd or annoying. Its use here after a goat headbutt is perfectly idiomatic. 頭突きしようとした uses the attemptive auxiliary 〜う/ようとする: [verb in hortative] + とする = "to try to [verb]; to be about to [verb]." 頭突きしようとした = "tried to headbutt." 遊べへん is the Kansai negative potential form: 遊べない (can't play) becomes 遊べへん in Kansai, where 〜ない replaces with 〜へん. This is one of the most distinctive Kansai morphological features. The potential negative へん applies to all verbs in Kansai, making it a systematic dialect feature rather than a casual contraction. 09 Key Grammar Patterns Summary

Kansai Copula や & Negative へん
や replaces だ; へん replaces ない. あかん, やらへん, バレへん, 離せへん. The core morphological system of Kansai dialect.
〜かもしれへん / 〜かもしれん
Kansai dubitative forms. 買ったかもしれへん, 慣れないかもしれん. Possibility and uncertainty in dialect.
〜てる / 〜てん / 〜てんじゃん
Colloquial progressive contractions + emphatic tag. 見てる, 忘れてん, 終わってんじゃん. The sound of live vlogging.
〜ちゃった / 〜すぎちゃった
Regrettable completion. 入れすぎちゃった, あげちゃった, こぼしちった. The grammar of cooking mistakes.
〜なきゃ / 〜とこう (Obligation & Prep)
乗せなきゃ (must put on), 入れとこう (let's put in [in advance]). The grammar of cooking urgency and preparation.
〜てきてた / 〜てきた (Emergent Change)
Darkになってきてた, 疲れてきた. The grammar of gradual processes becoming noticeable.
なんとしてでも / なんとかなる
"By any means" and "it'll work out somehow." The philosophy of rural improvisation in Japanese grammar.
〜まみれ / 〜そう (Seeming & State)
粉まみれ (covered in flour), 美味しそう (looks delicious). Visual and tactile description in cooking vlogs.
Effort & Texture Onomatopoeia
よいしょ, シャキシャキ, ぐしゃ. The non-lexical grammar of physical labor and sensory experience.
