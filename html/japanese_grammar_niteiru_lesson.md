# Japanese Grammar Lesson: 〜に似ている (To Resemble / Be Similar To)

In Japanese, when you want to say that someone or something resembles another entity, you use the verb **似る (niru)**. Because a resemblance is an ongoing state, it is almost always used in its continuous form: **似ている (nite iru)**.

---

## 1. The Core Sentence Structure

The standard formula to express "A resembles B" or "A is similar to B" is:

$$\text{[Entity A]} \quad \mathbf{は} \quad \text{[Entity B]} \quad \mathbf{に} \quad \mathbf{似ている}$$

*   **は (wa):** Marks **Entity A** as the topic/subject you are describing.
*   **に (ni):** Marks **Entity B** as the target or standard of comparison (the thing being resembled).
*   **似ている (nite iru):** The state of resembling or being similar.

---

## 2. Comprehensive Examples by Category

This grammar pattern applies broadly across people, animals, objects, abstract ideas, and actions.

### A. Comparing People (Appearance & Personality)
*   **君は僕に似ている。**
    *   *Kimi wa boku ni nite iru.*
    *   You are similar to me. / You look like me. (Casual)
*   **彼は父親にそっくり似ている。**
    *   *Kare wa chichioya ni sokkuri nite iru.*
    *   He looks exactly like his father.
*   **お姉さんはお母さんに性格が似ていますね。**
    *   *Onēsan wa okāsan ni seikaku ga nite imasu ne.*
    *   The older sister's personality resembles her mother's, doesn't it? (Polite)

### B. Animals & Nature
*   **この猫は虎に似ている。**
    *   *Kono neko wa tora ni nite iru.*
    *   This cat resembles a tiger.
*   **あの犬は狐に似た顔をしている。**
    *   *Ano inu wa kitsune ni nita kao o shite iru.*
    *   That dog has a face that looks like a fox.
*   **あの雲はソフトクリームに似ている。**
    *   *Ano kumo wa sofuto kuriimu ni nite iru.*
    *   That cloud looks like soft-serve ice cream.

### C. Physical Objects & Architecture
*   **このスマートフォンはiPhoneにとても似ている。**
    *   *Kono sumātofon wa aifon ni totemo nite iru.*
    *   This smartphone looks very similar to an iPhone.
*   **京都の街並みは、一部のヨーロッパの古都に雰囲気が似ている。**
    *   *Kyōto no machinami wa, ichibu no yōroppa no koto ni fun'iki ga nite iru.*
    *   The atmosphere of Kyoto's streets resembles some old European capital cities.
*   **この人工肉は本物の牛肉の食感に似ている。**
    *   *Kono jinkō-niku wa honmono no gyūniku no shokkan ni nite iru.*
    *   This artificial meat is similar to the texture of real beef.

### D. Abstract Concepts, Languages, & Systems
*   **イタリア語はスペイン語に非常によく似ている。**
    *   *Itaria-go wa Supein-go ni hijō ni yoku nite iru.*
    *   Italian is extremely similar to Spanish.
*   **この２つの数式は構造が似ているが、答えは全く違う。**
    *   *Kono futatsu no sūshiki wa kōzō ga nite iru ga, kotae wa mattaku chigau.*
    *   These two mathematical formulas are similar in structure, but their answers are completely different.
*   **君の考え方は、私の昔の哲学に似ている。**
    *   *Kimi no kangaekata wa, watashi no mukashi no tetsugaku ni nite iru.*
    *   Your way of thinking is similar to my old philosophy.

### E. Situations & Events
*   **今の経済状況は10年前の危機に似ている。**
    *   *Ima no keizai jōkyō wa jū-nen mae no kiki ni nite iru.*
    *   The current economic situation is similar to the crisis 10 years ago.
*   **この映画のストーリーは、あの有名な小説に似ています。**
    *   *Kono eiga no sutōrii wa, ano yūmei na shōsetsu ni nite imasu.*
    *   The story of this movie resembles that famous novel. (Polite)

---

## 3. Formality & Tense Variations

Depending on who you are talking to, or the timing of the comparison, you can conjugatate **似ている**:

| Formality / Tense | Japanese Form | Romaji | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Casual Present** | 似ている | nite iru | Resembles / Is similar to |
| **Polite Present** | 似ています | nite imasu | Resembles / Is similar to (Polite) |
| **Casual Past** | 似ていた | nite ita | Resembled / Was similar to |
| **Polite Past** | 似ていました | nite imashita | Resembled / Was similar to (Polite) |
| **Negative Casual**| 似ていない | nite inai | Does not resemble / Is not similar to |
| **Negative Polite**| 似ていません | nite imasen | Does not resemble / Is not similar to (Polite) |

---

## 4. Modifying Nouns Directly

If you want to use the resemblance to describe a noun directly (e.g., "A child who looks like their father"), you can use **似ている** or **似た** right before the noun.

*   **父親に似ている子供** (*Chichioya ni nite iru kodomo*) $\rightarrow$ A child who resembles their father.
*   **狐に似た動物** (*Kitsune ni nita dōbutsu*) $\rightarrow$ An animal that looks like a fox.
