How to Read This Index

  • Clan name is given in English, followed by the kanji and reading in hiragana/romaji.
  • Meaning explains the etymology or literal sense of the name where it is interesting or decipherable.
  • Famous people lists key historical figures with their Japanese names in kanji.
  • Clans are grouped by historical period. The "Common Family Names" section covers names you may encounter in modern Japan.

The Four Noble Clans

源平藤橘 (Genpei Tōkitsu)

The Four Noble Clans (源平藤橘, Genpei Tōkitsu) were the most powerful aristocratic families of the Heian period (794-1185). They shaped imperial politics, produced the first shoguns, and their descendants would dominate Japanese history for a millennium.

Minamoto 源 みなもと / Minamoto
Heian
"Source" or "origin" — bestowed by emperors on demoted princes
The Minamoto were cadet branches of the imperial line. When an emperor fathered too many sons, some were given the surname Minamoto and removed from succession. This created a vast network of warrior-aristocrats. The Seiwa Genji branch produced Minamoto no Yoritomo, founder of the Kamakura shogunate.
Famous People
  • Minamoto no Yoritomo 源頼朝 — 1st shogun
  • Minamoto no Yoshitsune 源義経 — tragic hero
  • Minamoto no Yoshiie 源義家 — early warlord
Taira 平 たいら / Taira
Heian
"Flat" or "peaceful" — also given to imperial offshoots
Like the Minamoto, the Taira were imperial descendants given a new surname. They reached their zenith under Taira no Kiyomori, who briefly dominated the court in the late 12th century. Their defeat by the Minamoto in the Genpei War (1180-1185) is one of the great tragic narratives of Japanese history.
Famous People
  • Taira no Kiyomori 平清盛 — de facto ruler
  • Taira no Shigemori 平重盛 — Kiyomori's son
  • Taira no Atsumori 平敦盛 — young noble, Noh subject
Fujiwara 藤原 ふじわら / Fujiwara
Asuka–Heian
"Wisteria field" — one of the most poetic clan names in Japan
The Fujiwara dominated Japanese politics for over 500 years through a strategy of marrying their daughters to emperors and ruling as regents. Founded by Nakatomi no Kamatari, they were the power behind the throne through the Nara and Heian periods. Their influence declined with the rise of the samurai.
Famous People
  • Fujiwara no Michinaga 藤原道長 — peak of Fujiwara power
  • Fujiwara no Teika 藤原定家 — poet & anthologist
  • Fujiwara no Hidehira 藤原秀衡 — Ōshū lord
Tachibana 橘 たちばな / Tachibana
Nara–Heian
"Mandarin orange" — a fragrant citrus, symbol of good fortune
The smallest of the Four Noble Clans, the Tachibana were founded by Agata Inukai no Michiyo, a powerful court lady. They never achieved the dominance of the Fujiwara but remained influential aristocrats. The name Tachibana survives today as one of the most common surnames in Japan.
Famous People
  • Tachibana no Moroe 橘諸兄 — 8th c. statesman
  • Tachibana no Hayanari 橘逸勢 — calligrapher

Ancient Clans (Asuka & Nara Periods)

飛鳥・奈良時代の氏族

Before the rise of the samurai, power in Japan was contested between aristocratic clans (uji) who served the imperial court. These families shaped Buddhism, statecraft, and early Japanese literature.

Soga 蘇我 そが / Soga
Asuka
"Revive I" — obscure etymology, possibly from a place name
The Soga were the first great political clan of Japan. They championed Buddhism against the native Shinto establishment and dominated the court through the 6th and 7th centuries. Their downfall came in the Isshi Incident (645), when they were assassinated by Nakatomi no Kamatari and Prince Naka no Ōe.
Famous People
  • Soga no Umako 蘇我馬子 — brought Buddhism to Japan
  • Soga no Iruka 蘇我入鹿 — assassinated in coup
Mononobe 物部 もののべ / Mononobe
Asuka
"Thing clan" — from their role as imperial arms-keepers
The Mononobe were a warrior-aristocrat clan who opposed the introduction of Buddhism, defending native Shinto traditions. They were defeated by the pro-Buddhist Soga in a series of political and military clashes, marking a turning point in Japanese religious history.
Famous People
  • Mononobe no Moriya 物部守屋 — anti-Buddhist leader
Nakatomi 中臣 なかとみ / Nakatomi
Asuka
"Middle retainer" — hereditary Shinto ritualists
The Nakatomi were one of the most ancient Shinto priestly families. Nakatomi no Kamatari led the coup that overthrew the Soga and was rewarded with the new surname Fujiwara, founding the greatest aristocratic clan in Japanese history.
Famous People
  • Nakatomi no Kamatari 中臣鎌足 — founder of Fujiwara
Ōtomo 大伴 おおともの / Ōtomo
Nara
"Great companion" — imperial military retainers
The Ōtomo were an ancient military clan who served as imperial guards and generals. Ōtomo no Yakamochi is traditionally credited as the final compiler of the Man'yōshū, Japan's oldest anthology of poetry. The clan declined in the Heian period.
Famous People
  • Ōtomo no Yakamochi 大伴家持 — Man'yōshū compiler
Ōe 大江 おおえ / Ōe
Nara–Heian
"Great bay" — from a geographic origin
The Ōe were a scholarly aristocratic family known for their learning in Chinese classics. Ōe no Masafusa and Ōe no Yasunori were prominent scholars. The name survives in the modern writer Ōe Kenzaburō, though he is not directly descended from this clan.
Famous People
  • Ōe no Masafusa 大江匡房 — scholar & statesman
  • Ōe no Kenzaburō 大江健三郎 — Nobel laureate (namesake)
Sugawara 菅原 すがわら / Sugawara
Heian
"Sedge plain" — from a type of marsh grass
The Sugawara were a scholarly clan who produced generations of imperial tutors and diplomats. Sugawara no Michizane was deified as Tenjin, the god of learning, after being unjustly exiled. Today, students pray at Tenjin shrines before exams.
Famous People
  • Sugawara no Michizane 菅原道真 — deified scholar

Kamakura & Muromachi Shogunal Clans

鎌倉・室町幕府の氏族

The Kamakura (1185-1333) and Muromachi (1336-1573) shogunates were dominated by warrior clans who controlled Japan through military might while maintaining a facade of imperial legitimacy.

Hōjō 北条 ほうじょう / Hōjō
Kamakura
"North line" — from a place in Izu Province
The Hōjō were the hereditary regents (shikken) of the Kamakura shogunate, effectively ruling Japan while the Minamoto shoguns served as figureheads. They established a sophisticated system of vassal control and survived until their destruction by the restorationist Emperor Go-Daigo.
Famous People
  • Hōjō Tokimune 北条時宗 — repelled Mongol invasion
  • Hōjō Masako 北条政子 — "nun shogun," power behind throne
Ashikaga 足利 あしかが / Ashikaga
Muromachi
"Foot/leg profit" — from a place in Shimotsuke
The Ashikaga were a branch of the Minamoto who established the Muromachi shogunate after overthrowing the Kenmu Restoration. Their era is famous for the development of Zen Buddhism, Noh theater, and tea ceremony, but also for the Ōnin War which plunged Japan into the Sengoku chaos.
Famous People
  • Ashikaga Takauji 足利尊氏 — founder of shogunate
  • Ashikaga Yoshimitsu 足利義満 — Kitayama golden age
Hosokawa 細川 ほそかわ / Hosokawa
Muromachi–Present
"Narrow river" — from a geographic feature
The Hosokawa were one of the most powerful Ashikaga vassal clans, serving as kanrei (deputy shoguns). They survived the Sengoku period, became daimyo of Kumamoto, and produced a prime minister (Hosokawa Morihiro) in the 1990s. They are also famous for their exquisite shodō (calligraphy) and chadō (tea ceremony) traditions.
Famous People
  • Hosokawa Katsumoto 細川勝元 — Ōnin War leader
  • Hosokawa Gracia 細川ガラシャ — Christian convert
  • Hosokawa Morihiro 細川護熙 — PM (1993-94)
Yamana 山名 やまな / Yamana
Muromachi
"Mountain name" — from a place in Harima
The Yamana were a powerful Muromachi-era clan who once controlled half of Japan's provinces. Their rivalry with the Hosokawa triggered the devastating Ōnin War (1467-1477), which destroyed Kyoto and ushered in the Sengoku period.
Famous People
  • Yamana Sōzen 山名宗全 — Ōnin War leader

Sengoku Period Warlords

戦国大名

The Sengoku period (1467-1615) was an era of near-constant civil war. Powerful regional lords (daimyō) fought for control of Japan, producing some of the most famous names in samurai history.

Takeda 武田 たけだ / Takeda
Sengoku
"Warrior field" — fitting for a samurai clan
The Takeda of Kai Province were one of the most feared military clans of the Sengoku period. Under Takeda Shingen, they developed the famous Takeda cavalry and nearly conquered all of eastern Japan. Their rivalry with Uesugi Kenshin at Kawanakajima is legendary.
Famous People
  • Takeda Shingen 武田信玄 — "Tiger of Kai"
  • Takeda Katsuyori 武田勝頼 — last Takeda lord
Uesugi 上杉 うえすぎ / Uesugi
Sengoku
"Upper cedar" — from a place in Tōtōmi
The Uesugi were originally from Tōtōmi but became powerful in Echigo Province. Uesugi Kenshin, known as the "Dragon of Echigo," was one of the greatest generals of the era. The clan later adopted the name through the Nagao-Uesugi succession.
Famous People
  • Uesugi Kenshin 上杉謙信 — "Dragon of Echigo"
  • Uesugi Kagekatsu 上杉景勝 — adopted heir
Date 伊達 だて / Date
Sengoku–Edo
"Achieve/reach" — from a place in Mutsu
The Date ruled the rugged Tōhoku region from Sendai. Date Masamune, the "One-Eyed Dragon," was known for his ambition, pragmatism, and early support for foreign trade. The Date survived into the Edo period as daimyo of Sendai.
Famous People
  • Date Masamune 伊達政宗 — "One-Eyed Dragon"
Sanada 真田 さなだ / Sanada
Sengoku
"True field" — from a place in Shinano
The Sanada were a relatively small clan from Shinano who became legendary for their tenacity. Sanada Yukimura's heroic last stand at Osaka Castle made him a symbol of loyalty in Japanese popular culture. Their castle of Ueda withstood two sieges by the Tokugawa.
Famous People
  • Sanada Yukimura 真田幸村 — "Crimson Demon"
  • Sanada Nobuyuki 真田信之 — survivor, Edo daimyo
Mōri 毛利 もうり / Mōri
Sengoku
"Fur/hair" — from a place in Sagami
The Mōri rose from minor status to control much of western Japan under Mōri Motonari, who famously taught his three sons to cooperate like three arrows that cannot be broken together. They were powerful allies of Toyotomi Hideyoshi.
Famous People
  • Mōri Motonari 毛利元就 — "three arrows" strategist
  • Mōri Terumoto 毛利輝元 — Western Army leader
Shimazu 島津 しまづ / Shimazu
Sengoku–Edo
"Island inlet" — from a place in Kyūshū
The Shimazu were one of the oldest and most resilient daimyo families, ruling Satsuma in southern Kyūshū for nearly 700 years. They were among the first to use firearms in Japan and later played a central role in the Meiji Restoration.
Famous People
  • Shimazu Yoshihisa 島津久 — unified Kyūshū
  • Shimazu Yoshihiro 島津義弘 — Sekigahara commander
Chōsokabe 長宗我部 ちょうそかべ / Chōsokabe
Sengoku
"Long clan I part" — complex name from a place in Tosa
The Chōsokabe unified the island of Shikoku under Chōsokabe Motochika, who started as a minor lord and conquered the entire island. They were later reduced by Toyotomi Hideyoshi, who allowed them to keep only Tosa Province.
Famous People
  • Chōsokabe Motochika 長宗我部元親 — "Conqueror of Shikoku"
Amago 尼子 あまご / Amago
Sengoku
"Nun child" — from a legendary ancestor who was a nun's son
The Amago were a powerful clan from Izumo (modern Shimane) who once controlled much of the San'in region. Their rise and fall was rapid: they went from minor retainers to regional hegemons, then collapsed under pressure from the Mōri.
Famous People
  • Amago Tsunehisa 尼子経久 — founder of Amago power
Azai 浅井 あざい / Azai
Sengoku
"Shallow well" — from a place in Ōmi
The Azai were a clan from northern Ōmi who allied with Oda Nobunaga through marriage, then betrayed him to join the anti-Oda alliance. Their tragic story, including the fate of Azai Nagamasa's wife Oichi (Nobunaga's sister), is a staple of Sengoku drama.
Famous People
  • Azai Nagamasa 浅井長政 — tragic daimyo
Asakura 朝倉 あさくら / Asakura
Sengoku
"Morning warehouse" — from a place in Echizen
The Asakura were cultured daimyo of Echizen who maintained close ties with the imperial court and Kyoto aristocracy. They were destroyed by Oda Nobunaga, who burned their capital of Ichijōdani, a tragedy that preserved a snapshot of Sengoku-era town life.
Famous People
  • Asakura Yoshikage 朝倉義景 — last Asakura lord
Saitō 斎藤 さいとう / Saitō
Sengoku
"Purified wisteria" — from a place in Mino
The Saitō were originally retainers of the Toki who seized power in Mino Province. Saitō Dōsan, a ruthless oil merchant turned daimyo, was known as the "Viper of Mino." His son-in-law was Oda Nobunaga, making this one of the most dramatic in-law relationships in Japanese history.
Famous People
  • Saitō Dōsan 斎藤道三 — "Viper of Mino"
  • Saitō Toshimitsu 斎藤利三 — Akechi retainer
Imagawa 今川 いまがわ / Imagawa
Sengoku
"Now river" — from a place in Suruga
The Imagawa were a prestigious clan claiming Ashikaga descent who ruled Suruga, Tōtōmi, and Mikawa. Imagawa Yoshimoto was the most powerful daimyo in eastern Japan until his shocking defeat and death at Okehazama (1560) at the hands of the young Oda Nobunaga.
Famous People
  • Imagawa Yoshimoto 今川義元 — killed at Okehazama
Hōjō (Go-Hōjō) 後北条 ごほうじょう / Go-Hōjō
Sengoku
"Later Hōjō" — unrelated to the Kamakura Hōjō
The Go-Hōjō were originally the Ise family who adopted the prestigious Hōjō name. They built a powerful domain in the Kantō region around Odawara Castle. Their five-generation rule ended with their destruction by Toyotomi Hideyoshi in 1590.
Famous People
  • Hōjō Sōun 北条早雲 — founder
  • Hōjō Ujiyasu 北条氏康 — "Lion of Sagami"
Rokkaku 六角 ろっかく / Rokkaku
Sengoku
"Hexagon" — from a place shaped like a hexagon
The Rokkaku were an ancient aristocratic clan from Ōmi who claimed descent from the Sasaki, a branch of the Minamoto. They were shugo daimyo who struggled to maintain their position against the rising Oda and were eventually reduced to minor status.
Famous People
  • Rokkaku Yoshikata 六角義賢 — survived the Sengoku
Mogami 最上 もがみ / Mogami
Sengoku–Edo
"Most upper" — from a place in Dewa
The Mogami ruled Dewa Province in the Tōhoku region. Mogami Yoshiaki expanded their territory significantly and survived into the Edo period, though they were later dispossessed. The Mogami River is one of Japan's fastest-flowing rivers.
Famous People
  • Mogami Yoshiaki 最上義光 — Tōhoku daimyo
Ōtomo (Kyūshū) 大友 おおとも / Ōtomo
Sengoku
"Great friend" — from a place in Bungo
The Ōtomo were one of the great clans of Kyūshū, early adopters of Christianity and Western firearms. Ōtomo Sōrin was baptized as a Christian and encouraged trade with Portugal. They were eventually overwhelmed by the Shimazu.
Famous People
  • Ōtomo Sōrin 大友宗麟 — Christian daimyo
Ryūzōji 龍造寺 りゅうぞうじ / Ryūzōji
Sengoku
"Dragon create temple" — from a place in Hizen
The Ryūzōji were a Kyūshū clan who briefly controlled much of the island before being supplanted by the Shimazu and absorbed into the Toyotomi order. They were known for their early use of firearms.
Famous People
  • Ryūzōji Takanobu 龍造寺隆信 — "Bear of Hizen"
Tsutsui 筒井 つつい / Tsutsui
Sengoku
"Cylinder well" — from a well shaped like a pipe/cylinder
The Tsutsui were a clan from Yamato Province (modern Nara) who controlled the area around the ancient capital. They were caught between the rising Oda and the militant Buddhist monks of Kōfuku-ji. Tsutsui Junkei is remembered for being the first to enter Honnō-ji after Nobunaga's death.
Famous People
  • Tsutsui Junkei 筒井順慶 — Yamato daimyo

The Three Unifiers of Japan

天下人 — 三英傑

Three men ended the Sengoku chaos and unified Japan: Oda Nobunaga broke the old order, Toyotomi Hideyoshi completed the conquest, and Tokugawa Ieyasu established 250 years of peace.

Oda 織田 おだ / Oda
Sengoku
"Weave field" — from a place in Echizen
The Oda were a minor clan from Owari Province who rose to dominate Japan under Oda Nobunaga. His innovative tactics, ruthless efficiency, and embrace of firearms shattered the old samurai order. He was betrayed and forced to commit suicide at Honnō-ji by Akechi Mitsuhide in 1582.
Famous People
  • Oda Nobunaga 織田信長 — "Demon King," first unifier
  • Oda Nobutada 織田信忠 — heir, died at Honnō-ji
Toyotomi 豊臣 とよとみ / Toyotomi
Azuchi-Momoyama
"Abundant retainer" — an imperial-bestowed surname
Toyotomi Hideyoshi was born a peasant named Kinoshita Tōkichirō. He rose through sheer ability to become Nobunaga's top general, then completed the unification of Japan after Nobunaga's death. The emperor granted him the surname Toyotomi, making him one of the few non-aristocrats to receive such an honor.
Famous People
  • Toyotomi Hideyoshi 豊臣秀吉 — "Taikō," second unifier
  • Toyotomi Hidetsugu 豊臣秀次 — nephew & heir
Tokugawa 徳川 とくがわ / Tokugawa
Edo
"Virtue river" — adopted for its auspicious meaning
Originally the Matsudaira of Mikawa, Tokugawa Ieyasu changed his surname after claiming descent from the Seiwa Genji Minamoto. He waited patiently while Nobunaga and Hideyoshi did the hard work of unification, then seized power at Sekigahara (1600) and established the Tokugawa shogunate that ruled Japan for 268 years.
Famous People
  • Tokugawa Ieyasu 徳川家康 — founder of shogunate
  • Tokugawa Hidetada 徳川秀忠 — 2nd shogun
  • Tokugawa Tsunayoshi 徳川綱吉 — "Dog Shogun"

Tokugawa-Era Daimyo Clans

江戸時代の大名氏族

Under the Tokugawa shogunate (1603-1868), Japan was divided into domains (han) ruled by daimyo. These clans formed the backbone of the bakuhan system and many survived into the modern era as prominent families.

Matsudaira 松平 まつだいら / Matsudaira
Tokugawa
"Pine flat" — Tokugawa Ieyasu's original surname
The Matsudaira were the original clan of Tokugawa Ieyasu. After he changed his name to Tokugawa, many branches retained the Matsudaira name as cadet houses. They formed a vast network of related daimyo across Japan, including the Matsudaira of Aizu and Echizen.
Famous People
  • Matsudaira Katamori 松平容保 — Aizu lord, Boshin War
Maeda 前田 まえだ / Maeda
Tokugawa
"Front field" — from a place in Owari
The Maeda were the richest tozama (outside) daimyo, ruling Kaga Province (modern Ishikawa) with over one million koku of rice revenue. Maeda Toshiie was one of Toyotomi Hideyoshi's top generals and his family wisely submitted to the Tokugawa early, preserving their vast domain.
Famous People
  • Maeda Toshiie 前田利家 — Toyotomi general
Kuroda 黒田 くろだ / Kuroda
Tokugawa
"Black field" — from a place in Harima
The Kuroda were granted Fukuoka Domain in Kyūshū for their service to Toyotomi Hideyoshi and Tokugawa Ieyasu. Kuroda Kanbei (Yoshitaka) was one of the most brilliant strategists of the Sengoku period, surviving imprisonment and torture to serve three unifiers.
Famous People
  • Kuroda Kanbei 黒田官兵衛 — strategist
  • Kuroda Nagamasa 黒田長政 — Fukuoka daimyo
Ii 井伊 いい / Ii
Tokugawa
"Well I" — from a place in Tōtōmi
The Ii were hereditary fudai (insider) daimyo who served as regents for the Tokugawa. Ii Naomasa was one of the Tokugawa Shitennō (Four Heavenly Kings). Ii Naosuke, as regent in the 1850s, signed the Harris Treaty and was assassinated, triggering the end of the shogunate.
Famous People
  • Ii Naomasa 井伊直政 — Tokugawa general
  • Ii Naosuke 井伊直弼 — assassinated regent
Sakai 酒井 さかい / Sakai
Tokugawa
"Alcohol well" — from a place in Mikawa
The Sakai were a prominent fudai daimyo family who served the Tokugawa from their earliest days in Mikawa. They held important posts including rōjū (elder council) positions and ruled domains including Matsushiro and Himeji.
Famous People
  • Sakai Tadatsugu 酒井忠次 — Tokugawa Shitennō
Honda 本多 ほんだ / Honda
Tokugawa
"Root many" — from a place in Mikawa
The Honda were one of the most trusted Tokugawa retainer families. Honda Tadakatsu was considered one of the greatest warriors of his generation, never having been wounded in over 100 battles. The Honda served the Tokugawa through the entire Edo period.
Famous People
  • Honda Tadakatsu 本多忠勝 — "Warrior who surpassed death"
Nabeshima 鍋島 なべしま / Nabeshima
Tokugawa
"Pot island" — from a place in Hizen
The Nabeshima ruled Saga Domain in Kyūshū and are famous for their exquisite porcelain, known as Imari or Arita ware. They were originally retainers of the Ryūzōji who inherited their domain. Nabeshima Naomasa was a progressive daimyo who modernized Saga.
Famous People
  • Nabeshima Naomasa 鍋島直正 — modernizing daimyo
Katō 加藤 かとう / Katō
Tokugawa
"Add wisteria" — from a place in Owari
The Katō were a samurai clan who served under Toyotomi Hideyoshi. Katō Kiyomasa was known as a fierce warrior and castle-builder who constructed Kumamoto Castle. He was also a devout Nichiren Buddhist and persecuted Christians in his domain.
Famous People
  • Katō Kiyomasa 加藤清正 — "Demon of the East"
Asano 浅野 あさの / Asano
Tokugawa
"Shallow field" — from a place in Mikawa
The Asano are most famous for the story of the 47 Rōnin. Asano Naganori was forced to commit seppuku after attacking Kira Yoshinaka in Edo Castle. His retainers, led by Ōishi Yoshio, avenged him and became national symbols of loyalty.
Famous People
  • Asano Naganori 浅野長矩 — lord of 47 Rōnin

Meiji Restoration Leaders

明治維新の指導者

The Meiji Restoration (1868) overthrew the Tokugawa shogunate and transformed Japan into a modern nation-state. These clans and families produced the leaders who guided Japan through this revolutionary transition.

Saigō 西郷 さいごう / Saigō
Meiji
"West hometown" — from a place in Satsuma
The Saigō were a low-ranking samurai family from Satsuma who produced one of the most iconic figures in Japanese history. Saigō Takamori led the forces that overthrew the shogunate, then rebelled against the new government in the Satsuma Rebellion (1877), becoming the "Last Samurai" of popular legend.
Famous People
  • Saigō Takamori 西郷隆盛 — "Last Samurai"
  • Saigō Tsugumichi 西郷従道 — admiral & politician
Ōkubo 大久保 おおくぼ / Ōkubo
Meiji
"Great long preserve" — from a place in Satsuma
The Ōkubo were a samurai family from Satsuma who produced one of the "Three Great Nobles of the Restoration." Ōkubo Toshimichi was the most powerful figure in early Meiji Japan, overseeing the abolition of feudal domains and the creation of prefectures. He was assassinated in 1878 by disaffected samurai.
Famous People
  • Ōkubo Toshimichi 大久保利通 — "Iron Chancellor"
Kido 木戸 きど / Kido
Meiji
"Tree door" — from a place in Chōshū
Kido Takayoshi, born Katsura Kogorō, was the third of the "Three Great Nobles." From Chōshū, he helped forge the Satchō Alliance that overthrew the shogunate. As a Meiji leader, he advocated for a constitutional government and civil rights.
Famous People
  • Kido Takayoshi 木戸孝允 — Restoration noble
Itō 伊藤 いとう / Itō
Meiji
"That wisteria" — from a place in Chōshū
Itō Hirobumi, born into a low-ranking samurai family in Chōshū, became Japan's first prime minister and the principal author of the Meiji Constitution. He studied in England, served as Resident-General of Korea, and was assassinated by a Korean nationalist in 1909.
Famous People
  • Itō Hirobumi 伊藤博文 — 1st PM of Japan
Yamagata 山縣 やまがた / Yamagata
Meiji
"Mountain county" — from a place in Chōshū
Yamagata Aritomo was the founder of the modern Imperial Japanese Army and one of the most powerful genrō (elder statesmen). He served twice as prime minister and established the military's political independence through the Imperial Rescript to Soldiers and Sailors.
Famous People
  • Yamagata Aritomo 山縣有朋 — army founder
Ōkuma 大隈 おおくま / Ōkuma
Meiji
"Great corner" — from a place in Hizen

Common Japanese Family Names

一般的な日本の名字

These are the surnames you are most likely to encounter in modern Japan. Each entry shows the kanji, reading, literal meaning, and a notable bearer. Japan has over 300,000 distinct surnames, but these represent the most common and culturally significant.

Note on Kanji Variants

Many Japanese surnames have both 新字体 (shinjitai) (simplified) and 旧字体 (kyūjitai) (traditional) forms. For example, 小沢 (simplified) and 小澤 (traditional) are both the same surname, Ozawa. The simplified form is standard in modern writing, but families may retain the traditional form on their official register.
