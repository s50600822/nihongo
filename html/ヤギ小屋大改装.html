The grammar of construction description (手の届く範囲で完結している簡素な作り) is technical and evaluative, describing the shed's design as simple but functional. 気が荒くなります uses the set phrase 気が荒い (ki ga arai, to be irritable; to have a rough temper): "[their] spirit becomes rough = [they] become irritable." 気 (spirit; temper) is a key Japanese psychological noun that combines with adjectives to describe disposition. さすがにジョンのパワーには耐えられなかったようです uses さすがに (as expected; after all; indeed) + passive potential negative past + ようです (it seems): "As expected, [it] seems [the shed] couldn't withstand John's power." 耐える (taeru, to endure; to withstand) in potential negative past describes the shed's structural failure. The grammar of attribution (ようです) frames the collapse as an inference rather than a certainty, a softening device common in Japanese. The entire sentence is a masterpiece of humble causation: the goat's power is acknowledged as formidable (さすがに), but the shed's failure is stated tentatively (ようです), preserving dignity for both parties.

Grammar: かってください is the polite imperative of かかる/掛ける (to hang; to suspend): "Please hang [it] here." The 〜ておく (to do in advance) auxiliary contracts to とく in casual speech, and the Kansai imperative form uses the え-row for 一 verbs. This is the grammar of task delegation during manual labor. そう話していたら uses そう (like that; thus) + 話す in progressive conditional ていたら: "while [I] was talking like that / as [I] was saying that." 〜ていたら is the standard Japanese pattern for "while [verb]ing, [something else happened]," creating a temporal frame where the main action (finishing roof removal) happens concurrently with or as a result of the background action (talking). 外し終えました uses the compound verb 外す (to remove; to detach) + 終える (to finish): "finished removing." 終える attached to a verb stem creates a completion compound: [verb stem] + 終える = "finish [verb]ing." まず屋根の板でしょ uses the dubitative copula でしょ (isn't it; right) as a self-directed confirmation: "First, [it's] the roof boards, [right]?" The grammar of self-confirmation (〜でしょ) is common in DIY narration where the vlogger walks through the steps aloud. The triple でしょ (roof, walls, wood) creates a checklist rhythm, enumerating the materials of demolition.

In Japanese, animal and machine sounds are often described with 言う (to say) rather than 鳴る (to sound), personifying the source. 蜂まみれ uses the suffix まみれ (covered in; smeared with) attached to 蜂: "covered in hornets." まみれ is typically used for undesirable substances (mud, blood, dirt), so applying it to hornets elevates the situation from a nuisance to a hazardous coating. The grammar of infestation (蜂まみれ) is alarmist and attention-grabbing. でかい is the colloquial/rough form of 大きい (big; large), commonly used in Kansai and masculine speech. Its repetition (でかい、でかい / でかいよ、でかいよ) is the grammar of escalating alarm, each repetition intensifying the perceived threat. おす (male) identifies the hornet's sex, a detail that matters because male hornets cannot sting, making the discovery slightly less dangerous than a female. The vlogger's zoological observation under pressure is characteristic of rural Japanese vlogs, where nature knowledge is displayed even in crisis. 繋がっている壁材を1枚にします uses the progressive attributive 繋がってる (connected) modifying 壁材 (wall material), with を marking the object of 1枚にします (make into one sheet): "[I'll] make the connected wall material into one sheet." The にする pattern [quantity] + に + する means "to make into [quantity]; to reduce to [quantity]." 釘がなくなっちゃった uses なくなる (to disappear; to become nonexistent; to run out) + ちゃった (regrettable completion): "the nail disappeared [unfortunately] = the nail came out [unfortunately]." The grammar of hardware failure (釘がなくなっちゃった) frames the loosened nail as a regrettable event, typical of the vlogger's soft complaint style.

This is a common vlog transition formula, marking a break in the action. さっきばらした廃材を運び出します uses the attributive past ばらした (scattered; spread out) modifying 廃材 (scrap wood): "the scrap wood [that I] scattered earlier." ばらす means "to scatter; to spread out; to take apart," and in DIY contexts it refers to dismantling something into its component pieces. 運び出す is the compound verb 運ぶ (to carry) + 出す (to take out): "to carry out; to remove." いやあ、遊びたいな uses the exclamation いやあ (noo; man) + desiderative 遊びたい (want to play) + reflective な: "Noo, [I] want to play." 06 Key Grammar Patterns Summary

〜てしまった / 〜てしまいます (Regrettable/Complete Action)
壊してしまった, 外してしまいます. The grammar of irreversible actions and thorough completion in demolition.
〜ていたら (Background Conditional)
そう話していたら屋根の板を外し終えました. "While [verb]ing, [something happened]." The grammar of concurrent narration.
〜でしょ (Self-Confirmation)
まず屋根の板でしょ. Dubitative copula as checklist marker in DIY narration.
〜まみれ (Covered In)
蜂まみれ. The suffix of undesirable coating, from dirt to hornets.
〜ておく / 〜とく (Preparatory Action)
固定しておきました, 沸かせとけ. Doing things in advance for later benefit.
〜んじゃないでしょうか (Hedged Self-Assurance)
いいんじゃないでしょうか. The multi-layered softening pattern of Japanese uncertainty.
Kansai や & 終わりや
もう今日の作業は終わりや. Kansai copula asserting finality and shared understanding.
Compound Verbs of Labor
叩き込む, 掘り起こす, 運び出す, 飛び出してきた. Directional compounds that animate physical action.
DIY Technical Vocabulary
コンパネ, バール, 角材, 頭出し釘, 基礎, パレット, ネジ. The specialized lexicon of rural Japanese construction.
