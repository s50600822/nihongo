01 Verse 1

もう我慢ばっかしてらんないよ 言いたいことは言わなくちゃ
Mou gaman bakka shiterannai yo, iitai koto wa iwanakucha.
"I can't keep enduring anymore. The things I want to say, I have to say them."

Grammar: ばっか is the casual truncation of ばかり (only; nothing but), here emphasizing that all she's been doing is enduring. してらんない is a heavily contracted colloquial form of していられない, which combines the progressive している (am doing) with the potential negative られない (cannot): literally "cannot be in the state of doing." 我慢ばっかしてらんない = "I can't keep just enduring [anymore]." 言わなくちゃ is the casual truncation of 言わなくては (いけない/ならない), the negative te-form + は pattern expressing obligation: "must say; have to say." The double contraction (してらんない, 言わなくちゃ) gives the opening an urgent, conversational energy. The line is a declaration of emotional honesty: the speaker refuses to keep swallowing her words.

The triple "bye bye bye" mimics the rhythm of departure and the repetition of trying to convince oneself it's really over. The grammar is simple, but the imagery carries the emotional weight.

Grammar: 夢にまで見た is an intensified version of 夢に見た (dreamed of): に marks the location of the dream, and ま adds "even; to the extent of," suggesting the dream was vivid, recurring, or deeply wished for. ような after a verb creates a descriptive approximation: "the kind of world [that is] like [I] saw in [my] dreams." 争いもなく uses the universal negative pattern: [noun] + も + negative = "not even [noun]; without even [noun]." なく is the adverbial form of ない, connecting to the following noun phrase. The line establishes the ideal: a peaceful, conflict-free everyday life that exists only in dreams. The contrast with what follows (でも現実は) makes the disappointment sharper.

Grammar: トラブる is a loanword verb from English "trouble," conjugated as a Japanese godan verb: トラブる → トラブって. Loanword verbs (和製英語) are a hallmark of casual modern Japanese. 悔やんだり is the tari-form of 悔やむ (to regret; to lament), used to list representative actions: "doing things like regretting." The tari-form implies this is one of several things she does; the others are left unspoken. してる is the colloquial contraction of している, the progressive form of する used as an auxiliary for the tari-form: 悔やんだりしてる = "am [in the state of] doing things like regretting." そんな rolling days uses the determiner そんな to characterize the days she's been describing: "those kinds of rolling days." The word "rolling" in the title and refrain suggests continuous motion, like a wheel that keeps turning regardless of obstacles.

Chorus

Koronjattatte, ii n ja nai no, sonna toki wa waratte ageru.
"Even if you fall down, isn't that okay? At that time, I'll laugh for you [with you]."

Grammar: 転んじゃったって is a multi-layered contraction: 転ぶ (to fall; to stumble) → 転んでしまう (fall completely) → 転んじゃう (colloquial) → 転んじゃった (past) → 転んじゃったって (concessive, equivalent to 転んじゃっても). The たって pattern means "even if [verb]ed." いいんじゃないの is a soft rhetorical question: いい (fine) + のだ (explanatory) + じゃない (negative) + の (softening final particle) = "isn't it fine?" The negative question seeks agreement rather than information. 笑ってあげる combines the te-form with the giving auxiliary あげる, which indicates the action is done for someone else's benefit: "I'll laugh [for you/with you]." The chorus is the song's moral center: falling is not failure; it's part of moving forward, and someone will be there to help you laugh it off.

The image is a small moment of human connection that interrupts the struggle: even on a crowded bus, in the middle of a difficult day, someone smiled, and it was enough to notice. The grammar of spontaneous visibility (見える) suggests the smile found her, not that she went looking for it.

The vulnerability of admitting dependence makes the encouragement that came before it more authentic: she's not preaching from above, she's sharing what she needs too.

Grammar: The structure mirrors the first pre-chorus exactly (夢にまで見たような + noun), but replaces "world" with "sweet love," shifting the ideal from a general state of peace to a specific romantic relationship. 恋人たち uses the plural suffix たち, which is reserved for people and personified beings, marking "lovers" as a collective group. 隠れ家 is a charmingly specific word: a hidden place where lovers can be alone, away from the world. The structure is grammatically beautiful: the subject is not "I" but "days we can't meet" (会えない日), which "continue" (続く) as the main clause, while the speaker's belief runs underneath as a concurrent, resistant action. The grammar makes belief an act of endurance.

"I want to be smiling as much as possible, but to protect [what matters] through to the end, it can't be helped, right?"

Grammar: なるべく is an adverb meaning "as much as possible; if possible; wherever practicable." 笑顔でいたい uses the copular particle で (te-form of だ) + いる (to be) + たい (want to): "want to be [in a state of] smiling face." 守り抜く is a compound verb: 守る (to protect) + 抜く (to pull through; to do thoroughly) = "to protect through to the end; to defend completely." [Noun] + の + ため(に) means "for the sake of; in order to," but here the の is omitted in casual speech: 守り抜くために = "for the sake of protecting through." 仕方ない is the set phrase 仕方がない (there is no way/method) used adjectivally: "it can't be helped; there's nothing to be done." The trailing でしょ seeks agreement: "right?" The line acknowledges a painful truth: maintaining a smile isn't always possible when you're fighting to protect something important. The grammar of necessity (ためには) overrides the grammar of desire (でいたい).

The implication is that lies are trivial, unworthy of serious consideration. 意味を持たない is the negative of 意味を持つ (to hold meaning; to have significance): "hold no meaning." All my loving is an English phrase inserted as a declaration, a moment of direct emotional statement that needs no translation. そうじゃなきゃ is a heavy contraction of そうでなければ (if it were not so; if that weren't the case): そう (so) + じゃ (colloquial だ) + なきゃ (colloquial なければ). やってらんない is the same pattern as してらんない from the opening: やっている (am doing) + られない (cannot) = "cannot be in the state of doing" = "can't keep going." The line is the bridge's philosophical anchor: authenticity (no lies) is not optional; it's the only condition under which she can continue.

06 Final Chorus & Outro

Chorus

"Oh, yeah, oh, even if I stumble, way to go! Yeah! Yeah! Mud-covered rolling star!"

Grammar: つまずいたって is the concessive form of つまずく (to stumble; to trip): [past] + たって = "even if [verb]ed." This is the same pattern as 転んじゃったって from the first chorus, but with a different verb for variety. way to go is an English congratulatory phrase repurposed here as self-encouragement: she's cheering herself on. 泥だらけ uses the suffix だらけ, which attaches to nouns to mean "covered in; full of; riddled with," often with a negative connotation. 泥だらけ = "covered in mud." But here the mud is worn as a badge of honor: a rolling star (転がる星) picks up mud because it's moving, because it's in contact with the ground. A clean star would be a stationary one. The grammar of concession (たって) plus the grammar of contamination (だらけ) creates a paradoxical celebration: the marks of struggle are proof of motion.

Oh, yeah, oh, sou, wakatterutte, oh, yeah, oh, tsumazuitatte way to go!

Grammar: わかってる is the colloquial contraction of 分かっている (understand; know), the progressive form of 分かる. Using the progressive with stative verbs like 分かる emphasizes a current, ongoing state of knowledge: "I do know; I am aware." The final particle って after わかってる is a casual quotative used as an assertion marker: "I know, [I'm telling you]." It responds to an implied objection: "Yes, I know it's hard. Yes, I know I'll fall." The outro is a conversation with herself or with doubt itself: acknowledging the difficulty (わかってるって) and immediately overriding it with the same encouragement (way to go!). The repetition of the entire chorus structure with slight variation creates the sense of a mantra, something she tells herself again and again until it becomes true through repetition.

07 Key Grammar Patterns Summary

〜てらんない / 〜ていられない
"Can't keep [verb]ing." Progressive + potential negative. Colloquial てらんない form. Used for emotional limits: "can't keep enduring," "can't keep going."
〜なくちゃ / 〜なきゃ
"Must [verb]; have to [verb]." Negative te-form + は (truncated). Colloquial obligation pattern. 言わなくちゃ, 見せなきゃ.
〜たって / 〜ちゃったって
"Even if [verb]ed." Concessive pattern. 転んじゃったって, つまずいたって. Expresses resilience in the face of failure.
〜ながら(も)
"While; although; even as." Continuative + concessive. 続きながらも. Expresses simultaneous difficulty and persistence.
〜たりする
"Do things like [verb]." Tari-form + する. Representative listing of actions. 悔やんだりしてる.
〜んじゃないの
Soft rhetorical question: "isn't it [adjective]?" Explanatory の + negative + の. いんじゃないの. Seeks agreement.
〜てあげる
"Do [verb] for [you]." Te-form + giving auxiliary. 笑ってあげる. Action performed for another's benefit.
〜ために(は)
"For the sake of; in order to." Expresses purpose and necessity. 守り抜くためには.
